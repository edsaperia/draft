# draft — project conventions

A group drafting engine: patches race, blind pairwise judgments rank them, adoption clears a rising confidence bar. SPEC.md is the source of truth for the mechanism; when spec and code disagree, the spec wins until Ed amends it.

Design reasoning — why a thing is the way it is, what it replaced, what was tried and rejected — lives in `design/DECISIONS.md`. This file is the operative reference: names, rules, and constraints that would bite if broken.

## V1 product decisions

- Target context: constitutional conventions for Newspeak House cohorts. Rosters typically 5–10, conventions 15–20; design must not preclude 100+/1000+ instances, but v1 tunes to small rosters (data-efficiency over throughput).
- Hosted multi-tenant web service; magic-link auth against roster emails. Invitations send from **docs.vote** via **Resend** — the sending domain was the real hosting decision.
- Documents are Markdown, rendered as rich text; usually a few pages, long-document behavior stays in scope for sim experiments.
- TypeScript end-to-end; engine-core is a pure, dependency-free library shared by sim-harness and product server.
- Sim personas are LLM-powered on a cheap model (Haiku-class) and speak the same participant API as human clients — no sim backdoor; mixed human/bot sessions are a goal.
- UI north star: **suggestion-mode with escalation** — the default surface reads like Google-Docs-style inline suggestions with approval; the race view is the escalation state that appears only where suggestions collide or stakes demand ceremony. Most of a session should feel like approving typo fixes; the machinery earns its visibility.

## The two kinds of decision

**A constitutional decision is one that would make past decisions mean something different.** That is the test. Disclosure, visibility, quorum, the approval threshold, the membership and whether the document ends at all are constitutional; the starting text, title, link, closing date, grant, drip, stake and AI proposals are ordinary; a member's name and picture are neither.

The line can fall **inside** one setting: the window is one question whose answers include *never*, so moving the closing date is ordinary and removing the ending is constitutional — **the route belongs to what a motion changes, not to what card it sits on** (Q329).

A `motion` is the act of proposing a change to any setting, and its route is a fact about the *setting*: an **ordinary** motion is an ordinary race judged at the threshold; a **constitutional** one is a unanimous vote on the proposed amendment — one refusal keeps what stands, and until everyone has answered, what stands stands. The founding collects minima by construction (§9.0a); unanimity is the one decision rule with no quorum question inside it to govern, which is what keeps the bootstrap shut at both ends. SPEC §9.6–9.7.

**Five rules that follow:**

1. **The test is time-indexed** (§9.6a): before judging opens there are no past decisions for a change to re-rate, so **before the start nothing is amended — only set**. The founder re-sets settings and re-shapes the membership freely until judging opens.
2. **Membership begins at first arrival.** An invitation is an invitation *to become* a member; invitees are listed as *invited* and count toward nothing (which is what keeps one unopened email from blocking judging). A roster change while founding answers are being collected is a **ground shift**: answers stand, their authors are notified and may revise.
3. **Delegate the decision, not the field** (Q341). A delegated question collects exactly the binding scalar — the threshold **at the close**, the quorum **number** in the founder's chosen form (count or share) — while the machinery it rides on (the ramp's shape and start, quorum's form) is pacing or wording, stays with the founder, and is **ordinary by the test**, since every adoption is recorded against the threshold as it stood. Hence two threshold cards (🌡️ *How sure must the room be?*, constitutional; 🪜 *Rising Approval Threshold?*, ordinary) and one quorum card with two eyebrows, *Asked as* and *The number*. Quorum floor: F = max(Q, min(⌈E/3⌉, F_max)), §4.2.
4. **Constitutional settings default to the members, ordinary ones to the founder** — the room can take an ordinary one back cheaply and cannot take a constitutional one back at all. **Retired everywhere, 2026-08-21** (Ed's no-defaults instruction; SPEC §9.0a amended, v0.63, closing Q511): nothing arrives delegated, because a default holder states an answer the founder has not given. Every setting is born convenor-held, both powers intact, its question **shut** — and **delegation is the act that opens a blind question**. It was retired at the surface first and left in the module for a day, where it did real damage: ten questions collected from the instant of creation, and because `settled()` counts a members-held setting as settled, the live page released the whole constitution at once instead of a clause at a time. **A holder radio reports an act, never a default**: delegated reads *roster*, held-and-set reads *founder*, held-and-unset stays blank — after the change `holder === 'convenor'` no longer tells *kept* apart from *untouched*, so the page must not render the two alike.
5. **The opening question is the amendment question**, so amending a constitution needs no new object anywhere on the surface.

**The tab you click does not move.** A closed pile takes margin **0** — its right edge is exactly the card's left edge, not `.chipcol`'s 14px paragraph gutter. The vertical half is corrected in `fitBand` **by measurement, never a constant**: the open card's strip hangs off `.headclause`, which starts below the card's own label, and the label wraps on a narrow card. Verify 0px in both axes after any change here.

**Two rules the constitution band carries.** *A constitutional setting a member had no say in is a decision they are owed*: it sits in their rail until they open it and press **OK**, then it leaves the rail and lives in the band — reading is not enough. **Ordinary** settings the founder made never enter a member's rail at all: nothing is being asked, and anybody may motion one at the threshold whenever they like. And *the headings are the people* — the founder's name and picture under **Convenor**, the membership's under **Membership**, arriving as they do, with a placeholder before anybody has one.

## Names

- **founder** — whoever creates and administers a document. "Convenor" persists in SPEC and engine code as the older term, like `roster` for membership. Never "admin".
- **member** — anybody on the roster; the surface says **membership**, the code says `roster`.
- **clerk** — a founder who is not a member. A founder who is one appears in the membership like anybody else.
- **Observers are not members** — no observer role, no row, no name, no picture, because nothing about them is known. Who may read a document is settled entirely by 🌍 **Visibility** (the engine's `chamber`). SPEC §9.3's observer role does not survive this (Q324).
- **approval threshold**, never "the bar" — and it is a **confidence**, not a vote share (§5), which the copy has to keep saying.
- **proposal rate**, never "economy" or "tokens".
- **participant** is the engine's word for whatever speaks the `participant-api`; it must never appear in anything a member reads.
- **"ordinary" never appears on the surface** — the kind pair is glyphic: **✏️** *anybody may propose changing it*, **🏛️** *a constitutional change, the ask-everyone route*. The word *constitutional* does survive on the surface; "ordinary" is engine vocabulary only.
- **"ceremony" never appears on the surface.** What is left is what happens: everyone answers, and when everyone is ready the document begins.
- **`draft` already means a candidate patch** everywhere in this project, so the thing the surface makes is a *document* or a *charter*, never a draft.

## Glossary — named parts

Use these names in all discussion, commits, and code. Literal and stable beats elegant.

**An entry is a name, a one-line job, and pointers — nothing else.** Rules belong to `SPEC.md`, `SURFACE.md` or `design/STYLE.md`; reasoning belongs to `design/DECISIONS.md`; post-mortems belong to *Gotchas* below. Every entry declares its kind in square brackets — `[file]` a path in the repo, `[symbol]` a name that appears literally in the source, `[concept]` a design idea or a planned part — and `npm run spec-check` checks the first two. See *What goes in this file* at the end.

**Retired names**, kept here so a lookup lands: `document-creation`, `founding-ceremony` and `setup.html` (the surfaces) → `session-view.html`; `edit-wallet` → `propose-wallet`; `setup-piles` → `constitution-section`; `drafting-desk`, `arrival-bar`, `bounty-board`, `redline` → gone. Their reasoning is in `design/DECISIONS.md`. The two superseded notes files, `design/session-view.notes.md` and `design/setup.notes.md`, were deleted 2026-08-23; `SURFACE.md` holds what was live in them and git holds the rest.

**Engine (mechanism, no UI):**
- `engine-core` [symbol] — the session state machine: candidates, races, adoptions, the threshold ramp, tokens and refunds, adoption floors, certification. Pure TS, node-free and browser-loadable. Rules: SPEC §2–§8, §13. Adoption batching and the cooldown metronome: SPEC §4.2. Amendments binding races in flight: SPEC §4.3.
- `adoption-threshold` [symbol] — the confidence bar a challenger's win-probability must clear; ramps over the session window on wall clock. Formerly "θ"; always use this name. SPEC §4, §5.
- `patch-engine` [concept] — text machinery: diffs, footprints, three-way merge, rebase, surgery.
- `overlap-gates` [concept] — the three-gate classifier for colliding patches: textual composition → semantic composition → rivalry. SPEC §4.
- `ranking-model` [concept] — Bradley–Terry preference model per race, plus a global salience model, with active pair sampling and deadlock detection. SPEC §5, §8.
- `router` [concept] — feed ordering, hot set, exploration and salience streams, floors-aware serving, notification digests. SPEC §8.
- `event-log` [concept] — append-only hash-chained log, seeded RNG, participant receipts. SPEC §11.
- `dedup-gate` [concept] — submission-time duplicate check (embeddings, edit distance, LLM equivalence) plus behavioral dedup probes. SPEC §5.
- `race-labeler` [symbol] — advisory naming and typing of disputes, from the oracle's `describeRace` with a deterministic nearest-heading fallback. Outside the state machine: labels never enter the event log and gate nothing (Q49).
- `document-modes` [concept] — two independent settings fixed at creation: the **window** (an end datetime, or none) and the **threshold shape** (fixed, or ramping). Only a perpetual document is constrained to a fixed threshold. SPEC §9.0. The surface asks the window on one card, ⏰, with *never* among its answers — SURFACE §8's `ORDER` row 14.
- `founding-ceremony` [concept] — the blind collection before any drafting: each member states the lowest they will accept and the document takes the maximum of each. A consent rule rather than a vote, which is what lets it escape the constitutional bootstrap. SPEC §9.0a; the surface's order is SURFACE §8.
- `sign-out` [concept] — a member declaring they are done, **holding** (staying in the quorum base) or **abstaining** (leaving it). Plain silence is never sign-out. SPEC §9.5.
- `freeze` [symbol] — what happens when active plus holding falls below quorum: races park, participants are notified, the record is cut. A stall with an alarm, not a death, and the perpetual mode's equivalent of a close. SPEC §9.5.
- `coherence-auditor` [concept] — machine drafter patrolling document drift on a fixed token budget. **Not a member**: it never judges and counts toward no quorum. Surface card: 🤖 **AI Proposals**, SURFACE §8 `ORDER` row 24. SPEC §10.

**Design system** (session-view's, tokenised — every surface adopts it rather than inventing its own):
- `cards.js` [file] — **the decision-card grammar, one implementation** (`design/cards.js`, `window.CARDS`): a pure layer (the diff/markdown engine, `resultOnly`, `speakerHtml`, `fieldHtml`, `fieldOf`, the drawn TICK/CROSS and MARK glyphs) and a `make(env)` factory (lane bars, proposal blocks, `clauseHeadHtml`, commit row, `laneBoxHtml`, the open/close geometry, `keepStill`). Card kinds and their commit rows: SURFACE §9. Seams: `o.marks`/`o.washAttrs` on the clause head, `valAttr` (session radios speak `data-v`, setup composers `data-mval`, motion judging `data-motion`), a bindable speaker tooltip.
- `wash` [symbol] — the lifecycle colour on anything that carries one: a clause, a `clause-head`, a held-open gap, a queue entry. Colour changes fade over `--wash-ms` (700 ms), keyed by what the wash is *about*, so a clause and the head of the card that swallows it share one key.
- `room-pulse` [concept] — one grey dot in the topbar that beats once per action by anybody, yourself included. Deliberately **content-free** — no count, no direction, no author — which is what keeps it inside SPEC §3.5. Grey at rest and grey at its brightest; it beats by growing a ring.
- `me` [symbol] — the user avatar at the right of the topbar, initials by default. The only face on the surface, since everybody else's authorship is sealed (SPEC §3.4); Q278 asks whether that stays true.
- `lifecycle palette` [concept] — three hues held as raw RGB channels: `--lc-open` 🔥💡⚔️ yellow, `--lc-weigh` 🌶️ pink, `--lc-yours` ✏️ blue (the accent), and one grey shared by `--lc-deciding` ⏳ and `--lc-closed` ✔✖↻. Two rules govern it — **grey means nothing is being asked of you**, and **hot for actions, cold for information**. 🔥 takes a fixed alpha clear of the ramp's ceiling (`FLAME_A`, 0.44) with the urgency ramp underneath, because 🔥 names a category and a ramp cannot express one. `--ok`'s green never leaves a card interior (Q264). The marks themselves: SURFACE §6.
- `--primary` [symbol] — the one accent: every wire, every selection, every control that is on. `--ok` — green, meaning *decided* and only that.
- `type scale` [concept] — `--t-lead / --t-body / --t-ui / --t-small / --t-cap / --t-micro`, and **every piece of text on the surface is on it**. One exception is allowed to be a literal: a glyph size, which tunes a silhouette to a box rather than text to a scale. `.eyebrow` carries the single upper-case label treatment at `--t-micro` and **names its users in its own selector**; a shared rule nothing points at is not a shared rule.
- `spacing scale` [concept] — `--s1`–`--s5` on a 4px grid; radii `--r-sm / --r-md / --r-lg`; elevation `--shadow-sm/md/lg/xl`, neutral and never tinted — a drop shadow rather than a glow, each step a 1px contact shadow plus one soft cast whose blur stays near 1.6× its offset. Layout constants `--rail-left / --rail-right / --nav-h`. **Open is said by depth, not by outline**: no active box carries a border, and `--shadow-xl` is the only step allowed to be big.
- `lanepick` [symbol] — the session-view's own radio, and what a settings choice is drawn with. A setting is exactly the judgment a lane bar puts: one of these, or none of them yet. No box round each option, which would compete with the radio for the job of saying which one is chosen.

**Product (UI and founding):**
- `session-view.html` [file] — **the one surface** (`design/session-view.html`, stage 8, Q418 (a)): one page from blank arrival through the founding to a live, drafted document. `setup.html`, `document-creation.html` and `founding-ceremony.html` are redirects.
  - **Three addresses, one page**: served from the product server it detects its address — `/` is the birth, `/d/:slug` a real document through `remoteCS` (a `ConstitutionSession`-lookalike over the blind `view()`: reads from the projection, commands as serialized POSTs folded under the cookie's identity, values re-hydrated on a 4s poll **but never under the open card**, dev chrome hidden). Opened as a file or from the static design server it is the fixture — no query is the founding fixture, `?fixture=session` the Hollow Oak session (`&band=1` shows the composition, `&closed=1` the closed page).
  - **One block is one engine line**: `blocksOf(text)` keys each line `L<i>` (a `# ` prefix is a heading), so a proposal on a paragraph is exactly one hunk and no mapping layer stands between the page and the engine's footprint. An empty document has one empty block keyed `L0`, and the engine holds it as zero lines, so its proposal is an insert at `[0, 0)` (Q649 (a)).
  - **It renders over `@draft/constitution`** — a `ConstitutionSession` born at the save (§9.7a) holds settled values, delegations, blind counts, resolutions, gates, motions and the register — and keeps rendering, its copy, and the **provisional layer** only: radio positions, unsent answers, a motion being typed. The `S` fields are exactly that and nothing more.

Per-user state lives on the roster rows; `S.myAns`/`S.okd`/`S.draft`/`S.mAns`/`S.myname…` are property accessors delegating to whoever is in the chair. Beside it, **⏩ settle the founding** completes the founding in one press, and it keeps 🌍 with the founder so a constitutional 🛡️ card and the crown route stay reachable by hand.

The five states, the founding order (`ORDER`), what blocks and what hides are **SURFACE §6 and §8**, and the checker asserts the order table against `ORDER` and `SEC`. Seams worth knowing: `blocksOrder` in session-view.html is where the pen is the one blocking grant; `orderReady` consults nothing but `ORDER`; `ansDue` is F7's *unless nothing else is outstanding* exception; `CHOSEN`, per setting, is what keeps every commit dark until there is an answer; the old default set survives in exactly one place, ⏩'s `SEED`.   - `birth-pass` [symbol] — **what is born arrives, it does not appear.** `birthPass` (setup.js) keys each element by what it is *about* (`data-para`, section id, `data-q`), remembers every key ever seen, and hands a newborn its full presence after one forced reflow. Two entrances and their pacing: SURFACE F14.
  - `card-morph` [concept] — **switching cards within one strip is a morph, not a teardown.** The box survives: its height glides from the old card's to the new card's while the body crossfades, and the head swaps in place, so the tab you clicked never blinks and never moves (0.0px on both axes). From anywhere else the old card closes into its own paragraph first, because there the document *would* jump by a card's height in one frame.
  - `dead-click-nudge` [concept] — **a click that lands on nothing beats the task that is waiting**, during the birth only. The rule is SURFACE F17. Dead is structural — no `a/button/input/textarea/select/label/[role=button]/[contenteditable=true]` ancestor — rather than a list of `data-*` hooks that would rot; `#prose` is excluded deliberately (Q654).
- `commit-row grammar` [concept] — discard, commit, and OK: **SURFACE §9.1**. Two code rules inside it: what-it-stands-as is **remembered, not derived** — one snapshot per card, living until that card commits, never one per opening, since provisional values survive a close; and a revert restores **only the keys the open card can write**, read off its own controls (`data-set`/`data-txt`/`data-num`/`data-ans`).
  - `setup-band` [concept] — **the constitution, at the top of the document.** A decision card replaces the clause it is about; a setting is about the whole document, so it opens where the whole document begins — in the document, never over it. The band's own table is SURFACE §8.
- `gate-cards` [concept] — 💡 **Proposing** and ⚖️ **Judging**: not settings, since nobody decided them — they follow from the others — but exactly the shape of an unacknowledged decision, so they take its rule. 💡 is also the ✏️ grant (Q461a). SURFACE §8 `ORDER` rows 20–21, F4, F8.
- `motion-controls` [concept] — **a motion is composed with the setting's own control**, never a free-text lane, and **the settled card IS the composer**: SURFACE §9.2, K1–K12. `PROPOSE` in session-view.html covers every composable setting — the checker asserts it equals the composable set — and filters the standing rule by value, never by label text (Q620).
- `reserved` [concept] — the founder's two retained powers: **✒️ drafting** (changing the setting directly) and **🛡️ assent** (a member-carried change waits on the founder's accept). A philosophical sword and shield: one writes, one only refuses. Held and relinquished separately and one-way; a `reserve` motion restores either or both (Q394). The routes, the timing, the crown's lapse and the delegation rule are **SPEC §9.6–9.7**; the surface is SURFACE §9's power cards and 👑 question rows.
  - **👑** is the mark beside a person who holds either power anywhere; **📯** the mark of one who holds none. *Code still carries the older names `unilateral` and `assent`.*
  - Built in `@draft/constitution` (`SettingState.powers`, `relinquish`). The founder's direct hand on a card **is** the drafting power (`founderDirect`), so an assent-only setting renders the founder the member composer; the composer's carried-to-👑 note keys on the assent power (`crownWaits`). The register's pair lives inside the applications value (`reserved` / `reserved-unilateral` / `reserved-assent` / `members`).
  - Out of scope by Ed's word: reserving the text (Q387); individual delegation, foundership transfer, inheritance (Q388). Residuals in Q349.

- `phase-ladder` [concept] — **one dev control that walks a real document through its whole life**, a phase per press (Q674–Q678). ## Gotchas — what bites

The post-mortems: what broke, why it broke, and the shape of the mistake so it is recognisable the next time. No other document claims this material — SPEC and SURFACE state rules, `design/DECISIONS.md` states reasons, and none of them state *the thing that went wrong*. Kept as one findable set rather than scattered through the glossary, because most of them are only useful to somebody who does not yet know they are about to make the mistake.

**Design system:**
- **Open is said by depth, not by outline**: no active box carries a border — not the decision card, not the drafting box, not the open rail entry. `--shadow-xl` is the only step allowed to be big, and it is still a light source rather than a halo (offset grows with blur, blur near 2.5× it, contact shadow keeping the near edge crisp).
- **A settings choice is the session-view's own radio** — `.lanepick`, not a hand-rolled radio in a bordered box. A setting is exactly the judgment a lane bar puts: *one of these, or none of them yet*. No box: a rule drawn round each option competes with the radio for the job of saying which one is chosen.
- **A hold is released by letting go, and by nothing else** (Ed, 2026-08-22: *I cannot submit it even if I hold it — the pencil flies back*). SURFACE W16. The charter's ✏️ Propose was bound per button inside `renderDoc` and released on **`pointerleave`**, and that is two bugs wearing one coat. A render detaches the button under the pointer, so the browser fires a boundary event at the node it just removed; and `.holding`'s own `scale(0.97)` insets the hit box by **0.78px**, which can cancel a stationary press with no render involved at all — measured, and the reason the earlier poll-deferral fix could not work: the live walk shows the hold already dead 500 ms in, *before* any render. It now takes the ✒️ pen hold's shape — bound once on the document, released on `pointerup`/`pointercancel` only, sliding-off-to-cancel deliberately gone. What made the timer survivable already is that it commits through `act(id, …)`, which resolves the draft **by id, not by node**: a hold that outlives its own button still lands. `spec-check` asserts the release set; `npm run journey` drives the real thing and fails on the pre-fix page.
- **Nothing rebuilds under a press** (Ed's live walk, 2026-08-22). A hold is a gesture in progress — the glyph is in the air, the control is under the pointer — and the live page re-renders on a **4s poll**, so a poll landing inside a hold replaces the very control being held. Four consequences, all fixed:
  - The **pen hold captured its button at `pointerdown`** and called `b.click()` a second later; a poll in between left `b` detached, and a click on a detached element never reaches the `document` listener that commits. The act vanished in silence — no command, no error, the card just sitting there. The hold belongs to the **open card**, not to one DOM node, so it re-finds the live commit control when its own has gone.
  - **Both polls now defer while a hold is in flight** (`penHold || SESSION.holding`). `SESSION.holding` is a module-level flag because the propose hold's own state lives inside `renderDoc` and is re-created by every render.
  - **A completed hold clicks for you, and that click must not look like the user's.** The hold's timer calls `click()` itself; the browser then delivers its own click on pointerup, and the guard that swallows that trailing half keyed on a flag the synthetic click had just cleared — so **every held commit on this surface fired twice** (Ed, 2026-08-22: *during the birth, after entering the email, the Link card opens immediately*). The two clicks land a millisecond apart, and `closeThen` defers a commit behind the collapse, so `S.open` is still set when the second arrives and the whole act runs again: on 📧 that is a second `POST /api/docs` asking for the address the first had just **reserved for itself**, answered truthfully with *that address is taken* — and the page did the right thing with the wrong news and walked the founder back to 📍. `penHoldFired` is true for exactly the duration of the synthetic dispatch, so inside a click handler it means precisely *this click is the hold's own*, and the flag is left alone for the real one. The birth also guards its own send, so no future double press can take the address from the creation making it.
  - **A wallet must not depend on its own animation finishing.** The pencil storm hides `#wallet` so the pencils can fly into an empty one and hands it back in its completion callback — so a missed callback hid a just-granted power *permanently*. A `setTimeout` safety net runs the same restoration; whichever lands first wins. This is the likeliest cause of Ed's earlier *I have no pencil wallet*.
- **`npm run journey`** (`scripts/journey-walk.mjs`, against a running dev server) walks **founding → 🍾 → grants → caret → proposal** on the live path and exits non-zero if it stalls. It exists because the two probes and `founding-walk.mjs` all drive the fixture, which never reaches `hydrateS`, never polls and never sends a command — every live-only bug this project has hit was invisible to them. **Since 2026-08-22 it drives the propose hold for its full three seconds with a real mouse, with a render forced into the middle**, asserting at the wire (`propose-text` 2xx, the wallet one lighter) rather than on page state, where a proposed draft and an unproposed one both read as *mine*. The disclaimer it replaces — that the holds could not be exercised because the tab is backgrounded — confused an *animation* with a *pointer held down*: rAF never fires here so no flight can be judged, but `page.mouse.down()` holds as long as you like and the commit is a `setTimeout`. That confusion is why the hold bug survived a first fix. It fails on the pre-fix page at *propose-text null · wallet 5→5 · held false*. Two harness rules it encodes, both learned the hard way: **press with a real pointer** (a synthetic `.click()` does not choose a rung), and **scroll the control into view first** — a pointer cannot press what is off screen, which is what made the founding look stuck at 💤. Two assertions were added on 2026-08-22 after both of that day's birth bugs walked straight past it: **the rail immediately after the save must hold the pen alone**, and **no `/api/` response may be 4xx or 5xx** — the double send's 409 was invisible because the surface *recovered* from it, so the check belongs at the wire rather than at the outcome. The refusal net does not reproduce the double send headless (the second click needs a real collapse animation to still find `S.open` set), so it is a net for the future rather than the proof of that fix; the rail assertion is the one that bites.
- **The card lifecycle lives in `SURFACE.md`** (spec pass 1, 2026-08-22): what opens a card, what it focuses, what closes it, what 🗑️ puts back, the one commit row and the hold ladder — rules C1–C5 and the lifecycle rows L1–L9. Kept here in one line each because they bite: a card opens only on a click and focuses its main decision; closing is not discarding; a card closes when its answer becomes the document's (a judgment too, since Q576 — files as ⏳); 🗑️ is always live, puts back only un-actioned input, and is the only bin; one commit row, one flat disabled look, 40px.
- **The open rail entry lifts, it does not jump**: the rail is rebuilt wholesale, so a clicked entry is a new element born already wearing the open shadow, and a transition cannot run from a value the element never had. `settleLift` paints it at rest, forces one reflow, then hands it the lift — the same technique the washes use.

**Product (UI and founding):**
- **The power clause, the 🛡️ radios, the power option as a proposal block and the `why`** are STYLE.md T6–T9: one sentence in Ed's vocabulary (*a member proposes; the membership passes and rejects; the Founder assents, refuses and amends*), the two 🛡️ radios saying *veto* and naming the setting (`PW_NOUN`, fallback *this*) with the negation bold, each option a `.propblock` at document size with *Choose this / Chosen* beneath, a blocked option dimming whole. The head keeps *may refuse* because the clause joins ✒️ and 🛡️ as verb phrases that *has a veto over* cannot be joined into. Blind spot: the setup-probe never opens a 🛡️ card with live radios — verified by rendering the labels directly.
- **⏩ keeps 🌍 with the founder** (Ed, 2026-08-22, asking whether the tooling could be more useful for this work). ⏩ already left four power cards live — title, link, rate, machines, being the settings it keeps — so the gap was never *no power card*; it was that every **constitutional** setting was delegated, and delegation is the state of holding neither power, so a constitutional 🛡️ card, the 👑 mark and the whole crown-question route were unreachable by hand in a settled document. Keeping 🌍 costs the fixture nothing it was saying (the value is the same `closed` the room was answering its way to, so every visibility sentence reads as before); what changes is the governance half — the clause gains its deviation and Ash is a 👑 rather than a 📯. Note the **setup-probe is unaffected**, and for a reason worth knowing: its founding scenario sets 🌍 by hand before pressing ⏩, and SEED only fills a key that is still empty — so the probe was always running on a founder-held 🌍 and this changes only the press-⏩-from-blank path. Power cards remain outside probe coverage by Ed's call.
- **A change carries a reason, and says who made it** (Q530, built 2026-08-22). The pen was the only route that changed a rule *without asking anybody* and the only one that left no reason behind. Now: **the founder writes one on the card**, and **everybody else is told what changed and reads it**. Which half you meet is which side of the act you stand on. Four things worth knowing.
  - **A first decision is not a change, and shows neither half.** The founder answering a question for the first time has changed nothing — there is no *from* — and a room told the founder has decided fifteen things nobody asked them about is noise. The test is the module's own `previousValue`, which is null until the pen changes a value that already stood. §9.6a says the same in the spec's words: before the start nothing is amended, only set.
  - **Nothing about the hash chain moved.** `previousValue` and `setWhy` **fold from `setting-set` events the log already carried**, the way `arrival` did in Q524; and the reason itself rides an **optional** `why?` on that event, which is hash-safe because `stableStringify` drops undefined keys — an event without one serialises exactly as it did before the field existed. The golden log passed unedited, which is the assertion of both.
  - **An ordinary setting owes an acknowledgement when it is *changed*, never when it is first made.** Constitutional settings owe one on any set, as they always have (*a rule you had no say in is a decision you are owed*); an ordinary one the founder simply made stays out of every rail, since nothing was asked and anybody may motion it. One the founder *changed* has undone something the room was living under, so it is news by the same argument.
  - **The module is the truth about what is owed, and the page now listens on both paths.** `hydrateS` only ever *added* to `S.okd`, so an acknowledgement given once could never be taken back — and a change re-owing an OK is the whole mechanism by which it announces itself. It is symmetric now, and `syncOwedOks` does the same for a local module, since the fixture never reaches `hydrateS`. It only ever removes: a change may re-open an acknowledgement, and nothing there may quietly grant one.
  - **The founder's rationale is not sealed** (Ed, 2026-08-22: *the rationale should have the founder's name and avatar on it, and 👑 so people know that it was a founder action, even if they have no name or avatar*). Every other rationale wears the featureless disc because authorship is sealed until the record (§3.4). A ✒️ act is attributed **by construction** — only the founder can perform it — so the disc would be a lie the first time it was drawn. `founderSpeaker` / `founderSpeakerLane` put the avatar where the disc goes, with the name and 👑 over the reason; the 👑 is what carries the sentence when there is neither, since an anonymous founder is ordinary.
  - Copy rule inside it: **a long value is shown, not narrated**. *from x to y* reads well for a rung, a percentage or a date and badly for ⏱️, whose value is three numbers in a sentence — the one number that moved is buried and the *to* is lost mid-clause. Past 32 characters the pair goes on two aligned `was`/`now` lines instead, where the eye finds the difference itself.
- **A unilateral rule change by the founder is an amendment, and is reported as one** (Ed, 2026-08-22, reframing Q530 mid-build). It gets no list of its own: it joins **the motions**, where every other amendment already lives, so the record renders it beside them without knowing it is different. `MotionRoute` gains **`'pen'`** — the four verbs' own ladder of who had to agree, minus 🪶, which is not an amendment because nothing exists yet to amend — and `RaisedRoute` is the narrower type for anything that *raises* one, since the pen is never raised: it opens and settles in the one act, so it is only ever seen `carried`, and every place that acts on a live motion tests `status === 'running'` and steps over it. **Folded, not emitted**: synthesised in the `setting-set` fold from the event the log already carried, with `amendedFrom(id)` beside it for the old value a motion payload never needs. Hash chain untouched; golden log unedited.
  - The dividend is the one Ed's framing predicted: **a clause now carries its own history whoever changed it**. `changeHalf` reads the amendment record rather than the setting's last-set fields, so the founder's pen and the membership's motion produce one line with one shape — and **who it names follows the route**, as it must: the pen is attributed by construction so the founder's name and face are on it, while a raised motion's mover is sealed until the record (§3.4), so that one is *the membership* and wears the featureless disc.
  - **The clause keeps it permanently** (Ed's answer, 2026-08-22). Two hoists made that work: `changeHalf` and `lastAmendment` are **function declarations**, because a member's settled card post-start renders through the composer's *earlier* return — as arrow consts the history was drawn on the news card and vanished the moment the OK turned it back into a composer. Above the fold it is news; once acknowledged the same block wears a **Last amended [date]** eyebrow, because a line a reader meets months later has to date itself or it reads as something that has just happened.
  - **The record's own Amendments section** (`amendmentBlocks`, before Backlog on the closed page): every carried change in order, one paragraph each, naming the **office** rather than the person — the pen is the Founder's by construction, a raised motion is the membership's act, and neither sentence has to reach into the anonymity ladder to know what it may say. An **absolute** date, never `whenOf`'s relative one: *today, 11:28* is false by morning and the record is read long after.
- **A lapsed member is owed an acknowledgement too** (Ed, 2026-08-22). E excludes the lapsed, and for every other purpose that is right — they are out of the quorum base and out of the electorate, because those are about who is *deciding*. An acknowledgement is not a decision: it is owed to somebody about a document they are **still a member of**, and lapse is a stall with an alarm rather than a departure (§9.5a) — revival is just logging in and their cast judgments keep counting. The person who was living under the old rule and went quiet is exactly the one a change ought to find. The two exclusions that stay are the two that mean something: a **removed** member is gone, and somebody who has **not arrived** never knew the old rule, so the change is not news to them — it is simply what the document says.
- **🍾 has no wallet, and that is where the family stops** (Q516, Ed: *am I going overboard with the wallets*). The four wallets are the four verbs, and the four verbs are a ladder of *who has to agree*; each is a **capacity you hold**. Beginning is not a level of authority and not a capacity — it is a moment that happens once, so a wallet for it is a button with a balance drawn on it, and it would beg a 🥂 wallet for the act nobody performs. What 🍾 does have is **its own glyph on its own commit** (it had borrowed ✒️, a category error: beginning is what *ends* the founding) and its own flight — **the cork leaves the button and lands in the document**. A pop, not a spend.
- **Surface copy audit rules** are STYLE.md §8 (T1–T35, spec pass 2): no spec references, no project-speak, a rename reaches the option labels and **one label per rung everywhere** (Q620), a shared body must not hard-code one caller's frame, read-only copy must survive the spec it summarises, raw values are not copy. Every string a member can read passes it — cards.js included, which the stage-8 audit never scanned (Q608).
- **The prose column invites before it asks** (Q513(c)): 📄 is the constitution's last clause, so the text is the last thing the founding asks for — but from **the save** the empty column says you may start whenever you like, in grey, asking nothing. The save is the moment because it is the first moment there is anywhere to put the words.
- **Task titles are Title Case**, and a title says what kind of answer it wants — STYLE.md T1–T2. The power tabs are two yes/no questions: ✒️ *Can the Founder Make Amendments at Will?* / 🛡️ *Does the Founder Have a Veto?* (Q615). Open: whether a card should be titled differently once settled (Q331).
- **One card per decision.** The test: **could somebody answer it differently and on its own?** A picture and a name are two cards; the grant, the drip and the stake were three. What is **not** split is a parameter and its own value — *rising, 55% → 78%* is one thought, and halving it splits a sentence rather than a decision. The test cuts both ways, which is what makes it a test: ⏰ *when it ends* **swallowed** *whether it ends*, because **never** is one of the answers to *when*; and 🎲 the stake stopped being a card at all, because it is a flat 1 in the mechanism (§13) and a card for it offered a decision nobody has.

**Lifted out of name entries (2026-08-23):**
- **🎩 is settled once the document begins, and `settled()` must say so** (Ed's live walk, 2026-08-21). 🎩 is not a catalogue setting, so `csState('hat')` is null and `hydrateS` can reconstruct it from nothing: on any reloaded live document it stood unanswered for ever. Harmless until the gates joined `ORDER` — then everything below 🎩 waited on a question that could not settle, so **💡 and ⚖️ never reached the rail, no gate could be acknowledged, no ✏️ wallet ever arrived, 🪪 never appeared, and most of the constitution's clauses were invisible**. §9.6a locks 🎩 at the start, and a locked card is settled: `S.seen.has('hat') || constituted()`. The general lesson is the one this file already teaches about the fixture — **a card whose settledness lives only in `S` is a card the live path cannot rebuild**, so every such card needs a truth the module can answer for.
- **✋ and 🖼️ are answered, and null is not the answer to *were you asked*** (Ed's live walk, 2026-08-22, Q645: *I'm never offered the "Your Name" and "Your Picture" tasks*). The same lesson as 🎩 above, in its sharpest form. `ready()` is permanently true for both — neither is in `CHOSEN`, and `myname.ready` is forced `() => true` because a task that cannot be committed is a task nothing can get past — so `S.seen` was the **whole** of `settled()` for them, and `hydrateS` could not rebuild it, so it simply declared both seen on every live render. Steps 9 and 10 of the founding order were dead on every real document while working perfectly in the fixture, which is why **no probe and no golden walk could ever have caught it**. What blocked the honest version is real: a blank name is a real answer — §9.0c shows it as **Anonymous**, *a name, not a gap* — and a picture is removed by choosing initials, so the value cannot say whether the question was put. The module therefore records **the act**: `nameSet`/`pictureSet` fold from the `identity-set` events the log already carries (`!== undefined`, never truthiness), the way `arrival` (Q524) and `previousValue` (Q530) do — no new event, no envelope change, **golden log unedited**. Two dividends: it is **per seat** by construction, where `S.seen` is page-global — the one "have you dealt with this" store that was not, so one seat's committed ✋ used to read as settled for everybody — and it survives a reload. ⏩ must answer both (a **null** picture is the answer *my initials*), or *settle the founding* leaves 🖼️ standing and the setup-probe diffs. Guarded live by `npm run journey`, which asserts the rail after 🎩 is exactly `myname, mypic` — **position, not presence**, since arriving in the wrong place is its own failure. Related, and fixed with it: **🎩 decides where the founder sits, not who they are** (Q646) — `convenor-membership-set` rebuilt the record from `freshMember` on every tick, wiping name, picture and both OK sets, and it carries **both** ways, since while the founder is a member their identity lives on the `MemberRecord` and the convenor struct goes stale.
- **An unproposed draft survives a data swap** (Ed, same walk: *when I ✒️ any constitutional question the text disappears*). Live, `SUGGS` is rebuilt from the server on every render and every 4s poll, and a draft you have not proposed exists nowhere but in that array. `syncCharter`'s `typing` guard only holds while the draft card is the open one, so opening any other card destroyed what you had typed. `setData` now carries a `mine && unproposed` draft across the swap — the same rule as every other provisional value: **closing a card is not discarding**.
- **And saving is not discarding either** (Ed, 2026-08-21: *text that I put into the document during the birth should be in the text after the birth*). The founder may type into the prose column from arrival, but `birthBoot`'s stash listener bailed on `!api.birth.pendingId` — and no pendingId exists until the 📧 send's response — so **everything typed before the verification mail went out was never stashed, and following the magic link dropped it**. The send now flushes `proseText()` to `/api/docs/pending` the moment there is somewhere to put it. The server half was always sound: the save takes the stash into `provisional`, and boot restores it into the column.
- **Clicking a rail entry travels to its card.** The charter's rail has done this for as long as it has existed (session.js's `bringIntoView`, aimed at the reading line); the constitution's rail never did — it set the open card and re-rendered, so a task whose clause sat below the fold opened where the reader could not see it and the press looked like it had done nothing (Ed, 2026-08-21). `scrollToCard` borrows session.js's own `smoothScrollBy`, so the probes' instant-jump seam applies here too. **Only from the rail**: a tab in the band is already where you are looking, and *the tab you click does not move* is the one geometric promise this surface makes.
- **The charter key uses a NUL as its separator**, and that is deliberate: `cs.text` is arbitrary member-written text, so the separator has to be something the document cannot contain. Cost: `grep` calls session-view.html a binary file — use `grep -a`, and don't "fix" the NULs.
- **Your own row clears the paragraph's own tab.** The Members block is one paragraph carrying two tab columns in one gutter — 🪪 at its head, your ✋🖼️📧 on the first list row, which is yours since you moved to the top. `fitStacks` cannot fix it: it shrinks a *pile's* peek and bails under two tabs, and a lone tab is 30px and cannot shrink (Q308's residual). `.memrow.meRow` is spaced to clear a full tab instead.
- **An address is checked because it is the address, not because it was typed** (Ed, 2026-08-22: *the address was taken — we shouldn't allow it to be set if it's taken*). 📍's commit has always been gated on `slugRefused()`, but the only caller of `checkSlug` was the **input** handler — so an address the founder never edited had no verdict at all and the gate stood open on an unasked question. The birth pre-fills 📍 from the title, and `suggestSlug` consulted the fixture's Hollow Oak `TAKEN` set, which knows nothing about the live host; accept the suggestion unedited and the first thing in the world to ask about the address was the **send**, answering truthfully *that address is taken* and walking the founder back to 📍 — the right answer to a question the card should have asked first, arriving at the one step that mints the document. (It is the same surface bug as the double send of the same morning, by a different road: both end at a correct 409 that reads as the page malfunctioning.) Four rules now hold it shut. The check is **idempotent and callable from anywhere** — `S.slugSeen` is the address the last verdict was about, whatever it said — and **`render` asks whenever there is an address to ask about**, which covers the pre-filled one and every path that sets it without a keystroke; only typing debounces. The commit additionally waits for a verdict (`slugKnown()`), which closes the window between a keystroke and the answer about it, but **a verdict that cannot be got counts as one**, so an unreachable host can never leave the founder at a permanently dark 📍 — the send's 409 stays the backstop it always was. **A creation's own reservation is not somebody else's**: the first send reserves the address, so asking the server about it afterwards is told, truthfully, that it is taken *by us* — the page answers for `api.birth.slug` itself rather than refusing the founder their own document (Q462b, on the check rather than the send). And a refusal **offers the nearest free address** — `GET /api/slug/:slug` carries a `suggestion` like the 409 does, because a block naming no way forward is worse than the bug.
- **The address is the machine's until you touch it, and the machine says when it picks** (Q534 (c), Ed 2026-08-22). 📍 is born pre-filled with `slugify(title)`, so on a collision the ordinary birth would open a task that is *already refused* — correct, but an unpleasantness at the third step of a founding. A **suggested** address moves itself to the nearest free one before the founder ever meets it; a **typed** one never moves, because there the address is theirs and a collision is a refusal to read and correct. `S.slugAuto` is the whole distinction: set in `fillSuggestedSlug` (the one place a suggestion is applied — it was three), cleared by the one act that can make the address yours. The move **says so**, naming both addresses — *Suggested from the title — docs.vote/charter is taken, so this one is docs.vote/charter-2* — since silently handing somebody a suffixed address is the machine deciding without telling them. Two guards worth keeping: the address it moves **to** is asked about in its own right on the next render rather than trusted from the response that offered it, and `autoSlugMoves` caps the chain at three so a server that keeps offering an address it then refuses cannot become a request storm. **`npm run slug-walk`** (`scripts/slug-walk.mjs`, against a running server, with one address reserved first) walks both halves; it fails on the pre-fix page at *the commit is dark on a typed address that is taken*. It exists because `journey` births with a unique title and so can never meet a collision, and both probes drive the fixture, where the check is synchronous and the live path is never reached.
- **A grant is the holder's, and nothing hid them** (SURFACE E8's audience column; found and fixed with Q639). A **member** was served two ⏳ tabs titled *Your Pen* and *Your Shield*, about powers they do not hold, cannot acknowledge and were never asked about — invisible while the clauses stood, since the tab had a sentence beside it. `hide: () => !amFounder()`, the seam 🍾 already uses; `spec-check` asserts both predicates and that the retired sentences never come back.
- Ladder rungs write `data-ans`/`data-ansval`, **never** `opt()`'s `data-set` — on the founder surface `data-set` already means *set the delegation*, and one attribute must not mean two things on one page. Each surface's handler decides where answers land.
- **Avatars align by box, not by content baseline**: inline-flex/grid boxes derive their baseline from their content, so a photo, initials and a drawn mark each sit differently beside the same text. The base `.av` takes `vertical-align: middle` (content-independent); the founded avatar takes a 2px optical nudge.
- **`may*` is the only thing a control may ask; `can*` is what the *document* permits** — `mayPropose`/`mayJudge`/`mayVoice`/`mayPen`/`mayShield` in session-view.html are `can* && viewerIsMember() && acked(k)`. **Filtered at ingest in `bindData`** (`withheld`) for the charter; by `visible` in the band. **What you may do is part of the charter column's data key**, so an OK re-keys the column exactly as a text change does.
- **`syncGrantAcks` watches a transition and never reads an absolute** — before the module hydrates every gate reports shut, so *gate closed, therefore drop the OK* would wipe acknowledgments on every load. Keyed by seat as well as by grant. The drop itself is not verified end to end (Q526).
- **Nothing may infer a power from the DOM**: every socket draws its tool, so `#penwallet i` exists whether or not the pen is held; `holdWallet` asks `mayPen()`. **The ghost must never become a removal**: `.gone` is `visibility: hidden`; `holdWallet` tests the token's existence; the trailing-click guard is `isPenCommit(held) && …`.
- **The spend-preview must never key on `:hover`** — `matches(':hover')` is false in headless Chromium with the pointer on the button; a `pointerover` on anything that does not spend is the proof it has left. It is render state, phase-locked by `startTime`, re-applied at the tail of every wallet render, because each wallet rebuilds its own innerHTML wholesale. Both surfaces register a **probe** with one shared listener.
- **The quarter-way floor is distance, not time** — callers pass the time at which their own easing reaches a quarter (250 of 1000 linear; 864 of 3000 on the pencil's bezier); the throw has a minimum rate, 1.8×. `flyGlyph` hands ownership over (`disarm`) so its own safety timeout does not delete a traveller mid-rewind.
- **`walletHeld` is wallet render state, never a class toggled from outside** — `renderWallet` rewrites `className` wholesale. **The strike hangs on the socket (`::after`), never inside the glyph**, whose `filter: grayscale(1)` would desaturate it. **`--slash` is the only red on the surface** and that is a live question (`?slash=muted` renders the comparison). **The navbar's height is load-bearing**: sockets are 24px because the avatar already sets 26 — verify the bar's height before and after any change here. The ∞ on ✒️ and 🛡️ is the text character in `.pmore`, **never an `<i>`**, since the flights find their token with `#penwallet i`.
- **The payload is text and the page escapes.** The holding sentence carries the founder's *raw* name — its other consumer sets it with `textContent` — so the single site that makes it markup escapes it. Escaping in the payload would double-escape the other consumer; the contract is pinned by a server test that names a founder with a tag (review #2: this was live XSS at an unauthenticated door).
- **Reading a document is a write, and that nearly sank the whole idea** (Q681). Presence stamps `now` (§9.5a), so merely *looking* at a ladder document pinned its log to the present and the next rung had no past left to write into — and since the bar reloads after every press, that happened between every pair of them. **A ladder document's clock is the ladder's**: the view path skips presence for it, dev-only, inside the label. Everything else goes through `Pen`, whose ceiling is *never later than real now*; out of room it stands still, since equal timestamps are legal.

## The spec pass

How SPEC.md and SURFACE.md are kept tabular, checkable and free of history (Ed, 2026-08-22 — he considered a skill and declined; the procedure lives here, the guarantee is `npm run spec-check`). Re-runnable; pass 1 was 2026-08-22 (Q540–Q582), and passes 2+ mostly live in steps 4–7.

1. **Claim a question block first** — re-read the tail of QUESTIONS.md, write the reservation into its Spent-numbers section, commit that alone. Numbers are claimed in the file, never in chat or a commit message.
2. **Extract** — walk one rule family (governance: who may change what, by which route, when; communication: what a member is told, what a control does); pull every normative sentence into a candidate row or rule. Leave derivations, history and commentary behind.
3. **Normalise** — find the general rule covering the most rows; restate the rest as instances. **Isolate** — whatever will not fit becomes a numbered exception: what it is, which rule it breaks, why, and the ruling behind it.
4. **Diff** — `npm run spec-check`, then read spec against CLAUDE.md and the code by hand for what the checker cannot see. Every disagreement is a finding.
5. **Ask** — the artifact (`design/spec-pass/pass-N.html`), led by a worked fragment so the shape can be judged before the items. **Findings** (drift against a ruling already made — fixed unless vetoed) separate from **questions** (genuine choices, recommendation first). Every item self-contained: the sentence or row quoted, reasoning, an example. Answers save in-page; *Copy all answers* gives the paste-back block.
6. **Fold** — answers land in SPEC.md (version bump) and SURFACE.md; history leaves for `design/SPEC-REASONING.md` with `→ why:` pointers; QUESTIONS.md spends the block and releases what is unused; code changes the answers imply are their own commits. Re-read every hot shared file immediately before editing it.
7. **Assert** — extend `spec-check` so each newly tabular rule is checked from then on. Never touch `packages/` in the extraction steps; where spec and code disagree, record it — changing code is a separate decision.

## Conventions

- Windows machine, PowerShell 5.1 — see global CLAUDE.md traps (no Get-Content/Set-Content rewrites; use the Bash tool for `gh` and multi-line git).
- **Checking a mockup:** serve `design/` over localhost (a tiny node http server) and measure the DOM with the browser tools — geometry, computed styles, element counts. **`file://` URLs are refused by the extension.** Two traps: the automation tab runs **backgrounded**, so `requestAnimationFrame` never fires, CSS transitions don't run, `:hover` never applies, and `setTimeout` is clamped to ~1s. Anything animated must be driven by stubbing the animation helper, and hover states can only be verified by reading the CSS rule. **A stalled sequence in that tab usually means rAF, not a bug.**
- **Mockup fixtures:** one array of items, each carrying its own content, progress and state — **never parallel literals kept in sync by hand**. Content is hand-authored; `sim-harness` has no session-state exporter, and the fixture holds diffs, rationales and captions the engine has no opinion about anyway.
- **The phase ladder:** `npm run ladder` (`scripts/ladder-walk.mjs`, against a running server) presses ⏭ five times and asserts the **surface** at each rung — a stagehand that reports thirty proposals and a page that draws none of them agree with each other and are both wrong. `--to=<rung>` stops and leaves the document standing, which is the eyeballing mode; `--seed=<n>` reproduces one. It drives the bar rather than the route, because the bar is what a person uses. See the `phase-ladder` glossary entry.
- **The founder's walk:** `node scripts/founding-walk.mjs` drives a blank arrival to a settled constitution headless and prints every task in the order the founder meets it — the rail, the clause, the card's strings and its commit row. It is how a STYLE.md pass over the founding is read, and it is the same drive the setup-probe's founding scenario uses.
- **Probe discipline:** any change to `cards.js` re-runs `design/tools/session-probe.js`; any change to the setup page re-runs `design/tools/setup-probe.js`. Both runs at one window size, both pages from scroll 0.
- **CI runs `npm run typecheck` (tsc --noEmit per workspace) as well as tests, lint and build — run all four before calling a tree green.** 2026-08-21: a push went red on typecheck alone, because the server test file hand-typed the view and the day's new fields never reached those types; vitest does not type-check tests, so tests passing says nothing about it.
- No deploys or pushes without Ed's say-so. **Since 2026-08-20 a push to `main` is a deploy** — CI fires the Render hook and verifies docs.vote afterwards (decision 476), so there is no gate behind the push. Committing is free; pushing is a decision.
- **Two sessions often run at once** (Ed, 2026-08-20). Three rules follow, each learned from something that went wrong the same evening. **Commit before compacting or clearing** — not because compaction touches the disk (it does not; files survive), but because it takes with it the only record of *what* the half-finished file in the tree was for: this session found `CLAUDE.new.md` and `part2.md` untracked with nothing to say what they were. **Claim a question number by writing it into QUESTIONS.md**, never by using it in a commit message or in chat — two sessions took 482 for the same question within an hour, because one of them held its numbers in prose where the other could not see them. And **re-read a long shared file immediately before editing it**, since the other session may have rewritten it since you last looked; the loser of a concurrent write is whoever wrote first.

## What goes in this file

The admission rule, written down because the two prior extractions had none and the file regrew past its pre-extraction size within two days of each — 52.7 KB out on 2026-08-20, 60.1 KB out on 2026-08-22, and 139 KB standing when this pass began. **Extraction without an admission rule only resets the clock.**

**This file is loaded whole into every session.** It is the operative reference and nothing else: the **names**, the **gotchas**, and the handful of conventions above. Before adding anything, ask which of four things it is.

1. **A rule** — what the surface does, what a control does, who may change what by which route. It goes to `SURFACE.md`, `SPEC.md` or `design/STYLE.md`, and what comes back here is a § pointer. A rule stated in two places is a rule that will shortly be true in only one of them.
2. **A reason** — why a thing is the way it is, what it replaced, what was tried and rejected. It goes to `design/DECISIONS.md`, or to `design/SPEC-REASONING.md` for anything keyed `R-nnn`. Nothing here has to argue for itself.
3. **A gotcha** — something that broke, why it broke, and the shape of the mistake so it is recognisable the next time. It goes to *Gotchas* above, and it is the one kind of thing this file is the sole owner of. There is **no byte budget**, because a budget would press hardest on exactly these.
4. **A name** — a new part of the system. It goes to the glossary above as a name, a one-line job, a `[file|symbol|concept]` kind and pointers. **If the entry wants a second paragraph, that paragraph is one of the first three things and belongs somewhere else.**

`npm run spec-check` holds the shape: every glossary bullet that names something leads with a backticked name and declares its kind, no *Gotchas* bullet leads with one, `file` and `symbol` kinds resolve, and every `SPEC §`, `SURFACE §` and `STYLE.md T` pointer lands on a section that exists. What it cannot tell you is that a paragraph is a rule wearing a name’s clothes — that is what the four questions are for.

The same discipline for the plan documents, taken from `design/MOBILE.md`, which is the only document in the set that did not go stale: **declare precedence in the opening** (*SURFACE wins where they disagree*), **cite rather than restate**, **schedule the edits to other documents rather than performing them in parallel**, and carry **per-stage acceptance criteria with file:line evidence**.

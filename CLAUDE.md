# draft — project conventions

A group drafting engine: patches race, blind pairwise judgments rank them, adoption clears a rising confidence bar. SPEC.md is the source of truth for the mechanism; when spec and code disagree, the spec wins until Ed amends it.

Design reasoning — why a thing is the way it is, what it replaced, what was tried and rejected — lives in `design/DECISIONS.md`. This file is the operative reference: names, rules, and constraints that would bite if broken.

## V1 product decisions

- Target context: constitutional conventions for Newspeak House cohorts. Rosters typically 5–10, conventions 15–20; design must not preclude 100+/1000+ instances, but v1 tunes to small rosters (data-efficiency over throughput).
- Hosted multi-tenant web service; magic-link auth against roster emails. Invitations send from **docs.vote** via **Resend** — the sending domain was the real hosting decision.
- Documents are Markdown, rendered as rich text; usually a few pages, long-document behavior stays in scope for sim experiments.
- TypeScript end-to-end; engine-core is a pure, dependency-free library shared by sim-harness and product server.
- Sim personas are LLM-powered on a cheap model (Haiku-class) and speak the same participant API as human clients — no sim backdoor; mixed human/bot sessions are a goal.
- UI north star: **suggestion-mode with escalation** — the default surface reads like Google-Docs-style inline suggestions with approval; the race view is the escalation state that appears only where suggestions collide or stakes demand ceremony. Most of a session should feel like approving typo fixes; the machinery earns its visibility.

## The two kinds of decision

**A constitutional decision is one that would make past decisions mean something different.** That is the test. Disclosure, visibility, quorum, the approval threshold, the membership and whether the document ends at all are constitutional; the starting text, title, link, closing date, grant, drip, stake and AI proposals are ordinary; a member's name and picture are neither.

The line can fall **inside** one setting: the window is one question whose answers include *never*, so moving the closing date is ordinary and removing the ending is constitutional — **the route belongs to what a motion changes, not to what card it sits on** (Q329).

A `motion` is the act of proposing a change to any setting, and its route is a fact about the *setting*: an **ordinary** motion is an ordinary race judged at the threshold; a **constitutional** one is a unanimous vote on the proposed amendment — one refusal keeps what stands, and until everyone has answered, what stands stands. The founding collects minima by construction (§9.0a); unanimity is the one decision rule with no quorum question inside it to govern, which is what keeps the bootstrap shut at both ends. SPEC §9.6–9.7.

**Five rules that follow:**

1. **The test is time-indexed** (§9.6a): before judging opens there are no past decisions for a change to re-rate, so **before the start nothing is amended — only set**. The founder re-sets settings and re-shapes the membership freely until judging opens.
2. **Membership begins at first arrival.** An invitation is an invitation *to become* a member; invitees are listed as *invited* and count toward nothing (which is what keeps one unopened email from blocking judging). A roster change while founding answers are being collected is a **ground shift**: answers stand, their authors are notified and may revise.
3. **Delegate the decision, not the field** (Q341). A delegated question collects exactly the binding scalar — the threshold **at the close**, the quorum **number** in the founder's chosen form (count or share) — while the machinery it rides on (the ramp's shape and start, quorum's form) is pacing or wording, stays with the founder, and is **ordinary by the test**, since every adoption is recorded against the threshold as it stood. Hence two threshold cards (✒️ *How sure must the room be?*, constitutional; 📈 *Rising Approval Threshold?*, ordinary) and one quorum card with two eyebrows, *Asked as* and *The number*. Quorum floor: F = max(Q, min(⌈E/3⌉, F_max)), §4.2.
4. **Constitutional settings default to the members, ordinary ones to the founder** — the room can take an ordinary one back cheaply and cannot take a constitutional one back at all. **Retired everywhere, 2026-08-21** (Ed's no-defaults instruction; SPEC §9.0a amended, v0.63, closing Q511): nothing arrives delegated, because a default holder states an answer the founder has not given. Every setting is born convenor-held, both powers intact, its question **shut** — and **delegation is the act that opens a blind question**. It was retired at the surface first and left in the module for a day, where it did real damage: ten questions collected from the instant of creation, and because `settled()` counts a members-held setting as settled, the live page released the whole constitution at once instead of a clause at a time. **A holder radio reports an act, never a default**: delegated reads *roster*, held-and-set reads *founder*, held-and-unset stays blank — after the change `holder === 'convenor'` no longer tells *kept* apart from *untouched*, so the page must not render the two alike.
5. **The opening question is the amendment question**, so amending a constitution needs no new object anywhere on the surface.

**The tab you click does not move.** A closed pile takes margin **0** — its right edge is exactly the card's left edge, not `.chipcol`'s 14px paragraph gutter. The vertical half is corrected in `fitBand` **by measurement, never a constant**: the open card's strip hangs off `.headclause`, which starts below the card's own label, and the label wraps on a narrow card. Verify 0px in both axes after any change here.

**Two rules the constitution band carries.** *A constitutional setting a member had no say in is a decision they are owed*: it sits in their rail until they open it and press **OK**, then it leaves the rail and lives in the band — reading is not enough. **Ordinary** settings the founder made never enter a member's rail at all: nothing is being asked, and anybody may motion one at the threshold whenever they like. And *the headings are the people* — the founder's name and picture under **Convenor**, the membership's under **Membership**, arriving as they do, with a placeholder before anybody has one.

## Names

- **founder** — whoever creates and administers a document. "Convenor" persists in SPEC and engine code as the older term, like `roster` for membership. Never "admin".
- **member** — anybody on the roster; the surface says **membership**, the code says `roster`.
- **clerk** — a founder who is not a member. A founder who is one appears in the membership like anybody else.
- **Observers are not members** — no observer role, no row, no name, no picture, because nothing about them is known. Who may read a document is settled entirely by 🌍 **Visibility** (the engine's `chamber`). SPEC §9.3's observer role does not survive this (Q324).
- **approval threshold**, never "the bar" — and it is a **confidence**, not a vote share (§5), which the copy has to keep saying.
- **proposal rate**, never "economy" or "tokens".
- **participant** is the engine's word for whatever speaks the `participant-api`; it must never appear in anything a member reads.
- **"ordinary" never appears on the surface** — the kind pair is glyphic: **✏️** *anybody may propose changing it*, **🏛️** *a constitutional change, the ask-everyone route*. The word *constitutional* does survive on the surface; "ordinary" is engine vocabulary only.
- **"ceremony" never appears on the surface.** What is left is what happens: everyone answers, and when everyone is ready the document begins.

## Glossary — named parts

Use these names in all discussion, commits, and code. Literal and stable beats elegant.

**Retired names**, kept here so a lookup lands: `document-creation`, `founding-ceremony` and `setup.html` (the surfaces) → `session-view.html`; `edit-wallet` → `propose-wallet`; `setup-piles` → `constitution-section`; `drafting-desk`, `arrival-bar`, `bounty-board`, `redline` → gone. Their reasoning is in `design/DECISIONS.md`.

**Engine (mechanism, no UI):**
- `engine-core` — session state machine: candidates, races, adoptions, the adoption-threshold ramp, tokens/refunds, adoption floors, certification. Caught up through 367(b): quorum rides the floor (§4.2), the drip is real minutes (`tokenDripMinutes`), amendments bind races in flight (`amend`/`standing-set`, §4.3 anchor re-seating — **a bar never jumps**), suspension is lapse engine-side (§9.5a), **setting races** carry ordinary motions (a candidate is a patch *or* a typed value; adoption of a value is a verdict, applied only by the host's `setStanding`, which is also the ground shift). The hash is pure TS — the engine is node-free and browser-loadable.
  **Adoptions batch on the cooldown metronome** (§4.2): when the cooldown from the last batch elapses, every race clearing threshold+floor adopts at once — the document changes at most once per cooldown, by as much as the room decided. One decision per race per batch (the ready set snapshots before anything lands), oldest first, each rebasing the next; **a race adopts only once somebody has actually judged in it**. `Session.tick(t)` is the host clock; the bridge's `tick(t)` rides it and the server's minute timer drives the bridge, so a due batch lands even in a quiet room. The cooldown is **engine tuning, never constitutional**, and a cleared race is never surfaced as waiting — no ⏳, since deriving "cleared and cooling" would be a standings reveal.
- `adoption-threshold` — the confidence bar a challenger's win-probability must clear; ramps over the session window (wall clock). Formerly "θ"; always use this name.
- `patch-engine` — text machinery: diffs, footprints, three-way merge, rebase, surgery.
- `overlap-gates` — the three-gate classifier for colliding patches: textual composition → semantic composition → rivalry.
- `ranking-model` — Bradley–Terry preference model (per race) + global salience model, with active pair sampling and deadlock detection.
- `router` — v/c_p feed ordering, hot set, exploration/salience streams, floors-aware serving, notification digests.
- `event-log` — append-only hash-chained log, seeded RNG, participant receipts.
- `dedup-gate` — submission-time duplicate check (embeddings, edit distance, LLM equivalence) plus behavioral dedup probes.
- `race-labeler` — advisory naming and typing of disputes: a name ("treasurer oversight") and a type (copy-edit · substantive · structural) per race, from the oracle's `describeRace`, with a deterministic nearest-heading + excerpt fallback. Outside the state machine — labels never enter the event log or gate anything; type is stored but not wired to routing (Q49).
- `document-modes` — two **independent** settings, both fixed at creation (SPEC §9.0). **Window**: an end datetime, or none (perpetual) — one question with one answer, which is why the surface asks it on one card (⏰ *When does it end?*) with *never* as an option. **Threshold shape**: fixed, or ramping. A windowed document may hold its threshold fixed; only a perpetual one is *constrained*, since a ramp needs an endpoint to ramp toward. A window gives you the closing ceremony and a drip paced against it; without one the drip runs against real time and the document ends by `freeze`.
- `founding-ceremony` — the stage before any drafting where the membership sets quorum and threshold: each member states, blind, the *lowest they will accept*, and the document takes the **maximum** of each. A consent rule rather than a vote, which is what lets it escape the constitutional bootstrap — the result satisfies every stated minimum by construction. Distribution published without names (SPEC §9.0a).
- `sign-out` — a member declaring they are done, choosing **holding** (they stay in the quorum base, so enough of them freezes the document — *I do not consent to you finishing without me*) or **abstaining** (they leave the base — *I trust you to finish up*). Plain silence is never sign-out; judgments already cast keep counting afterwards; the base is the whole roster minus abstainers, never the active remainder (SPEC §9.5).
- `freeze` — what happens when active plus holding falls below quorum: races park, participants are notified, the record is cut. A stall with an alarm, not a death — it thaws if enough return, and it is the perpetual mode's equivalent of a close.
- `coherence-auditor` — machine drafter patrolling document drift, fixed token budget. **Not a member**: it never judges and counts toward no quorum — it only proposes, and its proposals compete like anybody's. Surface card: **AI Proposals** 🤖, in the **Proposals** section beside the grant and the drip. **Ordinary**: defaults to the founder, judging never waits on it, a motion on it is a race; delegating it to the room stays the founder's option, most-restrictive answer winning.

**Design system** (session-view's, tokenised — every surface adopts it rather than inventing its own):
- `cards.js` — **the decision-card grammar, one implementation.** `window.CARDS`: a pure layer (the diff/markdown engine, `resultOnly`, `speakerHtml`, `fieldHtml`, `fieldOf`, the drawn TICK/CROSS and MARK glyphs) and a `make(env)` factory (lane bars, proposal blocks, `clauseHeadHtml`, commit row, `laneBoxHtml`, the open/close geometry, `keepStill`).
  **Env keys are functions read at call time** — session-view's `topUrgentId` is a reassigned `let`, its `chilled` a mutable Set, so nothing may be captured at `make()` time. Seams: `o.marks`/`o.washAttrs` on the clause head (the setup strip arrives pre-wrapped; session's chips/wash inject unchanged), `valAttr` (session radios speak `data-v`, setup composers `data-mval`, motion judging `data-motion`), a bindable speaker tooltip.
  **The contamination guard**: `design/reference/` holds byte-frozen copies of the whole page set (tag `post-merge` since 2026-08-21; `pre-cards` and `pre-constitution` before it) — **never edit; re-freeze on intentional change**, and check it has not rotted: the pre-constitution copy had drifted 200 diffs from HEAD before anybody noticed. `design/tools/session-probe.js` walks all 43 cards on `session-view.html?fixture=session` against the frozen one (whitespace-normalized outerHTML equality per card, plus 0.0px geometry). **Re-run it whenever cards.js changes, with both runs at one window size and both pages scrolled to 0** — a restored scroll position on one side reads as a constant offset in every rect.
  **The open/close animation uses `clip-path: inset(-60px -100px 0 -100px)`, never `overflow: hidden`** — the tab strip hangs entirely outside the card's box (`right: 100%`) and gets clipped for the whole motion otherwise. Related: the open paragraph needs `display: flow-root`, or margin collapse silently defeats fitBand's head-rule correction.
- `wash` — the lifecycle colour on any surface that carries one: a clause, a `clause-head`, a held-open gap, a queue entry. **Colour changes fade** rather than jump, over `--wash-ms` (700ms). Two things make that work: a queue entry's wash is painted as a **bar** (a `::before` whose width is the fill, `background-color` the hue) rather than a hard-stopped gradient, since **no browser interpolates between two gradients**; and because both rails are rebuilt wholesale on every render, each washed element is rendered wearing its **previous** colour and handed the new one after one forced reflow — **keyed by what the wash is *about***, so a clause and the head of the card that swallows it share one key and opening a card deepens the colour rather than repainting it.
- `room-pulse` — one grey dot in the topbar that beats once per action by anybody, yourself included. Deliberately **content-free**: no count, no direction, no author, so the only thing it can say is *the room is moving* — which is what keeps it inside SPEC §3.5, since a heartbeat with nothing in it cannot leak standings. Grey at rest and grey at its brightest; it beats by growing a ring, never by changing colour. In the mockup the other members are a timer on a fixed *irregular* cycle — irregular so it does not read as a machine, fixed so it can be QA'd.
- `me` — the user avatar at the right of the topbar. Initials by default. It is the only face on the surface, since everybody else's authorship is sealed (SPEC §3.4) — Q278 asks whether that stays true.
- `lifecycle palette` — **three** hues held as raw RGB channels: `--lc-open` 🔥💡⚔️ yellow, `--lc-weigh` 🌶️ pink, `--lc-yours` ✏️ **blue** (the accent), and one grey shared by `--lc-deciding` ⏳ and `--lc-closed` ✔✖↻. Two rules govern it:
  - **Grey means nothing is being asked of you** (not "you can no longer affect it" — a ⏳ race is revisable until it seals).
  - **Hot for actions, cold for information** — yellow and pink want something from you; blue and grey tell you where things stand.
  🔥 takes a **fixed alpha clear of the ramp's ceiling** (`FLAME_A`, 0.44) with the urgency ramp running underneath it, because 🔥 means *the question you are being asked next* — a category, and a ramp cannot express a category. Applied identically to the queue card's wash, the paragraph's wash and the gutter mark. **`--ok`'s green never leaves a card interior**; every *decided* state washes grey in a margin, and the two must not meet (Q264).
- **Open is said by depth, not by outline**: no active box carries a border — not the decision card, not the drafting box, not the open rail entry. `--shadow-xl` is the only step allowed to be big, and it is still a light source rather than a halo (offset grows with blur, blur near 2.5× it, contact shadow keeping the near edge crisp).
- **A settings choice is the session-view's own radio** — `.lanepick`, not a hand-rolled radio in a bordered box. A setting is exactly the judgment a lane bar puts: *one of these, or none of them yet*. No box: a rule drawn round each option competes with the radio for the job of saying which one is chosen.
- `--primary` — the one accent: every wire, every selection, every control that is on. `--ok` — green, meaning *decided* and only that.
- type scale `--t-lead / --t-body / --t-ui / --t-small / --t-cap / --t-micro` — **and every piece of text on the surface is on it.** One exception is allowed to be a literal: a **glyph** size (`.achip` 1.125rem, the glyph buttons 1.2/1.35rem, the serif italic *I* 1.05rem), which tunes a silhouette to a box rather than text to a scale. The `.eyebrow` class carries the single upper-case label treatment at `--t-micro` and **names its users in its own selector** (`.headlab`, `.fieldlab`, `.rtag`, `.rechead`, `.pwhere`); each user keeps colour and layout and nothing else. A shared rule nothing points at is not a shared rule.
- **Nothing rebuilds under a press** (Ed's live walk, 2026-08-22). A hold is a gesture in progress — the glyph is in the air, the control is under the pointer — and the live page re-renders on a **4s poll**, so a poll landing inside a hold replaces the very control being held. Four consequences, all fixed:
  - The **pen hold captured its button at `pointerdown`** and called `b.click()` a second later; a poll in between left `b` detached, and a click on a detached element never reaches the `document` listener that commits. The act vanished in silence — no command, no error, the card just sitting there. The hold belongs to the **open card**, not to one DOM node, so it re-finds the live commit control when its own has gone.
  - **Both polls now defer while a hold is in flight** (`penHold || SESSION.holding`). `SESSION.holding` is a module-level flag because the propose hold's own state lives inside `renderDoc` and is re-created by every render.
  - **A completed hold clicks for you, and that click must not look like the user's.** The hold's timer calls `click()` itself; the browser then delivers its own click on pointerup, and the guard that swallows that trailing half keyed on a flag the synthetic click had just cleared — so **every held commit on this surface fired twice** (Ed, 2026-08-22: *during the birth, after entering the email, the Link card opens immediately*). The two clicks land a millisecond apart, and `closeThen` defers a commit behind the collapse, so `S.open` is still set when the second arrives and the whole act runs again: on 📧 that is a second `POST /api/docs` asking for the address the first had just **reserved for itself**, answered truthfully with *that address is taken* — and the page did the right thing with the wrong news and walked the founder back to 📍. `penHoldFired` is true for exactly the duration of the synthetic dispatch, so inside a click handler it means precisely *this click is the hold's own*, and the flag is left alone for the real one. The birth also guards its own send, so no future double press can take the address from the creation making it.
  - **A wallet must not depend on its own animation finishing.** The pencil storm hides `#wallet` so the pencils can fly into an empty one and hands it back in its completion callback — so a missed callback hid a just-granted power *permanently*. A `setTimeout` safety net runs the same restoration; whichever lands first wins. This is the likeliest cause of Ed's earlier *I have no pencil wallet*.
- **`npm run journey`** (`scripts/journey-walk.mjs`, against a running dev server) walks **founding → 🍾 → grants → caret → proposal** on the live path and exits non-zero if it stalls. It exists because the two probes and `founding-walk.mjs` all drive the fixture, which never reaches `hydrateS`, never polls and never sends a command — every live-only bug this project has hit was invisible to them. It cannot exercise the three holds faithfully (backgrounded tab: no rAF, no transitions), so it reports the final Propose as *not exercised* rather than failed. Two harness rules it encodes, both learned the hard way: **press with a real pointer** (a synthetic `.click()` does not choose a rung), and **scroll the control into view first** — a pointer cannot press what is off screen, which is what made the founding look stuck at 💤. Two assertions were added on 2026-08-22 after both of that day's birth bugs walked straight past it: **the rail immediately after the save must hold the pen alone**, and **no `/api/` response may be 4xx or 5xx** — the double send's 409 was invisible because the surface *recovered* from it, so the check belongs at the wire rather than at the outcome. The refusal net does not reproduce the double send headless (the second click needs a real collapse animation to still find `S.open` set), so it is a net for the future rather than the proof of that fix; the rail assertion is the one that bites.
- **An opening decision card focuses its main decision** (Ed, 2026-08-22), which **reverses** the previous day's narrower rule (*only onto something you type into, never a radio*). That rule did not leave a radio card's focus alone — it sent the caret **past** the decision into the rationale lane, so a card asking *which of these* put your cursor in the box for explaining yourself. The main decision is **the first interactive control in the card's own DOM order**, which needs no per-card list because the grammar already puts the decision first: values, then rationale, then commit row. The commit row is excluded (it is where a card ends, never what it asks), and within a group the focus goes to the **chosen** option, because where a decision stands is the decision. **Focus is not a press**: a radio is put under the keyboard, never selected.
- **An opened card puts the caret in its composer** (Ed, 2026-08-21): if a card is asking for words, you can type them without going to find the box. Three rules keep it from being a nuisance. **Only on the opening** — the surface re-renders wholesale on every change and on the 4s poll, so focusing anywhere else would pull the caret out from under what you were typing; the seam is render's `S.open !== lastOpen` branch, plus `morphTo`, which sets `lastOpen` itself and so never reaches it. **Only onto something you type into** — the first text-entry control in the card's own DOM order (the title lane, a text or email input, a textarea, a contenteditable lane), never a radio, a date, a button or a bare number, so a radios-only card like 🌍 leaves focus alone. **And `preventScroll`**, because the card is mid-expansion and a focus that scrolls fights the animation it is landing in. The caret goes *after* any text already there, so typing extends a draft that survived a close rather than sitting in front of it.
- **A card closes when the answer becomes the document's, and stays open when the card is still the place the thing lives** (the rule, written down 2026-08-21 after Ed asked whether there was one). Setting it, saving it, acknowledging it — the clause below now says what you decided, so the card has nothing left to show and it closes. **Discarding closes it too** — the discard is a finished act and the close is its receipt (see `commit-row grammar`). **One deliberate exception survives**: **a judgment in the session view**, because it is revisable and the field is still worth reading — *submitting does not close the card*. Two that looked like exceptions were not. **📧** had been one, on the argument that what happens next (the mail, the link) belongs to that card and the inbox opens over it — but the inbox is the founder's own, in another window, so a card left open after its one act gives the press no receipt at all; **the send closes it**, first press and 📨 alike, and a *refused* send re-opens it, since a refusal has nowhere else to be read (Ed, 2026-08-21). **🎩** never went through the closing path because it had a commit control of its own; since Q522 it commits where everything else does, and there is one commit path on the surface.
- **A commit row is one object, so its controls share a height**: 40px, set by the row, on every card. Width belongs to the control — a glyph button is 52px, a labelled one as wide as its label. **Disabled controls stay flat, and there is one flat** (Ed, 2026-08-21, asking for the card buttons to be consistent): grey ground, grey border, muted ink, no lift, for every control on the row. Three grounds had grown — 🗑️ white, ✒️ grey, and a *labelled* approve (🏛️ Hold to ask everyone, ✏️ Propose, ✒️ Begin) keeping the **full accent**, so a dead button wore a live button's colour. The cause was architectural: system.css, setup.css and the page's own inline block all described one button, the inline one loaded last and won, and every disabled rule lived in the two that lost. **Shape is the page's, ground belongs to what the button is** — no rule states a colour twice. The commit row is the card's bottom band everywhere; the three per-type notes and the lock note sit above it (Q310).
- **What you have typed survives a close** (Ed, 2026-08-21): every provisional value — a radio, a number, a motion's draft, the title lane, the invitation boxes — lives in `S` until **🗑️** takes it. Closing a card is not discarding. The two that broke this were the ones whose value lived only in the DOM (the invitation boxes) or was reset per opening (the title lane).
- **The open rail entry lifts, it does not jump**: the rail is rebuilt wholesale, so a clicked entry is a new element born already wearing the open shadow, and a transition cannot run from a value the element never had. `settleLift` paints it at rest, forces one reflow, then hands it the lift — the same technique the washes use.

Choosing ranks the questions and never touches either text. It swallows only the clause it stands in, so the far one is quoted.
    - **One rail entry, not one per clause**, standing at the earlier of its two and titled over four lines: `Prioritise:` / the first name / `vs` / the second — on one line the two names collide and truncate, which is the one thing an entry made for weighing them must never do.
  - `suggestion-anchor` — **the prose carries no highlight.** All the lifecycle colour lives in the `clause-tab` beside it, so the charter reads as a charter and the margin does the work. **The geometry stays** — `.anch` and the `clause-head`'s block keep their padding and negative margin, because the card-opening motion is measured against the two boxes being identical — only the paint is gone. A held-open gap keeps its wash: there is no text there to leave unmarked, so the colour *is* the thing. **A clause is not a button**: clicking it puts a caret in it (`always-on-typing`), and the gutter mark is what opens the decision card. `insert-anchor` is a blank held-open gap with 💡 beside it. `chip-gutter` is the left margin they stack in, keeping the prose column unbroken.
  - `clause-tab` — the object in the `chip-gutter`: one per live or filed decision at that clause, carrying its `lifecycle mark`, and the **only** way into a decision card from the document. **An object, not a glyph with a hit area**: a resting ground in its own lifecycle hue, contact shadow, 34×30 with the glyph above document size, lifting on hover and going *down* on press.
    - **On an open card they are literally its tabs.** **The strip does not reorder** — a tab strip is a fixed set of places you move a highlight around; lifting the active tab to the top deals the column again and moves the tab out from under the pointer. The gutter pile only ever opens its *front* tab, index 0 in the same stack order the strip uses, so the mark you clicked is still index 0 when the card arrives.
    - The active tab **grows 8px out to the left** (the only direction available, since the column is right-aligned onto the card's edge). **The 8px goes on `padding-left` as well as `width` so the glyph does not move**, and the extra width is pushed out with a **negative right margin** — widening alone moves the glyph, which is the one thing the card-opening motion is built on it not doing. Rounded on the left only so the right edge is a joint; the active one deepened and 6px wider so it carries under the card's left edge with no seam, casting leftward only.
    - It **keeps its colour when active** rather than taking the card's white — the gutter holds the only lifecycle colour on that side of the screen. A flatter glyph set is Q288.
  - `tab-stack` — what a clause's tabs are when the card is **closed**: a *pile*, front tab as the object, the rest sliding up under it leaving a sliver each; opening expands it into the strip down the card's side. Four rules:
    - **The front is what most wants you**, and that order is deliberately *not* `KEEP_ORDER`. The contents rail ranks by what must not be lost (✏️ above 💡, since a proposal of yours carries the largest remaining act); the stack ranks by what most wants you (✏️ *below* 💡, since your own work is asking nothing). **Retention and priority are different questions with the same-looking answer.**
    - **The slivers carry hue, not a count** — an orange sliver says *something here is urgent* without drawing a flame. Mixed stronger than a tab face (42% against 18%), because four pixels of an 18% mix is a smudge, and squared at the top, because a radius eats a 4px band.
    - **The slivers are inert**: one stack, one target. A 4px sliver is not a control to ask anybody to hit.
    - **The pile is fitted, not fixed** — `fitStacks()` runs after layout over the whole gutter and shrinks each stack's peek until it reaches no further than the next mark, which makes two anchors claiming one strip *structurally* impossible. Each `chipcol` is laid out inside its own anchor and knows nothing of the one below, so this cannot be done locally. Residual: a single tab is 30px and cannot shrink (Q308).
    **Filed decisions live at the bottom of the strip as a pile of their own**, newest at the top (only the top glyph shows when shut, so it must be the last thing that happened here), lining up when clicked — **the card grows to hold them**, a floor on its height rather than a height. They stay **off the closed gutter**, where the pile is live-only. Decided-but-unread is **not** filed and stays with the live tabs, because it is still asking for its OK.
    **Code gotcha**: hole rectangles in the wire-shadow clip must be **merged before being punched** — the clip is one `evenodd` path, and two overlapping holes XOR back to solid. A pile overlaps by 26px of every 30.
  - `decision card` — **the clause, opened.** It *replaces* its paragraph rather than opening beneath it, so the thing being proposed about appears exactly once on screen. The shape is a post and its replies: `clause-head` → `proposal-block` per candidate → commit row, hairline between each band. One card for every type, differing only in what fills the two halves — quick card: clause + one proposal; race: clause + two; patch: the same per site, plus the ↑/↓ stepper; sealed record: clause + the whole field ranked. **Submitting does not close the card.**
    - **Opening**: the card grows from exactly the height at which only its `clause-head` is showing, so frame one looks like the document with the paragraph still in it; the gap slides open beneath the clause and the field fades in 60ms behind the motion. **The clause itself never moves** (0px on every card type).
    - **Closing runs the card's whole box back onto its paragraph** — height down to the `clause-head`'s own washed block, padding away, eyebrow shut, lift and white ground gone, top edge descending by exactly what it stops holding. It must not stop at head height: opening gets away with that discrepancy because it happens in the frame of the click; closing puts it after the motion, which is the one order the eye will not forgive.
    - The card's own mark closes it.
    - `clause-head` — the clause itself at the top of the card, at document size and colour, under an eyebrow. **It keeps the clause's own highlight and its own gutter mark**: the washed block inside it is given the same box as a `.anch` paragraph — **same negative margin, same padding** — so the wash lands on the same rectangle and the `.chipcol` inside it lands in the same gutter column. The mark you clicked moves 0px in both axes and the clause text sits on the prose's own axis to the pixel, which is what makes the card read as the clause *opening* rather than as something replacing it. The card's mark is the same control it was in the gutter, so it also closes the card; other live suggestions at that clause stack under it exactly as beside the paragraph.
      It carries a `lane-bar` **exactly when the judgment can keep the clause**: on a quick card or a patch the incumbent is one of the two things being judged; on a **race** both candidates are challengers and nothing on the card can vote to keep it (the adoption-threshold decides that, §5), so there the head is reference only.
    - `proposal-block` — one candidate: its wording stating its own change against the clause (see `result-only`), then its rationale behind a `sealed-speaker`, then its `lane-bar`. The reply in the post-and-replies shape, which is why each argument sits with its own wording instead of being mapped column-to-column.
    - `sealed-speaker` — the featureless head-and-shoulders disc a rationale sits behind: the disc says a *person* is speaking, and its blankness says the rest, because authorship is sealed until the record (§3.4). **Drawn, not a plain circle** — a bare disc reads as a bullet, and a bullet says *list item*.
    - `result-only` — a proposal states **the text as it would stand**, with what it adds marked green and **nothing struck through**; normal people struggle to read redlines. One rendering everywhere — card, lane and record — so a proposal reads the same wherever you meet it. Two rules:
      - **A marking floor**: below half the new text surviving from the clause, the proposal states itself plainly. This is what keeps a race candidate, usually a whole rewrite, from being lit green end to end (Q92).
      - **Punctuation is its own token**, without which a clause that gains a comma marks the whole word.
      Accepted blind spot: in a stacked card a proposal whose only change is a *partial* cut looks like the clause above it. The worst case is covered — a proposal removing the text **entirely** would render as blank, indistinguishable from unchanged, so it says *(all text removed)*, **drawn as a pseudo-element** so it is never content the caret can land behind or `htmlToMd` can serialise.
    - `lane-bar` — the control strip at the foot of each text: a radio marked **Prefer this** / **Preferred**, plus ✏️ *edit this*. The rule that governs it: **a lane may carry what is about that lane, and nothing else**. The radio is the **loudest thing in the lane** — UI size at weight 600 on a 1.5px edge with a 15px dot, above the ✏️ beside it — because the radio *is* the judgment, and a card whispering the one act it exists for is the wrong way round. (Clicking a clause cannot also mean *I prefer this*: that gesture is `always-on-typing`.)
    - `commit row` — the card's bottom band, and the third radio.
      - **Indifferent** at the left, the last of the trio — **labelled, not drawn as 🤷**, because every candidate carries a radio and *neither of these* is plainly one more answer to the same question. It stays out of the lanes because it is a judgment about the *pair* (§3.2). 🤷 has left the surface.
      - The green **✓** at the right, always present and **greyed until something is chosen** — an absent button gives the row no shape and the reader no idea what finishing looks like.
      - **❄️ appears only on the 🔥 card**, immediately left of the ✓ and paired with it. It is **not an act on the judgment** — nothing is skipped, recirculated or decayed and no evidence is touched — but a **toggle on the flame**: it takes the card out of the running for 🔥, so it drops to an ordinary 💡 and the next most urgent question takes the flame. Reversible, because a mood is. **Pressing it closes the card**; un-pressing it does not. Its pressed state is the toggle, so it needs no words.
      - **Every radio on a card lines up down its left edge**, locked cards included — the alignment is what says the three are answers to one question.
  - `deadlock-card` — what a ⚔️ race opens into, and **its own card**, not a race card with a bridge button: by the time you see it the race has nothing left to ask you (§8.3b). In order: the clause at the head (reference only); the card's one raised voice, in the band the field would otherwise start (*the room has stopped moving on this; more judging will not decide it*); **everything in flight**, full width, **oldest first** (arrival order is the only ordering that is not a ranking); what you said, as one grey record line however many pairs it took; then **the desk**.
    - **The reading room and the desk are one surface**: the desk is the `editing-card`'s own `laneBoxHtml` and commit row (🗑️ far left, hold-✏️ far right) — the wordings are what you write *against*. Before a draft exists the lane holds the clause and is a real editor; the first keystroke opens the draft with that character applied (`always-on-typing` on a box instead of a paragraph, the same function underneath).
    - Every wording carries ✏️ *propose edit* (the lane bar minus its radio), so you write from whichever came closest.
    - The field is `result-only` **with the floor forced off** — left alone the floor marks most of the field, silencing the card that most needs the marking. The floor exists to stop a *lane* being lit beside its incumbent; here the incumbent is at the head and comparison is the point.
    - **No standings, and therefore no lock.** Rankings would be the briefing §3.5 permits only where there is no live judgment to contaminate, and would have to close your judgments with it. Wordings and rationales are already public, so showing them leaks nothing and needs no rule.
    - Open: at eight candidates the comparison you need is each-against-the-others, which nothing computed against the incumbent can draw (Q301, Q305, Q306).
  - `sealed record` — every entry opens one, filed ones included.
    - **The card starts at the top of the field**: on a decided card the top of the ranking *is* the clause — the winner where it carried, the incumbent where it held — so the top entry **is** the head, keeping the head machinery (gutter mark, washed block on the paragraph axis, opening geometry) and giving up only its label, which becomes its own rank, outcome and score. No box round the winner: that would be a fourth device saying *this one*.
    - Then **one list of the whole field**, winner first, each proposal full width with its win probability as a **percentage**, right-aligned into a column.
    - **The incumbent is in the list**, at a true place: every score is the probability that proposal beats the current text, so the current text sits at **50% by construction**. That makes the column say *these two beat what we had and those three did not*. It wears a dashed edge, shows its 50% at a muted weight (a construction, not a measurement), and is labelled *the text it replaced* or *the text that stood*.
    - **Whichever text is the clause at the head does not print itself again.** The threshold is stated **once**, in the field label. Plus quorum met and what you did.
    - Unread decisions **pin themselves until you press OK**, bottom right where every card commits; reading alone does not clear it. **The button is a word, not a glyph** — a tray-and-arrow reads as *download*, an archive box as *storage*, an eye as *seen* rather than *done*; there is no glyph in common use for *I have taken this in*.
  - `evidence-meter` — closeness-to-resolution as a magnitude, never a direction. Lives **only** on queue cards, where the wash's fill *is* the bar; decision cards carry no progress. The bar sits on a **ground of the same hue across the whole card**, so the card *is* its colour and the bar says how far through that colour you are. The ground is **derived from the bar's own colour by swapping its alpha**, so the two cannot drift apart; and the bar's leading edge is drawn a second time in that colour, or at the bottom of the urgency scale the fill is too close to its own ground to show an edge — and the edge is the whole of what a progress bar says.
  - `queue-wire` — for the open judgment only, the run from its entry down the gutter into the place in the charter it refers to. Only the open judgment ever draws one, so its existence already says *this is the open one* — which is why it takes no accent.
    - **A patch is joined at its cards, not in the rail**: the cable lives in the `chip-gutter`, leaving each `clause-tab` by its **left side**, curving onto a run just outside the column, and curving back into the next. Joining the pointers said *these three entries are one*; the thing that is actually one is the three clauses. **A cable that never enters the column cannot cross a tab in it.** Consecutive segments share the run and overlap at each middle tab, giving the middle sites a drop off a through-run rather than two cables meeting end to end.
    - **6px, opaque, and exactly the colour of its own queue card** — the card's wash **composited over white and painted on a white cable of the same weight**: opaque as an object, identical as a colour, so it carries urgency for free. **Match what the eye sees, not what the token says**: a hue darkened toward the ink reads as a different object from the card, and a translucent cable shows the charter through it.
    - **A 10px cap at the document end only.** The far end is the wire's actual claim, *this clause, here*; the rail end is a card that has already said it.
    - **The colour layer is painted at full strength inside a group carrying the alpha**, so every run, cap and link composites exactly once — painting translucent shapes over each other doubles the alpha wherever they meet.
    - **Lifted to the cards' own height** with the same three-layer light as `--shadow-xl`, on the container, so the union of underlay and colour casts one shadow rather than two of the same cable stacked.
    - **Its shadow is clipped so that *every* card on the surface is punched out of it**, not only the two it joins — the rail is a layer of cards at one height, and a thing does not shadow its own layer. **Two nested groups: clip on the outer, the shadow's offset on the inner.** A `clip-path` resolves *after* the referencing element's own transform, so both on one group move the holes down with the shadow and leave a 3px sliver along every card's top edge. `clause-tab`s are punched out too — a tab is an object at card height sitting *outside* its card's box.
    - It **follows its card's colour** over the same 700ms as the washes, by the same technique: born wearing the previous colour, handed the new one after a forced reflow, **keyed by the judgment** so a redraw at a new scroll position does not re-run the fade. A sealed dot carries no wash, so its wire takes the fixed grey the dot wears.
  - `section-toggle` — the fold triangle, mirrored in the contents rail and the document gutter.
- `race-card` — the judging surface: contested text, two candidates, A/B/indifferent/propose-C. In the UI it is the session-view's escalation state. **`design/race-card.html` is stale and must not be copied from** — it predates the whole current card grammar (yellow cards, per-candidate verdict buttons, "Option A/B" labels). Session-view is the only current word on card design; bringing race-card.html forward is a rebuild, and Q70 asks whether it should exist at all.
- `composer` — **not a surface**: you compose by editing the charter. Its parts:
  - `always-on-typing` — every clause **and every heading** carries a caret (a section that cannot be renamed is one the charter cannot revise), and **every input into one is intercepted**: the charter is never edited in place; the keystroke opens the `editing-card` with that character already applied. This is what makes a caret in a constitutional document safe, and it is why **the gutter mark is the only way into a decision card** from the document (so the mark is a 24×22 target, not a bare glyph).
    - A selection may **span blocks**: the run is flattened to one string with a newline between blocks, the selection located in *that* string, and the edit applied to it — so deleting a whole section is one edit to one piece of text, and comes out as **one candidate, not a patch**. The block type travels with each origin, so a heading still reads as a heading in the lane.
    - The caret is the accent blue, and **where it lands is announced when the surface moved it** — a short flash that shrinks onto the bar and fades. It fires on the one caret move the reader did not make (typing in the charter carries the caret into the proposing lane of a card that has just appeared), **never on a click**. Its shape is not settable — CSS gives `caret-color` and nothing else (Q281).
  - `editing-card` — the same `decision card` shape as everything else: the clause at the head, your draft as the single reply, your rationale a field in the `sealed-speaker`'s own slot — **inside the same lifted box as the wording**, at one height with a hairline between, because the two are one *editing surface* and you write the sentence in the place and at the weight everybody else will read it in. Propose and Cancel below. A full-width lane, not two side-by-side columns: the original is one line up and your additions are **marked green as you type**.
    - Free to open; **the edit is spent at Propose**, which is where the price is said in words.
    - The rationale's placeholder (*We should change this because…*) is drawn **out of the text flow** — as inline content it sits in the line and puts the caret after it, so an empty field looks like text you cannot delete.
    - **The lane preserves spaces**: it rewrites its own markup on every keystroke so the green marking can follow the caret, and a trailing space collapses to nothing in the re-render unless the blocks are `pre-wrap`.
  - `lane-controls` — the only editing controls on the surface, top right of the `editing-card`'s box: **B**, *I*, and a two-segment **Rich / Markdown** toggle. Not in the topbar — nothing outside an open card can be edited, so a topbar toolbar would be greyed all session. **A candidate's text *is* markdown**: rich mode serialises back through `htmlToMd` so editing rendered never silently drops the emphasis it is showing, and switching view **converts the caret offset**, because markdown mode counts the syntax characters and rich mode does not. **Inline only** — bold, italic, code; block structure is carried by the run of clauses.
  - `draft-site` — a **run of adjacent blocks** (clauses, headings, or both) replaced by one piece of text. **One site is one candidate**, whatever the run's length: a patch is one judgment shown at several *separate* places, and a run is one place that happens to be several blocks long. Editing across a paragraph break joins them; editing somewhere else makes a second site and the draft becomes a patch in the making — a card at each place, one Propose, the entries cabled. **One draft at a time, because there is one caret** (Q266).
  - `propose-edit` — ✏️ on either lane of a decision card: start writing from *that* wording rather than the charter's. `Propose something else` survives only on a **deadlocked** race, where a bridge is the ask rather than an extra.
  - `proposal-row` — **the proposal's whole lifecycle is one commit row**, and the row does not move as the proposal advances: 🗑️ at the **very left** throughout, the commit control at the **very right**.
    - As a draft the right slot is a bare **✏️ you hold down**, and while you hold it a pencil **flies out of the `propose-wallet` and lands on the button**. The proposal goes in when it arrives; **letting go early sends it home with nothing spent** — what you are holding down for is the time it takes the edit to travel. Reduced motion: fades at the wallet, arrives at the button, same duration.
    - Once pressed the right slot becomes a pressed *✏️ Submitted* and 🗑️ becomes `withdraw`, **which hands the edit back in full** (§3.3a) and is why your entry stays pinned. **Withdrawing flies the pencil home** in 640ms, leaving in the pose it arrived in and landing flat. Discarding an *unproposed* draft flies nothing — nothing was spent.
    - **The flight is an arc, drawn fresh each time**: a quadratic bowed off the straight run by a signed amount, sampled into keyframes (a shape that changes every flight cannot be a CSS rule). Let go and it runs backwards along its own arc at four times the speed.
    - **The edit in flight is drawn exactly once, both directions** — outbound the wallet keeps its count and leaves the traveller's slot empty (not spent until it lands); inbound it holds its old count until the pencil is in it. **Render state, not a poke at the DOM**: the drip rebuilds the wallet every second and will otherwise put the flying pencil straight back.
    - 🗑️ is an ordinary outline button, not a red one — discarding and withdrawing are real things to do, not dangers. Propose is **blue, not green**: green means *decided*, and a submission starts one.
  - Not yet rebuilt: `the-briefing` (heat-panel, standings-panel, camp-map, why-digest, graveyard, bridge-bar) and `dominated-account` — the briefing as an escalation state unrolling above the lanes. **§3.5 permits it only where there is no live judgment left to contaminate**, so a cold-start edit gets no briefing at all.
- `motion` — a proposal to change a **setting** rather than a clause: the one act by which a settled document changes its own rules. **There is no new object on the surface for amending a constitution** — an ordinary motion is a decision card with a value where the prose would be, and a constitutional motion is the card that asked the question in the first place, live again. SPEC §9.6.
  - **Ordinary**: the race machinery whole — judged pairwise against the value as it stands, carrying at the threshold with quorum; rival values join the same race (propose-C over values); entries stake and refund like text proposals; identical values dedup by equality; a standing-value change mid-race is a ground shift.
  - **Constitutional**: a unanimous vote on the proposed amendment — accept it or keep what stands, one refusal keeps what stands, blind while it runs. **It settles live**: accept / keep / abstain, abstention never blocks, carrying the moment every *currently active* member stands at accept-or-abstain with zero keep. **No electorate snapshot** — the base is re-checked on every answer and roster event.
  - **An invitation is a constitutional motion**, so one member who says no keeps a joiner out.
  - Raising one is free and open to any member, limited by the holder rather than the setting — **one 🏛️ out per member at a time**, returned whole on withdrawal or settlement.
  - **The mover stands at accept from the open** (proposers prefer their own proposals; §3.3 counts an author's own preference without asking) — revisable, and 🗑️ stays their way out. The consent card offers all three answers, the third labelled **Abstain**.
  - **A carried amendment binds races in flight**: races always run under the constitution as it stands, past adoptions keep their recorded threshold, and incoherence is a ground shift.
- `assembly-press` — how a constitutional motion is put: a **full ten-second hold** on the 🏛️ button, deliberately sillier than the flying pencil — the members' avatars convene one by one into a circle around it, so **the assembly is the progress meter**, complete exactly when the motion goes in. Release early and it disperses, nothing sent. The composer's commit flips between Propose (a click) and the hold as the typed value changes route. Pairs with the one-🏛️-per-member limit. Under reduced motion the seats appear without the spring but **the stagger stays**, because the ring is the meter.
- `gazette` — public feed of resolved outcomes; the chamber view is its ambient rendering.
- `bounty score` — resolvable disagreement × salience: the leverage a good draft would have on a deadlocked race. It does its ranking inside the `needs-you-queue`, where a ⚔️ race stands near the top of your own margin beside the clause it is about. (The public *bounty-board* tab is retired — the sidebar queue does its job.)
- `record-builder` — closing publication: final text + the record (rankings, camps, graveyard, care map, minority map, backlog, audit log).
- `spectator-api` — strictly-public engine-core projection (gazette, live candidates, deadlocked races, document): the only surface spectator views may consume (Q42, backlog).
- `spectator-commentary` — optional LLM commentating view for convention spectators, fed exclusively by the spectator-api; unlike the sim's omniscient `commentator`, it sees no private data (Q42, backlog).

**Tooling:**
- `server` — `packages/server` (`@draft/server`): the thin product host. node:http, no framework. `npm run server` at the root. **Live at docs.vote since 2026-08-20** — one Render service, the alpha home, CI deploying on green and verifying the live host afterwards with `npm run verify` (PRODUCTION.md stage 10). **A push is a deploy.** Since 2026-08-20 23:40 the service is **stateless apart from Postgres** — no disk (498b), so deploys can hand over without stopping the old instance; the one nine-minute 502 was a health-check path changed in the same push as the route it pointed at — **land the route first**. Operator docs: `docs/OPERATING.md` (the map) and `docs/runbooks/` (the procedures — deploys/health/shutdown, the Postgres cutover, backups and the drill).
  - **Two stores, one seam** (stage 6): `Persistence` is the interface; `FilePersistence` is the JSONL layout, `PgPersistence` one row per log entry (`event` as `text`, never jsonb — it rejects NUL and lone surrogates). **The cutover is two variables**: `DATABASE_URL` is where Postgres is (inert alone); `DRAFT_STORE=pg` is the switch (set in production since the cutover), absent means `file`, and it never falls back silently. `dist/draft-tools.mjs` (import · export · verify · drill · repair-tail) moves the log between stores with **every rolling hash asserted identical** — the oracle is never relaxed, and no JSONL log is ever deleted. `pg` is the project's only runtime dependency, spent there alone.
  - **`/healthz`** says build · store · document count · uptime; one request line per response with the query string dropped (tokens travel there); **SIGTERM drains every write chain before exiting** (stage 7), which is what makes frequent deploys safe.
  - **Text proposals ride the same whitelist** (stage 8): `propose-text {baseVersion, hunks, why}` and `withdraw-text {candidate}`, strictly shaped, refused before the document has begun; a stale base is a 400 carrying the engine's *patch targets version N; current is M*. The member view serves the document **as the engine holds it** (`text`, `textVersion`) and a blind projection of the text races — `clauses` (contested spans, candidates with hunks and rationale and a `mine` flag, my own `judged`/`shifted`), `mine`, `records` (the last fifty outcomes; `p`/`threshold` only where the event carried them), ten cards, the wallet — never standings, never an author, never anybody else's judgments (§3.5). **One cookie per document** (`draft_session_<docId>`; the legacy name is read for its own document only). **Faces are reserved server-side**: `faces.ts` mirrors setup.js's lists (asserted equal by test); one grapheme, pictographic, not furniture, not somebody else's — *Taken — X got there first.*
  - **One hash-chained JSONL log per document; loading is `replay`.** The log is the only persistence, and **it holds answers in plaintext, so the data dir is as sensitive as the room.**
  - Magic-link auth: creation *is* the §9.7a mail, an invitation link arrives you (§9.6a), revival is logging in. Stateless HMAC cookies.
  - **A command whitelist injects the authenticated actor — a body never names who acts** — with `view()` as the only member read. `tick()` each minute for lapse/freeze. Every historical slug routes.
  - Mail rides the fold: Resend via `RESEND_API_KEY`, else `data/outbox.jsonl` + console.

## Conventions

- Windows machine, PowerShell 5.1 — see global CLAUDE.md traps (no Get-Content/Set-Content rewrites; use the Bash tool for `gh` and multi-line git).
- **Checking a mockup:** serve `design/` over localhost (a tiny node http server) and measure the DOM with the browser tools — geometry, computed styles, element counts. **`file://` URLs are refused by the extension.** Two traps: the automation tab runs **backgrounded**, so `requestAnimationFrame` never fires, CSS transitions don't run, `:hover` never applies, and `setTimeout` is clamped to ~1s. Anything animated must be driven by stubbing the animation helper, and hover states can only be verified by reading the CSS rule. **A stalled sequence in that tab usually means rAF, not a bug.**
- **Mockup fixtures:** one array of items, each carrying its own content, progress and state — **never parallel literals kept in sync by hand**. Content is hand-authored; `sim-harness` has no session-state exporter, and the fixture holds diffs, rationales and captions the engine has no opinion about anyway.

It is how a STYLE.md pass over the founding is read, and it is the same drive the setup-probe's founding scenario uses.

- **CI runs `npm run typecheck` (tsc --noEmit per workspace) as well as tests, lint and build — run all four before calling a tree green.** 2026-08-21: a push went red on typecheck alone, because the server test file hand-typed the view and the day's new fields never reached those types; vitest does not type-check tests, so tests passing says nothing about it.
- No deploys or pushes without Ed's say-so. **Since 2026-08-20 a push to `main` is a deploy** — CI fires the Render hook and verifies docs.vote afterwards (decision 476), so there is no gate behind the push. Committing is free; pushing is a decision.
- **Two sessions often run at once** (Ed, 2026-08-20). Three rules follow, each learned from something that went wrong the same evening. **Commit before compacting or clearing** — not because compaction touches the disk (it does not; files survive), but because it takes with it the only record of *what* the half-finished file in the tree was for: this session found `CLAUDE.new.md` and `part2.md` untracked with nothing to say what they were. **Claim a question number by writing it into QUESTIONS.md**, never by using it in a commit message or in chat — two sessions took 482 for the same question within an hour, because one of them held its numbers in prose where the other could not see them. And **re-read a long shared file immediately before editing it**, since the other session may have rewritten it since you last looked; the loser of a concurrent write is whoever wrote first.

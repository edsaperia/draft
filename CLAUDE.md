# draft — project conventions

A group drafting engine: patches race, blind pairwise judgments rank them, adoption clears a rising confidence bar. SPEC.md is the source of truth for the mechanism; when spec and code disagree, the spec wins until Ed amends it.

Design reasoning — why a thing is the way it is, what it replaced, what was tried and rejected — lives in `design/DECISIONS.md`. This file is the operative reference: names, rules, and constraints that would bite if broken.

## V1 product decisions

- Target context: constitutional conventions for Newspeak House cohorts. Rosters typically 5–10, conventions 15–20; design must not preclude 100+/1000+ instances, but v1 tunes to small rosters (data-efficiency over throughput).
- Hosted multi-tenant web service; magic-link auth against roster emails. Invitations send from **docs.vote** via **Resend** — the sending domain was the real hosting decision.
- Documents are Markdown, rendered as rich text; usually a few pages, long-document behavior stays in scope for sim experiments.
- TypeScript end-to-end; engine-core is a pure, dependency-free library shared by sim-harness and product server.
- Sim personas are LLM-powered on a cheap model (Haiku-class) and speak the same participant API as human clients — no sim backdoor; mixed human/bot sessions are a goal.
- UI north star: **suggestion-mode with escalation** — the default surface reads like Google-Docs-style inline suggestions with approval; the race view is the escalation state that appears only where suggestions collide or stakes demand ceremony. Most of a session should feel like approving typo fixes; the machinery earns its visibility.

## The two kinds of decision

**A constitutional decision is one that would make past decisions mean something different.** That is the test. Disclosure, visibility, quorum, the approval threshold, the membership and whether the document ends at all are constitutional; the starting text, title, link, closing date, grant, drip, stake and AI proposals are ordinary; a member's name and picture are neither.

The line can fall **inside** one setting: the window is one question whose answers include *never*, so moving the closing date is ordinary and removing the ending is constitutional — **the route belongs to what a motion changes, not to what card it sits on** (Q329).

A `motion` is the act of proposing a change to any setting, and its route is a fact about the *setting*: an **ordinary** motion is an ordinary race judged at the threshold; a **constitutional** one is a unanimous vote on the proposed amendment — one refusal keeps what stands, and until everyone has answered, what stands stands. The founding collects minima by construction (§9.0a); unanimity is the one decision rule with no quorum question inside it to govern, which is what keeps the bootstrap shut at both ends. SPEC §9.6–9.7.

**Five rules that follow:**

1. **The test is time-indexed** (§9.6a): before judging opens there are no past decisions for a change to re-rate, so **before the start nothing is amended — only set**. The founder re-sets settings and re-shapes the membership freely until judging opens.
2. **Membership begins at first arrival.** An invitation is an invitation *to become* a member; invitees are listed as *invited* and count toward nothing (which is what keeps one unopened email from blocking judging). A roster change while founding answers are being collected is a **ground shift**: answers stand, their authors are notified and may revise.
3. **Delegate the decision, not the field** (Q341). A delegated question collects exactly the binding scalar — the threshold **at the close**, the quorum **number** in the founder's chosen form (count or share) — while the machinery it rides on (the ramp's shape and start, quorum's form) is pacing or wording, stays with the founder, and is **ordinary by the test**, since every adoption is recorded against the threshold as it stood. Hence two threshold cards (🌡️ *How sure must the room be?*, constitutional; 🪜 *Rising Approval Threshold?*, ordinary) and one quorum card with two eyebrows, *Asked as* and *The number*. Quorum floor: F = max(Q, min(⌈E/3⌉, F_max)), §4.2.
4. **Constitutional settings default to the members, ordinary ones to the founder** — the room can take an ordinary one back cheaply and cannot take a constitutional one back at all. **Retired everywhere, 2026-08-21** (Ed's no-defaults instruction; SPEC §9.0a amended, v0.63, closing Q511): nothing arrives delegated, because a default holder states an answer the founder has not given. Every setting is born convenor-held, both powers intact, its question **shut** — and **delegation is the act that opens a blind question**. It was retired at the surface first and left in the module for a day, where it did real damage: ten questions collected from the instant of creation, and because `settled()` counts a members-held setting as settled, the live page released the whole constitution at once instead of a clause at a time. **A holder radio reports an act, never a default**: delegated reads *roster*, held-and-set reads *founder*, held-and-unset stays blank — after the change `holder === 'convenor'` no longer tells *kept* apart from *untouched*, so the page must not render the two alike.
5. **The opening question is the amendment question**, so amending a constitution needs no new object anywhere on the surface.

**The tab you click does not move.** A closed pile takes margin **0** — its right edge is exactly the card's left edge, not `.chipcol`'s 14px paragraph gutter. The vertical half is corrected in `fitBand` **by measurement, never a constant**: the open card's strip hangs off `.headclause`, which starts below the card's own label, and the label wraps on a narrow card. Verify 0px in both axes after any change here.

**Two rules the constitution band carries.** *A constitutional setting a member had no say in **and was here for** is a decision they are owed*: it sits in their rail until they open it and press **OK**, then it leaves the rail and lives in the band — reading is not enough. A setting that predates their arrival is owed nothing and is simply what the document says (SPEC §9.0a, §9.6a). **Ordinary** settings the founder made never enter a member's rail at all: nothing is being asked, and anybody may motion one at the threshold whenever they like. And *the headings are the people* — the founder's name and picture under **Convenor**, the membership's under **Membership**, arriving as they do, with a placeholder before anybody has one.

## Names

- **founder** — whoever creates and administers a document. "Convenor" persists in SPEC and engine code as the older term, like `roster` for membership. Never "admin".
- **member** — anybody on the roster; the surface says **membership**, the code says `roster`.
- **clerk** — a founder who is not a member. A founder who is one appears in the membership like anybody else.
- **Observers are not members** — no observer role, no row, no name, no picture, because nothing about them is known. Who may read a document is settled entirely by 🌍 **Visibility** (the engine's `chamber`). SPEC §9.3's observer role does not survive this (Q324).
- **approval threshold**, never "the bar" — and it is a **confidence**, not a vote share (§5), which the copy has to keep saying.
- **proposal rate**, never "economy" or "tokens".
- **participant** is the engine's word for whatever speaks the `participant-api`; it must never appear in anything a member reads.
- **"ordinary" never appears on the surface** — the kind pair is glyphic: **✏️** *anybody may propose changing it*, **🏛️** *a constitutional change, the ask-everyone route*. The word *constitutional* does survive on the surface; "ordinary" is engine vocabulary only.
- **"ceremony" never appears on the surface.** What is left is what happens: everyone answers, and when everyone is ready the document begins.
- **`draft` already means a candidate patch** everywhere in this project, so the thing the surface makes is a *document* or a *charter*, never a draft.

## Glossary — named parts

Use these names in all discussion, commits, and code. Literal and stable beats elegant.

**An entry is a name, a one-line job, and pointers — nothing else.** Rules belong to `SPEC.md`, `SURFACE.md` or `design/STYLE.md`; reasoning belongs to `design/DECISIONS.md`; post-mortems belong to *Gotchas* below. Every entry declares its kind in square brackets — `[file]` a path in the repo, `[symbol]` a name that appears literally in the source, `[concept]` a design idea or a planned part — and `npm run spec-check` checks the first two. See *What goes in this file* at the end.

**Retired names**, kept here so a lookup lands: `document-creation`, `founding-ceremony` and `setup.html` (the surfaces) → `session-view.html`; `edit-wallet` → `propose-wallet`; `setup-piles` → `constitution-section`; `drafting-desk`, `arrival-bar`, `bounty-board`, `redline` → gone. Their reasoning is in `design/DECISIONS.md`. The two superseded notes files, `design/session-view.notes.md` and `design/setup.notes.md`, were deleted 2026-08-23; `SURFACE.md` holds what was live in them and git holds the rest.

**Engine (mechanism, no UI):**
- `engine-core` [symbol] — the session state machine: candidates, races, adoptions, the threshold ramp, tokens and refunds, adoption floors, certification. Pure TS, node-free and browser-loadable. Rules: SPEC §2–§8, §13. Adoption batching and the cooldown metronome: SPEC §4.2. Amendments binding races in flight: SPEC §4.3.
- `adoption-threshold` [symbol] — the confidence bar a challenger’s win-probability must clear; ramps over the session window on wall clock. Postponing the close never lowers it. Formerly "θ"; always use this name. SPEC §4, §5, §4.3.
- `patch-engine` [concept] — text machinery: diffs, footprints, three-way merge, rebase, surgery.
- `overlap-gates` [concept] — the three-gate classifier for colliding patches: textual composition → semantic composition → rivalry. SPEC §4.
- `ranking-model` [concept] — Bradley–Terry preference model per race, plus a global salience model, with active pair sampling and deadlock detection. SPEC §5, §8.
- `router` [concept] — feed ordering, hot set, exploration and salience streams, floors-aware serving, notification digests. SPEC §8.
- `event-log` [concept] — append-only hash-chained log, seeded RNG, participant receipts. SPEC §11.
- `dedup-gate` [concept] — submission-time duplicate check (embeddings, edit distance, LLM equivalence) plus behavioral dedup probes. SPEC §5.
- `race-labeler` [symbol] — advisory naming and typing of disputes, from the oracle's `describeRace` with a deterministic nearest-heading fallback. Outside the state machine: labels never enter the event log and gate nothing (Q49).
- `document-modes` [concept] — two independent settings fixed at creation: the **window** (an end datetime, or none) and the **threshold shape** (fixed, or ramping). Only a perpetual document is constrained to a fixed threshold. SPEC §9.0. The surface asks the window on one card, ⏰, with *never* among its answers — SURFACE §8's `ORDER` row 14.
- `founding-ceremony` [concept] — the blind collection before any drafting: each member states the lowest they will accept and the document takes the maximum of each. A consent rule rather than a vote, which is what lets it escape the constitutional bootstrap. SPEC §9.0a; the surface's order is SURFACE §8.
- `sign-out` [concept] — a member declaring they are done, **holding** (staying in the quorum base) or **abstaining** (leaving it). Plain silence is never sign-out. SPEC §9.5.
- `freeze` [symbol] — what happens when active plus holding falls below quorum: races park, participants are notified, the record is cut. A stall with an alarm, not a death, and the perpetual mode's equivalent of a close. SPEC §9.5.
- `coherence-auditor` [concept] — machine drafter patrolling document drift on a fixed token budget. **Not a member**: it never judges and counts toward no quorum. Surface card: 🤖 **AI Proposals**, SURFACE §8 `ORDER` row 24. SPEC §10.

**Design system** (session-view's, tokenised — every surface adopts it rather than inventing its own):
- `cards.js` [file] — **the decision-card grammar, one implementation** (`design/cards.js`, `window.CARDS`): a pure layer and a `make(env)` factory over it. Card kinds and their commit rows: SURFACE §9. Its inventory and its seams: `design/DECISIONS.md`.
- `wash` [symbol] — the lifecycle colour on anything that carries one: a clause, a `clause-head`, a held-open gap, a queue entry. Colour changes fade over `--wash-ms` (700 ms), keyed by what the wash is *about*, so a clause and the head of the card that swallows it share one key.
- `room-pulse` [concept] — one grey dot in the topbar that beats once per action by anybody, yourself included. Deliberately **content-free** — no count, no direction, no author — which is what keeps it inside SPEC §3.5. Grey at rest and grey at its brightest; it beats by growing a ring.
- `me` [symbol] — the user avatar at the right of the topbar, initials by default: **your own** face, not the only one. Who is in the room is membership, and public (SPEC §9.0c); what SPEC §3.5a seals is whose name is on a proposal (Q854).
- `lifecycle palette` [concept] — three hues held as raw RGB channels (`--lc-open`, `--lc-weigh`, `--lc-yours`) and one grey shared by `--lc-deciding` and `--lc-closed`. ## Gotchas — what bites

The post-mortems: what broke, why it broke, and the shape of the mistake so it is recognisable the next time. No other document claims this material — SPEC and SURFACE state rules, `design/DECISIONS.md` states reasons, and none of them state *the thing that went wrong*. Kept as one findable set rather than scattered through the glossary, because most of them are only useful to somebody who does not yet know they are about to make the mistake.

**Design system:**
- **Open is said by depth, not by outline**: no active box carries a border — not the decision card, not the drafting box, not the open rail entry. `--shadow-xl` is the only step allowed to be big, and it is still a light source rather than a halo (offset grows with blur, blur near 2.5× it, contact shadow keeping the near edge crisp).
- **A settings choice is the session-view's own radio** — `.lanepick`, not a hand-rolled radio in a bordered box. A setting is exactly the judgment a lane bar puts: *one of these, or none of them yet*. No box: a rule drawn round each option competes with the radio for the job of saying which one is chosen.
- **A hold is released by letting go, and by nothing else** (SURFACE W16). The charter’s ✏️ Propose released on `pointerleave`, which a render under the pointer fires by itself. It takes the ✒️ pen hold’s shape now, released on `pointerup`/`pointercancel` only; `spec-check` asserts the release set and `npm run journey` fails on the pre-fix page. Post-mortem: `design/DECISIONS.md`.
- **Nothing rebuilds under a press** (Ed's live walk, 2026-08-22). A hold is a gesture in progress — the glyph is in the air, the control is under the pointer — and the live page re-renders on a **4s poll**, so a poll landing inside a hold replaces the very control being held. Four consequences, all fixed:
  - The **pen hold captured its button at `pointerdown`** and called `b.click()` a second later; a poll in between left `b` detached, and a click on a detached element never reaches the `document` listener that commits. The act vanished in silence — no command, no error, the card just sitting there. The hold belongs to the **open card**, not to one DOM node, so it re-finds the live commit control when its own has gone.
  - **Both polls now defer while a hold is in flight** (`penHold || SESSION.holding`). `SESSION.holding` is a module-level flag because the propose hold's own state lives inside `renderDoc` and is re-created by every render.
  - **A completed hold clicks for you, and that click must not look like the user's.** The hold's timer calls `click()` itself; the browser then delivers its own click on pointerup, and the guard that swallows that trailing half keyed on a flag the synthetic click had just cleared — so **every held commit on this surface fired twice** (Ed, 2026-08-22: *during the birth, after entering the email, the Link card opens immediately*). The two clicks land a millisecond apart, and `closeThen` defers a commit behind the collapse, so `S.open` is still set when the second arrives and the whole act runs again: on 📧 that is a second `POST /api/docs` asking for the address the first had just **reserved for itself**, answered truthfully with *that address is taken* — and the page did the right thing with the wrong news and walked the founder back to 📍. `penHoldFired` is true for exactly the duration of the synthetic dispatch, so inside a click handler it means precisely *this click is the hold's own*, and the flag is left alone for the real one. The birth also guards its own send, so no future double press can take the address from the creation making it.
  - **A wallet must not depend on its own animation finishing.** The pencil storm hides `#wallet` so the pencils can fly into an empty one and hands it back in its completion callback — so a missed callback hid a just-granted power *permanently*. A `setTimeout` safety net runs the same restoration; whichever lands first wins. This is the likeliest cause of Ed's earlier *I have no pencil wallet*.
- **`npm run journey`** (`scripts/journey-walk.mjs`, against a running dev server) walks founding → 🍾 → grants → caret → proposal on the **live** path, which is the path the two probes and `founding-walk.mjs` never reach. It fails on the pre-fix page at *propose-text null · wallet 5→5 · held false*. What it encodes and why each assertion is there: `design/DECISIONS.md`.
- **The card lifecycle lives in `SURFACE.md`** (spec pass 1, 2026-08-22): what opens a card, what it focuses, what closes it, what 🗑️ puts back, the one commit row and the hold ladder — rules C1–C5 and the lifecycle rows L1–L9. Kept here in one line each because they bite: a card opens only on a click and focuses its main decision; closing is not discarding; a card closes when its answer becomes the document's (a judgment too, since Q576 — files as ⏳); 🗑️ is always live, puts back only un-actioned input, and is the only bin; one commit row, one flat disabled look, 40px.
- **The open rail entry lifts, it does not jump**: the rail is rebuilt wholesale, so a clicked entry is a new element born already wearing the open shadow, and a transition cannot run from a value the element never had. `settleLift` paints it at rest, forces one reflow, then hands it the lift — the same technique the washes use.

**Product (UI and founding):**
- **The power clause, the 🛡️ radios, the power option as a proposal block and the `why`** are STYLE.md T6–T9: one sentence in Ed's vocabulary (*a member proposes; the membership passes and rejects; the Founder assents, refuses and amends*), the two 🛡️ radios saying *veto* and naming the setting (`PW_NOUN`, fallback *this*) with the negation bold, each option a `.propblock` at document size with *Choose this / Chosen* beneath, a blocked option dimming whole. ## The spec pass

How SPEC.md and SURFACE.md are kept tabular, checkable and free of history (Ed, 2026-08-22 — he considered a skill and declined; the procedure lives here, the guarantee is `npm run spec-check`). Re-runnable; pass 1 was 2026-08-22 (Q540–Q582), and passes 2+ mostly live in steps 4–7.

1. **Claim a question block first** — re-read the tail of QUESTIONS.md, write the reservation into its Spent-numbers section, commit that alone. Numbers are claimed in the file, never in chat or a commit message.
2. **Extract** — walk one rule family (governance: who may change what, by which route, when; communication: what a member is told, what a control does); pull every normative sentence into a candidate row or rule. Leave derivations, history and commentary behind.
3. **Normalise** — find the general rule covering the most rows; restate the rest as instances. **Isolate** — whatever will not fit becomes a numbered exception: what it is, which rule it breaks, why, and the ruling behind it.
4. **Diff** — `npm run spec-check`, then read spec against CLAUDE.md and the code by hand for what the checker cannot see. Every disagreement is a finding.
5. **Ask** — the artifact (`design/spec-pass/pass-N.html`), led by a worked fragment so the shape can be judged before the items. **Findings** (drift against a ruling already made — fixed unless vetoed) separate from **questions** (genuine choices, recommendation first). Every item self-contained: the sentence or row quoted, reasoning, an example. Answers save in-page; *Copy all answers* gives the paste-back block.
6. **Fold** — answers land in SPEC.md (version bump) and SURFACE.md; history leaves for `design/SPEC-REASONING.md` with `→ why:` pointers; QUESTIONS.md spends the block and releases what is unused; code changes the answers imply are their own commits. Re-read every hot shared file immediately before editing it.
7. **Assert** — extend `spec-check` so each newly tabular rule is checked from then on. Never touch `packages/` in the extraction steps; where spec and code disagree, record it — changing code is a separate decision.

## Conventions

- Windows machine, PowerShell 5.1 — see global CLAUDE.md traps (no Get-Content/Set-Content rewrites; use the Bash tool for `gh` and multi-line git).
- **Checking a mockup:** serve `design/` over localhost (a tiny node http server) and measure the DOM with the browser tools — geometry, computed styles, element counts. **`file://` URLs are refused by the extension.** Two traps: the automation tab runs **backgrounded**, so `requestAnimationFrame` never fires, CSS transitions don't run, `:hover` never applies, and `setTimeout` is clamped to ~1s. Anything animated must be driven by stubbing the animation helper, and hover states can only be verified by reading the CSS rule. **A stalled sequence in that tab usually means rAF, not a bug.**
- **Mockup fixtures:** one array of items, each carrying its own content, progress and state — **never parallel literals kept in sync by hand**. Content is hand-authored; `sim-harness` has no session-state exporter, and the fixture holds diffs, rationales and captions the engine has no opinion about anyway.
- **The phase ladder:** `npm run ladder` (`scripts/ladder-walk.mjs`, against a running server) presses ⏭ five times and asserts the **surface** at each rung — a stagehand that reports thirty proposals and a page that draws none of them agree with each other and are both wrong. `--to=<rung>` stops and leaves the document standing, which is the eyeballing mode; `--seed=<n>` reproduces one. It drives the bar rather than the route, because the bar is what a person uses. See the `phase-ladder` glossary entry.
- **The founder's walk:** `node scripts/founding-walk.mjs` drives a blank arrival to a settled constitution headless and prints every task in the order the founder meets it — the rail, the clause, the card's strings and its commit row. It is how a STYLE.md pass over the founding is read, and it is the same drive the setup-probe's founding scenario uses.
- **Probe discipline:** any change to `cards.js` re-runs `design/tools/session-probe.js`; any change to the setup page re-runs `design/tools/setup-probe.js`. Both runs at one window size, both pages from scroll 0.
- **CI runs `npm run typecheck` (tsc --noEmit per workspace) as well as tests, lint and build — run all four before calling a tree green.** 2026-08-21: a push went red on typecheck alone, because the server test file hand-typed the view and the day's new fields never reached those types; vitest does not type-check tests, so tests passing says nothing about it.
- No deploys or pushes without Ed's say-so. **Since 2026-08-20 a push to `main` is a deploy** — CI fires the Render hook and verifies docs.vote afterwards (decision 476), so there is no gate behind the push. Committing is free; pushing is a decision.
- **Two sessions often run at once** (Ed, 2026-08-20). Three rules follow, each learned from something that went wrong the same evening. **Commit before compacting or clearing** — not because compaction touches the disk (it does not; files survive), but because it takes with it the only record of *what* the half-finished file in the tree was for: this session found `CLAUDE.new.md` and `part2.md` untracked with nothing to say what they were. **Claim a question number by writing it into QUESTIONS.md**, never by using it in a commit message or in chat — two sessions took 482 for the same question within an hour, because one of them held its numbers in prose where the other could not see them. ## What goes in this file

The admission rule, written down because the two prior extractions had none and the file regrew past its pre-extraction size within two days of each — 52.7 KB out on 2026-08-20, 60.1 KB out on 2026-08-22, and 139 KB standing when this pass began. **Extraction without an admission rule only resets the clock.**

**This file is loaded whole into every session.** It is the operative reference and nothing else: the **names**, the **gotchas**, and the handful of conventions above. Before adding anything, ask which of four things it is.

1. **A rule** — what the surface does, what a control does, who may change what by which route. It goes to `SURFACE.md`, `SPEC.md` or `design/STYLE.md`, and what comes back here is a § pointer. A rule stated in two places is a rule that will shortly be true in only one of them.
2. **A reason** — why a thing is the way it is, what it replaced, what was tried and rejected. It goes to `design/DECISIONS.md`, or to `design/SPEC-REASONING.md` for anything keyed `R-nnn`. Nothing here has to argue for itself.
3. **A gotcha** — something that broke, why it broke, and the shape of the mistake so it is recognisable the next time. It goes to *Gotchas* above, and it is the one kind of thing this file is the sole owner of. There is **no byte budget**, because a budget would press hardest on exactly these.
4. **A name** — a new part of the system. It goes to the glossary above as a name, a one-line job, a `[file|symbol|concept]` kind and pointers. **If the entry wants a second paragraph, that paragraph is one of the first three things and belongs somewhere else.**

**The eviction rule (Q736): a gotcha whose mistake is now caught by a named automated guard is the guard's job, not context's.** It reduces to one line — the failure, and the guard that catches it — and its post-mortem moves to `design/DECISIONS.md`. What stops the mistake recurring is the red build, not the paragraph; and a mistake with a guard on it is by construction the one least likely to bite again, so it is the cheapest thing in the file to evict and the most expensive to keep. Ed's *no byte budget* ruling (Q723–731) stands and is not touched by this: an **unguarded** gotcha is capped by nothing, because those are the ones that earn their place. The fix for a flagged gotcha is always to move the post-mortem, **never to stop naming the guard** — a rule that rewarded hiding the guard would be worse than no rule.

**The entry rule (Q737): every glossary bullet, at any depth, names something, declares its kind, and fits in one line.** A nested bullet is an entry like any other — that is the gap the prose sub-bullets grew in, five deep under `session-view.html` and eight under `constitution-section`, each of them a paragraph wearing a parent entry's clothes. A bullet that names nothing is one of the other three things above and has a home already; a bullet that names something and runs past the cap is a name with a rule, a reason or a post-mortem stuck to it, and the fix is to send that part where it belongs, not to re-word the sentence shorter.

`npm run spec-check` holds the shape: every glossary bullet names something and declares its kind, no *Gotchas* bullet leads with a backticked name, `file` and `symbol` kinds resolve, no glossary entry runs past 400 characters, no gotcha that names a guard runs past 400 either, and every `SPEC §`, `SURFACE §` and `STYLE.md T` pointer lands on a section that exists. What it cannot tell you is that a paragraph is a rule wearing a name’s clothes — that is what the four questions are for.

The same discipline for the plan documents, taken from `design/MOBILE.md`, which is the only document in the set that did not go stale: **declare precedence in the opening** (*SURFACE wins where they disagree*), **cite rather than restate**, **schedule the edits to other documents rather than performing them in parallel**, and carry **per-stage acceptance criteria with file:line evidence**.

# SPEC-REASONING — why the rules are the way they are

The reasoning behind SPEC.md's mechanism sections: the dated rulings, what each replaced, and what was tried and rejected. SPEC.md states rules only and points here as `→ why: R-nnn`. This file is to the spec what `design/DECISIONS.md` is to CLAUDE.md's glossary — mechanism reasons here, surface reasons there (Q564). Born 2026-08-22 in spec pass 1, from the history stripped out of SPEC.md v0.64, which covered **§9 Sessions** alone; the pass of 2026-08-29 (v0.90, `R-061` onward) added **§3 The move**, **§4 Resolution** and **§8 Routing**. The full prose of every superseded paragraph is in git — at tag `spec-v0.64` and earlier for pass 1, and at `SPEC.md` v0.89 for the sections the later pass took.

Entries are condensed, not verbatim. Each carries the ruling's date and question number so the QUESTIONS.md fold can be found.

---

**R-001 Nothing arrives delegated** (Ed, 2026-08-21, Q511; SPEC v0.63). Replaced §9.7's *constitutional settings default to the roster, ordinary to the convenor* (2026-08-18) and, before it, §9.0a's *everything delegated by default* (2026-08-17). The roster-default argument was that a default you must argue your way out of is stronger than a ticked radio. Three things were wrong with it in practice: a default holder states an answer the convenor has not given (a document one second old asserted ten settings belonged to the room); the questions opened at creation, so the roster could be answering before the convenor had seen one; and no surface can tell a default from a decision. What the old rule protected survives elsewhere: anonymity rests on the consent rule (R-013), and the room's claim on a constitutional setting is the delegate/reserve asymmetry (R-008). The cost, stated: a convenor who never delegates holds a constitution the room only ever gets to refuse. The §9.7 paragraph survived the fold of Q511 because Q511 was folded into §9.0a alone (spec pass 1, finding 543).

**R-002 The hat is a card** (Ed, 2026-08-21; spec pass 1, finding 545). §9.6a had the question as a *pre-checked row in the membership list* (2026-08-18, after a morning as *a roster fact, not a role*). Two rulings since made that false: *nothing is pre-answered* removed the pre-check, and the single-file founding needed a place for 🎩 between the register and the convenor's own row. It had been hard-coded *member by default*, which is exactly why it never appeared as a task. Locked at the start since §9.6a (2026-08-18): the right to re-tick oneself dies with the start.

**R-003 The Text is a setting like any other** (Ed, 2026-08-21, Q440). §9.7 had said *the starting text sits outside all of this… holding it crowns nobody* and *the text is delegated as soon as proposals open* (2026-08-19). "Starting" named the moment we were looking at it from, not a property of the setting. The start lays both powers down, so a drafting engine's default is that adoptions stand by themselves; the road to a held Text is a post-start reserve motion, after which each adoption is a 👑 question — assent over the drafting mechanism itself, the largest single power in the design, kept available for consistency. Reserving the text from creation is out of scope (Q387).

**R-004 The pen is a route** (Ed, 2026-08-22, Q530). *A unilateral rule change by the founder is still just a kind of amendment.* `MotionRoute` gained `'pen'` — the four verbs' own ladder of who had to agree, minus 🪶, which is not an amendment because nothing exists yet. A pen motion is never raised: it opens and settles in the one act, so it is only ever seen carried; folded from the `setting-set` event the log already carried, hash chain untouched. It carries a reason and is owed an acknowledgement by everybody who had no say. §9.0's *never by anybody's unilateral hand* (2026-08-16) predated v0.54's separately-held powers and had been false since.

**R-005 The pen works on constitutional settings after the start** (spec pass 1, finding 549, Ed 2026-08-22: stands). §9.0a v0.63 had said *a convenor who keeps one still cannot change it after the start except by the unanimous route* — written the day roster-defaults were retired, as the consolation for losing them. It described a constraint the module never had: `setSetting` succeeds post-start whenever ✒️ is held, on any setting, `by: 'crown'`, on the reasoning that the assent was consented on the way in (Ed's 366). The room's claim is not a bar on the pen but the asymmetry of R-008: delegation is the convenor's free act, and the reserve motion lands without their assent.

**R-006 The verb is delegation, and it is never a button** (Ed, 2026-08-19, superseding v0.51's *unreserve*; Q349(b)). *The title is delegated to the members* is a constitutional sentence among the setting's own options, preferred and committed like any value. From the moment proposing opens the founder may delegate anything they hold (*members can propose about anything that can change, apart from names and pictures*). A delegable setting delegates into its founding question until the start; past that the same verb is a hand-over — the settled value stands, only the holder changes. The title and link take no founding question because a blind consent question over prose is nonsense (there is no most-protective title for maxima to find); their hand-over waits for the text to confirm, which is when the outside world can first knock (the quill line).

**R-007 Two powers, separately held** (Ed, 2026-08-19, v0.54; Q403, Q454). Reservation had been one bit. Splitting it into unilateral change and assent gave every held-able setting four states, and Ed's two examples fixed their meaning: the membership (invite-and-remove kept unilateral while carried applications need nobody's accept) and the threshold (presented for assent while the founder cannot re-set it to taste). *Delegation is the state of holding neither power* (Q403) retired the 2026-08-17 third-value modelling — *the members decide* is no longer a value in any list; giving up the second power is the delegation. Timing (corrected the same day): giving up assent alone is available from creation; giving up unilateral change alone waits for the start, because the assent-only state is inert before it — no motions to assent to, and §9.6a lets the founder set freely regardless. The glyphs were 🔧 and 👑 until the glyph batch of 2026-08-21 (Q454) made them ✒️ and 🛡️ — a sword and a shield, one writes, one only refuses — which also freed 👑 to mean only the crown mark (finding 544). The engine keeps `unilateral` / `assent` as field names. Assent is the more restrictive power because it stops members doing what they have decided (Q395), which is why the blind applications question once ordered the holder states that way; since Q506 the pair is 🤝's own and the value is the join policy alone.

**R-008 The road back lands without assent** (Ed, 2026-08-19, Q394). A member moves to return a power to the founder's reserve; it carries like any amendment, one refusal keeps it the members'; it lands without the founder's assent, willing or lapsed alike, because the release from an unwanted crown is already in their hands — delegating is always their own free act. The payload may name one power or both, defaulting to both; the surface offers only the whole crown (*simpler wins, revisit at QA*). Two consequences Ed drew: the zero-reservation founder keeps one standing property — the room can restore powers to them; and a reservation returned to a lapsed founder is a constitutional monarchy (R-011).

**R-009 👑 and 📯** (Ed, 2026-08-18, closing Q379 the wide way). The mark's job is to say *this founder still holds levers*, whichever they are, so a founder who reserves anything is a 👑 and one who reserves nothing is a 📯 (🏴 until the same day). The rule's own test, Ed's words: *a convenor who reserves nothing and is not a member has no role at all* — a name in the record, the mechanism owing them nothing and asking them nothing. The one residual proves it: they may still change their own name and picture, which is §9.0c's category — identity binds nobody, so it is not a role.

**R-010 Reserved is assent, not silence, at the end of either route** (Ed, 2026-08-18, superseding v0.48). A reserved setting may still be moved against by its own kind's route — reservation never alters the route — but a change the members pass goes to the convenor as a 👑 question, accept or reject. v0.48 had exempted the constitutional route on the reasoning that consent already includes the convenor; it does not include a clerk, and a member-convenor's accept in the vote is their answer as a member, not their assent as holder. Since v0.54 this is the assent power specifically. There is no timeout because, where the pen is held, none is needed: a convenor sitting on a passed change is simply quiet, and quiet is what the lapse clock measures. The 👑 question is a task of its own kind — news-pinned, Accept / Reject (Q351) — which is SURFACE.md's E12.

**R-011 The crown lapses like a member** (Ed, 2026-08-18, superseding v0.48's holder transfer; Q351(b)). §9.5a's clock runs on the convenor too, and lapse is automatic abstention: while the crown sleeps, assent on reserved matters is granted automatically, so a change the members pass applies as if accepted; nothing changes hands; revival is logging in, restoring the requirement from that moment. A pending 👑 question at crown lapse passes. At the close it fails closed instead (Q467): lapse is absence and the close is everybody's deadline. What keeps a monarchy mortal is that an absent king can refuse nothing. Whose clock — whether administering counts as activity — is Q349(a).

**R-012 A consent rule rather than a vote** (Ed, 2026-08-16/17). Taking the maximum of stated minimums dodges the constitutional bootstrap — *by what quorum do you decide the quorum?* — because the result satisfies every stated minimum by construction and nobody can say the rules were imposed on them. The cost: the most demanding member sets the bar, and asking for full quorum is a perfectly reasonable position — a document that cannot move without everyone is a legitimate thing to want. Answers are blind for the reason judgment is blind: otherwise the room anchors on whoever answers first. The distribution is published without names because the shape of what people asked for makes the resulting bar easier to live with; the identity of whoever needed most is not.

**R-013 Disclosure is consented, ordered by privacy** (Ed, 2026-08-17, §3.5a). The disclosure family is constitutional because a judgment was cast under a promise about who would see it. Ordered most-private-first (anonymous · sealed · public; nobody · each · everybody; never · after), each member states the most exposure they will accept and the document takes the most private — the same rule seen from the other end. That is what makes anonymous the strong default without anything being preselected: it holds unless every member is content with more, so a single person keeps the whole document unnamed. Where the convenor decides instead, that guarantee is not available — a convenor may open a document in which members are named without their having agreed to it; the price of the founding being optional, worth a convenor knowing. Elective signing is a real and costly option: in a small roster an unsigned candidate among signed ones says something about its author — a cost for the roster to weigh, not a reason to withhold it. Disclosure changes what the mechanism measures (a room that can see whose text it is can prefer the person), and in some rooms that is wanted — which is exactly why the answer belongs to the roster.

**R-014 Delegate the decision, not the field** (Ed, 2026-08-18, Q341). The threshold is two decisions of different kinds: what consent is about is *how sure the room must be before a change is permanent* — the bar at the close, one number, the only part a minimum can bind, since an early adoption is challengeable under the ramp's own logic; how the bar gets there is pacing, ordinary by §9.6's test because every adoption is recorded against the bar as it stood. Hence two cards (🌡️ constitutional, 🪜 ordinary) and one quorum card with two eyebrows. A delegated question must be phrased over the thing consent is about and collect exactly the binding scalar; the frame is fixed before the question is served, so a room always answers something well-posed; and a question whose meaning depends on another setting is not served until that settles (the drip's units depend on the window). Quorum's form: shares suit a windowed convention, counts a perpetual document where a drifting membership would silently re-rate every parked race.

**R-015 A blind question does not resolve while an invitation is outstanding, and never on one voice** (Ed, 2026-08-19, Q413; v0.61). The electorate is E, the arrived, which left one road to a rule settled on a single voice: a founder who delegates and answers before anybody opens their email. *A founder obviously does not intend to delegate to themselves, nor would they use this tool if they were writing a document on their own — more members are coming.* A consent rule computed over a single answer is that answer, which is not a delegation to anybody. A founder standing alone has both halves of the choice they already had: invite somebody, or take the setting back. The remedy for an email that will never be opened is §9.6a's free hand on the roster until the start.

**R-016 Membership begins at first arrival; inheritance is owed nothing** (Ed, 2026-08-18; Q257, Q339; amended 2026-08-25, Q842). An invitation is an invitation *to become* a member; until somebody opens the document they are *invited*, with no wallet, no answers owed and no place in any count — which is what keeps one unopened email from blocking judging. A late arrival inherits the constitution rather than reopening it (otherwise a long-lived document never settles). It **used** to be owed an OK on every constitutional setting it had no say in — the unacknowledged-decision rule covering inheritance with no new machinery — and that is what Ed reversed: *a setting that predates you is simply what the document says; a power handed to you is news addressed to you.* An acknowledgement is a piece of news, and a rule that was already there when you walked in is not news to you; nine of them stacked in a new member's rail on their first morning is the whole constitution demanding to be dismissed one card at a time before the document will speak. The ruling is a widening of the reason this entry already carried rather than a contradiction of it. Nothing is owed before the start (Q414): §9.6a lets the founder re-set freely until then, so an earlier OK would acknowledge a rule that may not be the rule; a gate opening is the exception, **being a thing that happened rather than a rule that was set** — and that is exactly the line the amendment generalises. What a member is owed is what *happened while they were here*: a rule set or changed after their arrival, and a power handed to them. A lapsed member is owed one too (2026-08-22): lapse is a stall, not a departure, and the person who was living under the old rule and went quiet is exactly the one a change ought to find — which is the same test read the other way, since they *were* here.

**R-017 The roster is the ground of every founding answer** (Ed, 2026-08-18). *How much of these particular people* — so a roster change while answers are collected is a ground shift: answers stand, their authors are told and may revise until the question settles; blindness is unaffected since nobody sees anybody's answer either way. How they are told is SURFACE.md E20 (Q572: a ↻ acknowledgement task on their own answer card, no mail).

**R-018 A constitutional motion is a unanimous vote, not a re-asked founding** (Ed, 2026-08-18, revising the one-rule reading). The founding collects minima by construction because there is no constitution yet for a vote to govern. An amendment is a different act: a specific value is on the table, and *what will you accept?* is the wrong question about it — re-collecting minima can synthesise a third value nobody proposed, and a failed re-ask could still move the setting through fresh maxima. A unanimous vote escapes the bootstrap on its own terms: unanimity is the one decision rule with no quorum question inside it to govern, because the answer to *by what quorum do you amend the quorum?* is *everyone*. What the two rules share is the property that matters: no member is ever bound by a constitutional value they did not personally accept. Surface consequence: the card that asked the founding question is the card the amendment re-opens.

**R-019 An ordinary motion is the whole race mechanism** (Ed, 2026-08-19, Q390: *we've built a sophisticated system for handling proposals, and motions are just a kind of proposal and work the same way*). The standing value is the incumbent; a rival value joins the same race (propose-C over values); an entry stakes one edit and refunds by §7; withdrawal returns it whole; the floor and quorum gate the carry; deadlock detection applies; a standing-value change mid-race is a ground shift. Values are simpler than prose in one place: equality is decidable, so an identical value is the same proposal — proposing what stands is rejected, proposing a live rival's value is an invitation to co-sign. Moving the close never lowers the threshold (§4.3): it keeps its value and rises more slowly to the same ceiling.

**R-020 A constitutional motion is free, and limited to one per member** (Ed, 2026-08-18, Q327). An ordinary motion is a proposal and priced like one. A constitutional motion is a member asking everyone to decide again about a rule that binds them; charging for that would put a price on consent, the one thing that must stay free. What keeps it from abuse is a limit rather than a price, and the limit is the member, not the setting: one open at a time, returned on withdrawal or settlement, so the cost of raising one is the inability to raise another until the room has answered. The hold and the assembling circle are SURFACE.md's (the `assembly-press`).

**R-021 Live electorate, abstention never blocks, the mover stands at accept** (Ed, 2026-08-18). Abstention is an explicit answer; what blocks is a standing keep. There is no snapshot at open: the set whose answers are needed is the quorum base as it is now, re-checked on every answer and roster event — an arrival means one more answer is needed, a departure can complete a motion. Nobody active at the moment of carriage is bound by an amendment they did not accept or stand aside from, which is §9.6's property restated; no ground-shift machinery is required, because nothing about the question changed, only who must answer it. A standing keep blocks but does not kill; a blocked motion ends by withdrawal. The mover's own answer is accept from the open, as §3.3 counts an author's preference without asking.

**R-022 A carried amendment binds races in flight** (Ed, 2026-08-18, Q328: *historical constitutions don't bind races in flight*). A race is always evaluated against the constitution as it stands; nothing already decided is re-rated — every past adoption keeps its recorded bar-as-it-stood, which is what §9.6's test protects: the record, not the future. Where an amendment leaves a running race no longer making sense, that is a ground shift (§4.4). Engine-side, §4.3's anchor re-seating: a bar never jumps because timings changed.

**R-023 How a member is removed** (Ed, 2026-08-19, Q401(a); v0.60). Three rungs, most protective first, each mapping to real constitutional practice: everyone's consent, the subject's included (one refusal keeps them in, their own counted — you cannot be thrown out of a constitution you consented to be bound by; the pre-v0.60 rule); 🏛️ without the subject (partnerships expel by unanimity of the others — a decision class of its own, the live-electorate settle check with the subject filtered out, their answer refused); an ordinary proposal (clubs and charities expel by a supermajority — a one-candidate race, `remove:<id>`, the admit race's mirror). Whichever rung holds, the subject sees the motion running — nobody is removed in secret. Whether the subject may judge their own ordinary removal race is Q401(b). The pressure valves otherwise are sign-out, the freeze and the advisory-inactivity signal.

**R-024 Two membership settings; every application its own race** (Ed, 2026-08-18/19; Q348, Q397, Q506). Ed's split: *separate "Membership", the register of people who are members, from "Membership Policy", how people become members after the ceremony* — the policy card was titled *Membership Policy* until renamed *Applications* the same day. A submitted application becomes an ordinary motion to grant membership, deliberately a different door from invitation-by-consent: the policy is the constitutional act, each application its ordinary consequence. Q397 fixed the shape: a one-candidate race against the membership as it stands, and every application its own race — two applicants are never raced against each other, because admissions are not rivals (values on one setting share a race precisely because they exclude each other, and admitting one costs the other nothing). A second is a proposed application (Q348a); under *apply* the application text is the applicant's rationale. The rationale lane is always offered and a blank one is a real proposal (v0.57 relaxing v0.56). Q506 moved the register's power pair out of the applications value onto the setting's own powers, leaving the join policy as the whole value. *Superseded in part, 2026-08-26 (plan-queue entry 94, v0.74): one price of admission on 🪪 for every route in; 🤝 is a switch — may strangers apply — and an application is a stranger proposing their own invitation, paying 🪪's price, so the second is gone; the register's pair became the ✉️ door's, and ❌ got its own (§9.7 rule 9). Every application its own race stands.*

**R-025 The applicant's preference is not a mover** (spec pass 1, Q565, Ed 2026-08-22: (a)). §9.7½ said the applicant is *counted toward no quorum and no floor*; §3.3 and §8.2 count every author's derived preference as a mover. At a roster of four, F = 2, the applicant's own preference would be half their own floor and one member's judgment would admit them. The exception is stated in §9.7.3 (X11). Whether the engine-bridge already excludes the applicant's derived preference from the admit race's floor is recorded in QUESTIONS.md as a code check. **Checked 2026-08-22 (Q583): it did not** — the engine's derived-preference loop never looked at suspension, so the applicant was half their own floor at a roster of four. Fixed in engine-core as the general rule E already implies: a suspended author's derived preference is not a mover; their cast judgments still count.

**R-026 AI proposals are ordinary** (Ed, 2026-08-18, Q352). The coherence auditor is not a member — it judges nothing and counts toward no quorum — so switching it on or off re-rates nothing already decided. A member could put the document through an AI themselves; the tool is a convenience for the membership. Machine members left §9.6's roster bullet when they left the roster.

**R-027 Pacing is the founder's, not a founding question** (Ed, 2026-08-19, Q415; v0.61 — reverting his own 2026-08-18 override, made with *we can change this later* attached). Q341 drew the line: the bar at the close is consent, the ramp that reaches it is pacing. The catalogue had defaulted 🪜 to the members and marked it delegable, which meant giving up both powers pre-start would open a blind question no surface had a body for — there is no stated consent rule a room could answer for a shape-and-start pair. Delegating it is a hand-over once the text confirms, so 📯 stays reachable. Its consent order survived in the catalogue for a case that never performs a resolution; dropped in spec pass 1 (Q560).

**R-028 Proposing opens at the start** (spec pass 1, Q570, Ed 2026-08-22: (a)). §9.0b (2026-08-17) had three gates to three holders — reading to nobody, proposing to the convenor and then to *you* (a confirmed text and your own founding answers), judging to the room — so that a member who had answered could draft while the rest were still answering. The build took a different shape from the wallets and the server: ✏️ is granted at 🍾 (*no power arrives without acknowledgment*), and the engine bridge that holds candidates is born at constitute, so `propose-text` is refused before the start. Ed's ruling keeps the build: members answer delegated constitutional questions before the start, and cannot propose until they have their wallets. The three-holders design is recorded here as the one not built; its surviving half — the proposal rate applies prospectively when it lands, nothing clawed back (Q343) — still holds.

**R-029 Seven judge-gates** (spec pass 1, finding 556). §9.0b said *judging needs the whole constitution*; the code gates on exactly the settings a judgment is recorded under or counted towards — threshold, quorum, authorship, signing, judgments, visibility, lapse — which is the reason §9.0b itself gave. Not gated: the ending, removal, applications, rate, machines, title, link. A delegated question on any setting still blocks the start while it collects.

**R-030 No observer role** (Ed, 2026-08-18; Q324 closed in spec pass 1, Q577). Observers are not members: no row, no name, no picture, because nothing about them is known; who may read is settled by 🌍 alone. §9.3's sentence promising an anonymized live metrics feed to observers is struck; the feed itself is parked with Q42 (spectator commentary, backlog) as a spectator-api note.

**R-031 The convenor holds no power over routing** (Q54, closed in spec pass 1, finding 547). §8.5's *The convenor's in-session powers: none* was about feeds — nobody, the convenor included, touches who is served which pair — and had been contradicted wholesale by §9.7 since reservation existed. Reworded to say the narrow thing.

**R-032 The freeze: the clock says so, and proposals still park** (Ed, 2026-08-22, Q573 (b)). No mail on a freeze: a member logging out is intentional on their part. Proposals made after the freeze still go into the record and are saved with the document when it closes, for the next session. Whether the freeze and the close are communicated any further is undesigned — Q463.

**R-033 The kinds are derived, not argued** (Ed, 2026-08-18). The test — *a constitutional decision is one that would make past decisions mean something different* — produces the list, bullet by bullet: disclosure and visibility (a judgment was cast under a promise); quorum and the threshold (every adoption means *this cleared that bar with that many behind it*); the roster (quorum is a fraction of it); whether the document ends (the ramp is the bar). Everything else is ordinary because no past judgment means anything different when the title, the link, the date or the wallet changes. Q329: the route is read off the value at compose time, so ⏰'s card offers `routeOf` and the price flips as you type.

**R-034 Two independent axes at creation** (Ed, 2026-08-16, amended 2026-08-18). The window (an end datetime or perpetual) and the threshold shape (fixed or ramping) are independent; only the perpetual case is constrained, since a ramp needs an endpoint. Without a window the document ends by freeze, so a perpetual document is one whose ending is decided by its roster rather than its calendar. The proposal rate is two settings (the grant; the drip with its cap), not an economy and not tokens; the stake is not a third, being a flat 1 (§13). The drip runs on real minutes everywhere (Q353), so moving the close touches nobody's wallet. A perpetual document has no inter-session reset — Q252.

**R-035 The link is never broken** (Ed, 2026-08-18/19, Q460, Q462b). Nothing exists until the magic link is followed, so the link is the instantiation; the address is reserved on send for the pending stash's TTL, the one oracle on pending documents. Verification comes first because there are no passwords and the address is the only way back in. Text pasted before the save rides the pending creation (*copy the text, open docs.vote, paste it, then do the tasks* is a reasonable order to work in). Every link the document has ever had keeps working because members are invited by a link sent once, often weeks before they open it — a rename that broke old links would make the one ordinary setting with a real cost for being used as intended.

**R-036 Identity is not authorship; Anonymous is a name** (Ed, 2026-08-18/19). A name is how you appear in the room; authorship is whether a name is attached to a proposal, sealed unless every member consented to more. A document can show fourteen named people and not one named candidate, and that is the ordinary case. A blank name renders as Anonymous because an unnamed member is an ordinary member and the surface must not read their silence as an error; at the stranger's door an unnamed founder is *the founder*, which makes this a rule about how a member appears in the room.

**R-037 Lapse** (Ed, 2026-08-18, closing Q345 with the sunset option). Each member states the shortest quiet spell they would accept being lapsed after and the document takes the longest, so nobody is lapsed faster than they accepted. Sign-out applied by clock rather than by declaration; it passes §9.6's test because it re-rates nothing decided; on a perpetual document it is the answer to the member who will neither participate nor leave. Warnings by mail first; the package with the lapse; revival by logging in.

**R-038 Sign-out, holding and abstaining** (Ed, 2026-08-16). Holding keeps you in the quorum base (*I do not consent to you finishing without me*); abstaining leaves it (*I trust you to finish up*). The split makes a walkout visible: quorum-busting requires the explicit, logged act of signing out holding. The base is E minus abstainers, never the active remainder, or every departure would lower the bar. An abstainer is told when the base drops materially below where it stood when they left, so the choice can be revisited rather than inherited.

**R-040 Appendix A is engine tuning** (spec pass 1, Q562, Ed 2026-08-22: (a)). The appendix was titled *Constitution defaults* and since Q511 the constitution has none. What remained split cleanly: numbers the mechanism runs on and nobody is asked (cooldown, hot set, deadlock test, refund, rival gate, boost) and constitution settings whose "defaults" were sim/fixture values (⏩'s `SEED`). The first group stays as the published routing and calibration parameters §8.5 promises; the second lives only in §9.7.1, with the fixture values in sim-harness. The document-level half of Q335's split.

**R-042 Foundership carries a read** (Ed, 2026-08-22, v0.64). Without it a clerk under *members only* was shut out of the document they convened — a rule no product has implemented and the surface should not state, since the convenor holds the founding, the record's distribution and, wherever they reserve, the pen. The server always behaved this way; what was wrong was that the clause stated the opposite, so under *members only* — and only when the founder is a clerk — the clause names them: *Only members and the Founder can see the document.* It said this as a second sentence appended to the members-only one until Ed's QA of 2026-09-02 (Q1143, STYLE §7 item 80) made it one sentence naming both audiences.

**R-043 Out of scope** (Ed, 2026-08-19). Reserving the text — every change needing the founder's assent to merge — is how suggested amendments work in many editing products and against the spirit of this one; parked as Q387. Delegating powers to individual members, transferring the foundership deliberately or on lapse, inheritance — the 👑 is a benevolent dictator for life rather than a literal monarch, and nobody inherits it; sequel material, Q388. The holder is the constitutional fact, consented once by everybody on the way in: nobody joins a crowned convenor's document unknowingly, since the 👑 stands beside their name.

**R-044 Laying a power down is news** (Ed, 2026-08-22, Q571 (b)). A relinquishment changes no rule's value, so Q530's pen machinery (keyed on `previousValue`) did not fire, and the members learned of it only by noticing a clause was shorter. **R-056 A shielded Text parks an adoption, it does not revert one** (Ed, 2026-08-27, answering the feasibility pass on Q440; v0.85). Q440 built the road to a shielded Text and the refusal at the end of it was hollow: `reportAdoptions` opened the 👑 question only *after* engine-core had emitted `adopted` — the version bumped, the document rewritten, every rival rebased onto the new text — so answering *reject* wrote `rejected` onto a record beside a clause that stood anyway. The largest single power in the design was a note in a log. Ed's ruling is that **it is never adopted**: a race that clears bar and floor while the convenor holds 🛡️ on the Text **parks awaiting assent** — races in flight are not rebased, the ramp keeps running, and the candidate leaves every feed. **Accept** adopts it then, through the ordinary `adopted` path, rebasing as usual and on the confidence and the bar it cleared at the moment the room decided rather than at the convenor's convenience. **Refuse** retires it as a failed proposal — refund 0, carrying *Proposal refused by ‹name› 🛡️*, which is what its author reads on their sealed record; a refund would price a refusal as a withdrawal. The rejected alternative is **adopt-then-unwind**, which is what the code did: in an event-sourced engine there is no rollback, only a compensating event, and *un-adopt* cannot be written as one — the versions are a stack, the rivals have been rebased onto a text that would have to be un-published, and every judgment cast since was cast against it. Parking is the same decision taken one beat earlier, where it costs nothing. Its **consequence, which is this build's own reading and not Ed's ruling, is one at a time**: while a candidate is parked the batch performs no text adoption and makes no second park, and a batch parks at most one text leader. That is what lets accept be the ordinary adoption with no new machinery — nothing has changed the text since the park, so the parked patch is still against the current version — and the alternative, letting the text move under a parked candidate, needs a rebase path for parked candidates and a story for one whose rebase fails, buying nothing a convenor's answer does not already unblock. **The invariant is stated in full at `sweepAdoptions`, because it binds every door and not only that one**: no text adoption of any kind while a candidate is parked. `adopt`'s rebase loop takes only `live` candidates, so a parked one is never rebased and cannot be made safe after the fact — a second adoption door added later (a convenor's ✒️ on the Text, say) that does not honour the rule applies hunks against a version they were never written for. The mechanism costs the engine one switch and no knowledge of powers: the host declares `textAssent` through `ConstitutionAmendment`, and it declares *whether assent is owed* rather than the raw power, so a lapsed crown grants by itself and lapse auto-passing falls out of the mechanism instead of needing a second rule. A question pending at the close fails closed (§4.6) and its candidate is recorded *undecided* at refund 0 — a text leader clearing the bar in the final batch included, which is neither adopted nor left waiting for an answer nobody has time to give. What the room is told while an adoption waits is filed open (Q1015): the surface says nothing today, and the build deliberately adds nothing rather than inventing copy.

The objection survives as the **default**: not told is laid down, so the 📄 switch starts at *lay down* and a convenor who never opens the table gets exactly the document they got before — a reserved text is a thing they must ask for, in the one place the asking makes sense, and never a thing they drift into. **Rule 1 is untouched, and is why every other zone starts at *keep***: a power is born the convenor’s, so a switch sitting at *keep* asserts nothing new, where 📄’s position is rule 8 as amended read literally.

Three consequences worth stating. **This is the post-start hand-over, not a pre-start release** (rule 2): the batch rides the `constituted` event and is folded *after* the constituted-at time is stamped, because before the start a release that would leave neither power on a delegable setting **is delegation** and opens a blind founding question — up to ten of them, every one of which then refuses the start (§9.0b, R-045). A card that laid a zone down one key at a time would seal the document it was starting. **The act is the batch** (R-044): one command carrying one list at one time-stamp, so what a press lays down is one news entry and one acknowledgement however many powers it moved; N acts at N stamps would be N cards. And **the answer only ever lowers** — a switch returned to *keep* means *lay nothing further down here*, never a reclaim, because a power promised away on a setting’s own tab comes back only where it went from. The same rule is what makes a zone read **mixed** rather than *kept* once part of it has gone: two controls over one power must not disagree about it. Rejected alongside: per-setting switches on the card, which is the tabs again with a worse view of them; and zones editable from the browser, the zones being Ed’s and a source literal one table wide. **R-043 stands for Q388** — delegating powers to individual members, transferring or inheriting the foundership — and loses only its first clause.

**R-058 The Founder's pen on the Text is a route, and its amendment passes the instant it is submitted** (Ed, 2026-08-27, backlog entry 160; Q1020, Q1021; v0.87). What it replaces: the Text was **the one setting with no route at all**. §9.7.1's row read *none — changed by drafting*, X1 said so in the exceptions, and the code agreed all the way down — `setSetting` refuses `startingText` outright, `confirmStartingText` refuses after the start, and engine-core changed the document in exactly one way, a candidate winning a race at the adoption threshold. So a convenor who kept ✒️ on the Text under R-057's new switches could do nothing whatever with it: a power that named an act the system had no verb for. Rule 5 already said *where the convenor holds ✒️ the route is the pen*, on a setting of either kind; this is that sentence reaching the last setting it had not reached, and X1 survives, narrowed to what it was always really about — the Text has no **motion** route, a pen amendment never being raised, and a motion button on 📄 still being a second door to the same room.

The mechanism is a **direct adoption**: submitted and adopted in one act, no stake, no race, no judgment. It could not honestly reuse `adopted`, which requires a posterior and a threshold and has neither here — there was no room to have been confident. What it must not lose is the **ground shift**: a race in flight on the same footprint is rebased, not killed, exactly as under an ordinary adoption, which is why both doors run one shared loop rather than two that agree today.

Three things follow rather than being decided. **A pen adoption opens no 👑 question**, even where the convenor also holds 🛡️ on the Text: R-056's question exists so a shielded convenor can refuse *the room's* adoption, and asking them to assent to their own act is asking them twice. **A lapsed crown does not act**: lapse grants assent (R-056's road) and performs nothing, which is `doorPen`'s rule and not a new one. And **nothing is decreed while a candidate sits parked awaiting assent** — R-056 rests on the invariant that nothing has changed the text since the park, and a decree is exactly what would change it; the parked candidate is not live, so the shared rebase loop skips it, and teaching the loop to rebase a parked patch would need a story for a rebase that fails and re-opens the question R-056 declined. The refusal is R-056's own one-at-a-time rule reaching the second door rather than a new rule, and it is honest: a convenor holding both powers already owes the room an answer.

**Where the acknowledgement is served — amended by Ed, 2026-08-29** (decision D47, answering Q1021; Q1034; v0.89). The rule above says the amendment is owed an acknowledgement and does not say where, and the question is not incidental: the module's acknowledgement channel is `ok-owed`, whose payload is a list of **setting ids**, so the cheap answer was 📄's own key `startingText` — one owed OK however many amendments it stood for, reading *the Founder changed the text*. **That is what the R-058 build shipped**, and the record is worth keeping plainly: the build put the question to Ed, did not wait on it, and took its own recommendation, naming the richer answer and leaving it unbuilt behind a number. Ed has ruled the richer answer: **a news card beside the amended clause**, in the charter rail against the paragraph that moved, **one card and one OK per amendment**.

What the ruling buys is *which paragraph*, which 📄's key cannot say — and the cost of not saying it is not small on a document of any length: a member told *the text was amended* has the whole charter to search. It costs an owed kind of its own (`amendment-owed` / `amendment-ok`, `oweAmendment` / `ackAmendment`), modelled on entry 162's release batch — this project's one worked example of an owed kind that is not a setting — but **deliberately without its batching**: 162 groups because one press of 🍾 lays down thirty-four powers that belong to no clause, where one ✒️ commit is one amendment at one place and the card's whole job is to name that place. And 📄's owed OK is **removed** with it rather than kept as a fallback, because the moment the card exists the OK is a second home for one fact (STYLE **T36**).

One thing follows rather than being decided, and it is a persistence rule the reversal happened to enforce: the owing moves from the `text-amended` **fold** to `recordTextAmendment`, on the **command path**. `replay` calls `apply` directly while `emit` pushes to the log, so an owing performed in a fold appends events to every session that replays that log — the log growing every time it is read. Entry 162 stated the rule; the first reading of this one broke it invisibly, since `rollingHash()` returns the last *pushed original* entry's own hash and cannot see an appended event. The replay test now asserts the log's **length** as well as its hash.

Where a member **acknowledges** it (Q1021): 📄's own key, through `oweOks`, which collapses a run of amendments into one owed OK reading *the Founder changed the text*. The richer answer — a news card on the amended clause itself, in the charter rail beside the paragraph that moved — is a new owed-kind and a surface of its own, and is filed rather than built. The record names the **office** and never the wording: a document's text is pages long, and the document is its own record of what it says.

**R-059 Every hold on the surface is one second, and gravity is said by what flies** (Ed, 2026-08-29, backlog entry 206, QA on the commit-gesture switch of backlog 184: *the click gesture is good but all "hold" times should now be only 1 second long*). What it replaces is a **graded ladder**: 🪶 and ✒️ one second, the pencil's ✏️ three, the 🏛️ `assembly-press` ten, on the principle SURFACE §7.2 stated outright — *an act that spends something out of a wallet is held, and its gravity is the length of the hold*. The idea was sound and the numbers were not. A hold is a gesture somebody performs with a finger down, and three seconds of it is long enough to feel like a fault in the page rather than a ceremony; ten is long enough that a member who means it waits out a progress meter for an act they had already decided on. The click gesture, adopted a day earlier, made it worse rather than better: under `click` the length is no longer time the member is *spending*, it is time they are *waiting*, with the control inert and nothing to let go of. **The ruling is one number, 1000 ms, for every hold on the surface**, and Ed asked for it to be one constant rather than five agreeing literals — `HOLD_MS` in `design/session.js`, exported as `SESSION.holdMs` beside `SESSION.gesture` and read by `design/session-view.html` for its wallet commits and its assembly, so the five that disagreed by a factor of ten cannot drift again. `spec-check`'s `checkWallets` asserts every numeric *hold ms* cell in both of §7's tables against it, which is stronger than the three per-literal checks it replaces.

**What it costs, stated plainly: the ladder stops saying gravity by length.** That was a real signal and nothing replaces it exactly. Two things carry it instead, and both were already there. **What flies** — a feather, a pen, the cork out of the button into the document title, the pencil the width of the screen, and for 🏛️ the whole room convening into a ring around the control — which is a difference in kind rather than in degree, and reads at a glance where four durations only read in comparison. And **the words on the control**: 🏛️ says *Ask all members* where ✏️ says *Propose*. Two consequences follow rather than being decided. The pencil's **quarter-way floor comes down with the hold**, 864 → 288, because the floor is a quarter of the *distance* and `cubic-bezier(.45, .05, .3, 1)` reaches a quarter of the way at 0.288 of its duration whatever that duration is — 864 of 3000 and 288 of 1000 are the same point on the same arc; the throw's **minimum rate of 1.8× stands**, Ed naming it, and at the new floor a standing-start push runs at exactly that minimum where it used to run at 5.4×. And the assembly's **stagger compresses to fit rather than being dropped** — *the ring is the meter*, so the seats now spread over `HOLD_MS − 80 − 320` (the lead-in, and the seat's own 320 ms transition in `design/setup.css`) so the last one lands as the hold ends; a ring that completed early would stop being the meter, which is the one thing the assembly is for. Untouched deliberately: the commit gesture itself, the flights that are not holds (`REFUND_MS` 640, each grant's inbound 640, `STORM_MS` 900), and `nudgeHome`'s `rewind` 4, `push` 160 and `hang` 90.

**R-060 A park with no convenor auto-passes, and a vacant seat holds no shield** (Ed, 2026-08-29, answering the batch review's deferred finding F7 on the R-056 build; v0.88). R-056 made a shielded Text **park** an adoption rather than revert one, and rested two things on that park being answerable: `sweepAdoptions` performs no text adoption of any kind while a candidate is parked, and the 👑 question is served only to the convenor — `crownTasks` and the server's `awaitingAssent` are both gated on the reader being them. The one thing that answers a park nobody is left to answer was the **crown lapse auto-pass**, and that fires once, on the transition *into* lapse, inside `runClocks`. So a seat vacated **after** the park — the convenor's own member row removed by a carried removal motion, which was then the one door that did it — served the question to somebody who had gone, left no clock that would ever auto-pass it, and blocked every text adoption in that document for the rest of its life. The room's drafting stopped permanently and nothing on any surface said why. Ed's words: *a park with no convenor auto-passes, the way a lapse does.* The rejected alternative was **leave it standing and let the close resolve it** — §4.6 already fails a pending question closed and records its candidate *undecided* — which is technically a resolution and is the wrong one: it prices a convenor's departure as a permanent freeze on the document's text, and the document may have no close at all, a perpetual one having only the freeze line. Auto-passing is the same judgement lapse already makes, that a seat with nobody in it abstains and abstention on an assent grants.

**One half of this is the build's reading and not Ed's ruling, and is marked as such: the shield reads *down* while the seat is vacant.** `textAdoptionNeedsAssent()` already carried `!crownLapsedFlag`, and R-056 chose to declare *whether assent is owed* rather than the raw power to the engine precisely so a sleeping crown falls out of the mechanism instead of needing a second rule; a vacated seat is that fact in a stronger form — nobody asleep, nobody at all — so the same clause covers it. Without it the auto-pass clears the park that is standing and the very next race parks against an empty seat, which is the finding again one adoption later. The predicate is `convenorSeatVacant()`, a claim about **state** — the convenor's own member record present and `removed` — never about which event produced it, so it reads the same after a replay as after the act — and §9.6a's free resignation duly arrived with no second rule and no second predicate. `resign` refused the convenor outright when this was written, pointing at a pre-start 🎩 untick that is no route at all once 🎩 is locked; entry 248 put that spec/code disagreement to Ed, and under the reading this build took the spec was right and the code moved: after the start a member convenor resigns like anybody, `resign` calls `crownSeatVacated` as the carried-removal arm does, and the seat it leaves is this vacancy. Before the start the refusal stands, since a pre-start resignation would mark the record `removed` and manufacture a vacant seat in a document that has not begun. **A convenor who is not a member is not a vacant seat**: unticking 🎩 deletes the record from the membership altogether, and exception X15 says a convenor with no powers and no membership is still a person the room may restore powers to — the clerk convenor is the ordinary founding and their shield stands.

The mechanism reuses what a lapse already emits: `crown-question-auto-passed`, whose fold sets `auto-passed` and treats it as accepted and whose bridge arm already becomes `engine.assent(t, parked, 'accept')`, so the constitution log gains no event kind and the replay case stays bit-identical. It does **not** emit `crown-lapsed` — a vacancy is not a lapse, `crownLapsedFlag` being about a crown that may wake up again where a removed member does not return to the seat. And it takes **text** questions only, which is a deliberate narrowing of the lapse loop rather than a copy of it: applying a carried invitation or removal without assent because the convenor left is a governance consequence nobody has ruled on, and such a question blocks nothing while it stands, where a parked text adoption blocks everything. That asymmetry is filed open as **Q1033**, with the wider version of it beside — `crowned()`, `holdsAnythingReserved()`, `reservedTarget()` and the doors' pairs all still read a vacated seat as held, and this build changes exactly one of them, because exactly one of them blocks the engine.

**R-061 Proposing no longer forfeits the pair, because judgment is blind anyway** (Ed, 2026-08-16; SPEC v0.16, §3.3 and §3.5). The forfeit priced a peek at mid-flight state: drafting against a live race once showed you something the card did not, so the pair you had been asked about was surrendered as the price of looking. §3.5's blind-composition rule removed the thing being paid for — drafting against a race still in the judgment stream now reveals the text and nothing else — so the charge had nothing left to charge for. What the drop buys is the most informative judgment the system can collect: an author who drafts still judges the pair they were asked about, and may well end up preferring somebody else's draft to their own, which is a judgment made against interest. The dependency runs one way and is worth stating: §3.5's constraint is what allows the forfeit to be dropped rather than merely relocated. Weaken the blindness and the forfeit's argument returns.

**R-062 The author's preference is derived, not recorded** (Ed, Q245; SPEC v0.18, §3.3). *While a candidate is live, its author prefers it to the current text* is what a live candidate means — an author who stopped preferring it would withdraw it (§3.3a) — so the preference is computed against the incumbent as it now stands, every time, and never goes stale. Assuming it saves a pointless question; assuming any other would be fabrication. It was built the **recorded** way first, and that was the fault worth remembering: a recorded comparison is stamped with the ground it was cast on, and a race's ground widens whenever a new candidate joins it, locking everything cast on the old one (§4.4). A human recovers, because the pair is re-served and they answer again — nobody re-asks an automatic vote. So the first author into a contested area lost their voice the moment anyone joined them while later authors kept theirs, and the adoption floor became a function of submission order. The fault was modelling a standing fact as a dated one. It counts toward the floor because an author is a voice on the roster, and excluding them would systematically under-count the preferences of the people who cared enough to write.

**And therefore the pair is never served** (Ed, 2026-08-29, backlog 253, SPEC v0.94). *I seem to be able to vote on my own text amendment proposals. I think that I should automatically be counted as preferring proposals that I have made vs the status quo.* The counting half was already this ruling; what was missing was the asking half, and the two are the same sentence read forwards and backwards. If the preference is derived rather than recorded, the engine holds the answer before the card is drawn — so serving the author their own candidate against the incumbent asks a question the engine wrote itself, and whatever they press either agrees with it or is overridden by nothing (an explicit judgment *does* still win, which is the point: it is a second answer to a settled question, offered for no reason). The limit is exactly where the derivation stops. *By proposing you only know that it's preferred to the status quo* — nothing is implied about which of two challengers is better, so a **rival** pair of the author's is a real question and is still asked. The exclusion is the router's (`bestPairFor`'s shared scan and `explorationCard`, the two doors that serve against an incumbent) and never `judge`'s: the ruling is about what is put in front of somebody, not about what the engine will accept from a host that asks directly.

**R-063 Preference is not measurement** (SPEC v0.18, §3.3, beside R-062). A derived preference is a real preference — it feeds the ranking and counts as a voice — but no sampling effort was spent on it and its answer was known in advance. So the line runs between two questions the mechanism asks separately: everything asking *what does the room prefer, and how many voices are in* counts it, and everything asking *have we measured this enough* does not — the deadlock test (§8.3), the rival-pair gate, the per-race comparison counts in the record, and the performance a refund pays on (§7). The refund is the case that forces it: one favourable comparison already reaches the refund cap, so if an author's own preference were performance, submitting and retiring would pay back more than the stake with nobody else involved.

**E = 1 is the one case measurement cannot be asked of anybody else** (Ed, 2026-08-29, backlog 253, SPEC v0.94). *Have we measured this enough* is a question about a room, and in a document of one there is no room to ask it of: the only person who could answer is the author, and since the pair is no longer served (R-062) their answer can never arrive. The gate would then mean *never*, which is not a stricter reading of the rule but the abolition of the document. So at E = 1 the derived preference is both the floor and the room, and the proposal adopts on submission — under the bar and the cooldown like any batch, and still under the ceiling a room of one can reach (Q840: 0.798, so a bar of 80 refuses it). **Q835's exception is overturned by this.** That ruling met the same problem — a sole member with no card — by serving them the self-judgment so their explicit answer could clear the gate; `design/DECISIONS.md` records *excluding the author from `feed` and letting the floor carry the adoption* as the reading it rejected, for fear of a document that adopts on nobody's judgment. Ed's ruling is that at E = 1 nobody's judgment is the only judgment there is, and that asking somebody a question whose answer you already hold is the worse of the two. Nothing above E = 1 moves: two rival authors still meet a floor of 2 on derived preferences alone and still wait for the room.

**R-064 Withdrawal is retirement, and a ground shift** (Ed, 2026-08-16; SPEC v0.16, §3.3a). Judgments already cast stay in the log and in the ranking because other candidates' estimates rest partly on comparisons against the withdrawn one, and removing a node degrades everything it touched. The stake refunds in full because charging for withdrawal would price exactly the behaviour worth encouraging: an author who must pay to tidy up will instead leave a candidate they no longer believe in standing in the race, consuming judgments that could have gone elsewhere. The ground shift is what makes withdrawal **safe to offer**. A candidate splitting a camp could otherwise be pulled at a chosen moment to change which of the survivors wins; re-serving on the new ground means the room decides that question again rather than having it decided by the timing of a withdrawal.

**R-065 Why disclosure is constitutional, and what anonymity rests on** (Ed, 2026-08-17; SPEC v0.19, §3.5a). The section was revised the day it was written: it had said the disclosure family was never the convenor's and the founding was therefore compulsory. Ed's ruling is that the convenor chooses which, and may delegate the numbers and the disclosure independently — a convenor may fix the quorum and still hand the room its own privacy, or the reverse. It is constitutional rather than cosmetic because **disclosure changes what the mechanism measures**: a room that can see whose text it is can prefer the person, which is §3.3's *preference is not measurement* problem again in the form of deference. And in some rooms that is fine or even wanted — a standing committee that knows each other, a body whose members are accountable for their positions — which is exactly why the answer belongs to the roster rather than to this spec. The price of making the founding optional is stated where it falls: where the **roster** decides, anonymity is structural, sitting at the top of the privacy lattice so that it holds unless every member is content with more and a single person keeps the whole document unnamed; where the **convenor** decides, that guarantee is not available, anonymity is a default like any other, and a convenor may open a document in which members are named without their having agreed to it. Worth a convenor knowing they are paying it.

**R-066 Elective signing is two rungs, and it is a costly option** (v0.70, beside R-047; SPEC §3.5a). Signing was its own setting until v0.70, with rungs *nobody · each · everybody*. *everybody* was the same fact as authorship **public**, said a second time on a second card, which made *nobody named* and *everybody signs* both settable at once; *nobody* and *each* are what the two pairs of authorship rungs now say. The ordering is the argument: a document that names nobody unless they volunteer is more private than one that names everybody at the close, and one that names everybody at the close but lets you volunteer sooner is more private than one that names you as you propose. The cost is real and is stated rather than designed away: in a small roster an unsigned candidate among signed ones says something about its author, so a document that allows the choice is not neutral between the two. Ed's ruling (2026-08-17) is that this is a cost for the roster to weigh, not a reason to withhold the option.

**R-067 *End* means the end of everything, not the close of each decision** (Ed, 2026-08-28; v0.83, SPEC §3.5a). The judgments ladder's second rung read *revealed after the decision they contributed to*, which read as a per-decision reveal and was never that: the rung means the end of the document, and the surface label says so. A genuinely per-decision rung — names shown as each decision closes — is a change to what the sealed record shows and when, and is filed rather than built. Live disclosure is not on the ladder at all: §8.3's no-standings rule is not a privacy preference but the thing that keeps judgment blind while it is still being collected.

**R-068 The hot set is three, and v / c_p does the division of labour** (calibration sweep 2026-08-13; SPEC v0.7 and v0.16, §8.1 and §8.3). The hot set was ~6 and is ~3: depth of evidence per race beats breadth at small-roster scale, the best welfare and the lowest variance of any setting tested. The routing formula needs no further rules to allocate work, and the consequence is the reason it was chosen: abundant cheap judgment sees everything, including the exploratory tail; scarce expensive judgment only ever reaches the top of its list, so its whole budget lands at decision margins. Frequent participants discover the edge cases; occasional participants cast them. At population scale the same formula prices the participation long tail instead of fighting it. Also recorded here rather than in the spec: at small rosters an author's own derived preference (§3.3) is a large fraction of a floor that may be three or four — worth watching.

**R-069 A salience diagonal is served, not offered, and only when prioritising is the work** (Ed, 2026-08-17; SPEC v0.20 and v0.21, §8.3a, replacing the flat ~1-in-10 rate). Prioritisation is only worth anybody's attention when there is genuinely too much to do; below that, asking which of two questions matters more is asking which one is the hot one, and the answer is already on the surface. Hence the **volume** gate: below E live questions the salience model simply goes uninformed, which costs nothing it was going to be used for — salience sets routing weight, bounty order and backlog order, and none of those has work to do in a document with fewer open questions than members. Hence the **audience** gate: everyone else's attention is better spent judging, which is the act that resolves questions rather than ordering them, and this is not a heuristic about who is keen — a diagonal costs a member with an empty queue nothing and costs a member with a full one a judgment. **Served, not offered**, because asking first (*would you like to help prioritise?*) needs somewhere to put the question and the answer is not in doubt. **And it terminates**, because an empty queue must not become an endless stream of prioritisations. **No completion state**, because salience is a continuous ranking with no threshold to clear and no quorum to ratify — which is precisely what allows it to be advisory — so a progress bar on a diagonal claims a finish line that does not exist.

**R-070 A second gate reopens the diagonal stream, and its bias is known** (Ed, 2026-08-17, answering the timing objection to R-069; SPEC v0.21, §8.3a). The audience gate starves salience of exactly the data it needs *while a session is busy*, because that is when nobody has an empty queue. So above 2E live questions the room is saturated enough that ordering the work is itself the valuable act, and diagonals return to the judgment stream at a low rate for everybody — the old ~1 in 10. The known limitation, recorded rather than corrected: below 2E salience is measured on a biased sample, the members who reach the end of their queue, who are systematically the most active. The ranking this feeds never touches adoption (§4.2), which is what makes the bias tolerable, and the alternative — spending scarce judgment on ordering instead of deciding — is worse.

**R-071 Rival pairs ask a conditional question, and it cannot be recovered elsewhere** (Ed, 2026-08-14, resolving Q48; SPEC v0.9, §8.3). *If this text changes, which change is better?* Its signal is precious precisely because it cannot be recovered from incumbent comparisons. Two serving rules follow. Cards never offer "keep the current text" on a rival pair, because a pro-incumbent judge expresses that on incumbent-involving pairs, which the router owes them. And rival pairs are served sparingly until the race shows evidence that at least one challenger plausibly displaces the incumbent: there is little decision value in finely ranking challengers that are all losing to the status quo, their order mattering only to the backlog.

**R-072 Deadlock disclosure is a rule about evidence before it is a rule about clarity** (Ed, 2026-08-17; SPEC v0.23, §8.3b). Deadlock detection is a property of the race; disclosure of it is per-participant, and withheld until the race has nothing left to ask that participant. Judgments from members who have not yet judged the race are exactly the ones most likely to separate its candidates, so **a deadlock disclosed before the room has finished judging is disclosed early** — the old behaviour told a member the race was stuck before they had contributed the one thing they could still contribute. It also keeps the disclosure ladder consistent with §3.5: what a participant is shown is a function of what has been asked of them and what they have given, never of what the machine knows. There is no public board that could disagree with the rule, the bounty board having been retired the same day (Ed, 2026-08-17: *I don't think we need the bounty board anymore; the sidebar-queue does its job*); its score survives and does the ranking it always did, in each participant's own queue.

**R-073 The quorum and the formula floor are one term, and the room's number can only raise it** (Ed, 2026-08-18, closing Q338; SPEC v0.48, §4.2 and §8.2). Before the review of 2026-08-18 the spec carried the room's quorum and the statistical floor under different names in two places, and the engine implemented only the formula — so a room that had chosen its own number was adopting at a floor it had never agreed to. F = max(Q, min(⌈E/3⌉, F_max)) is the two folded into one term: the formula half is a statistical minimum the room's number can raise but never lower.

**R-074 The cooldown paces the room; the ramp stabilises the document** (Ed, 2026-08-19, closing Q399, and 2026-08-27, closing Q946; SPEC v0.8 and v0.81, §4.2, beside R-052). Ed's words on the kind of thing it is: *effectively a convenience rather than a constitutional decision, part of the UI*. Batching is what keeps it cheap — since everything cleared lands at each tick, no decision waits longer than one cooldown however busy the document is, where the serialized form (one adoption per cooldown, globally) queued a busy document's sixth decision for half an hour, which is why it went. It must stay short (≤5 min): the rising threshold is what makes the session stabilise, the cooldown only paces how changes land for the people in the room, and rationing adoptions by time starves the chains of sequenced adoptions an interconnected document needs (calibration sweep 2026-08-13 — 15–30 min cooldowns cut welfare and halved resolution; re-verify under batching when the sweep is next run). The two back each other up against hasty adoption, each looking redundant while the other is intact, which is why neither may be weakened without the other being checked (sweep: no cooldown plus a softened ramp over-adopts and churns). A cleared race is not surfaced as waiting because there is no "cleared and cooling" state to show — the batch evaluates fresh at its own moment, a leader that slips back before the tick simply not adopting — and deriving one would be a standings reveal (§3.5), telling the room what has won before it lands and exactly how long remains to counter-judge it. On the operator home, R-052 carries the alternatives; when a second kind of room exists (a six-month charter beside a twenty-minute meeting) it becomes a birth-time engine parameter beside the grant and the drip, and nothing about the operator value has to be undone to get there.

**R-075 The ramp tracks irreversibility, which is why it starts at the start** (Ed, 2026-08-18, Q342; SPEC v0.7 and v0.49, §4.3).

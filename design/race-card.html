    The comparison problem

    Two rival rewrites of the same paragraph can differ from the base a little or entirely, and those need different visual treatments. Rather than pick one, the card has a view toggle with three modes, and the engine picks the default per card:

    • Fair copy — clean final text, no markup. What the charter would actually say. This is the mode judgment should usually happen in: you're choosing an outcome, not admiring an edit. Always one keystroke away.
    • Marked up — legislative amendment style: each option's word-level diff against the current text (struck deletions, green insertions). Wins when patches are small — see the Keys card, where the whole question is one added proviso. Degenerates when a candidate rewrites the paragraph wholesale (the Money card becomes a red-green porridge), which is itself honest: the markup tells you it's a rewrite, not an amendment.
    • Differences — the workhorse for rival rewrites: everything the two options agree on is dimmed, and only the spans where they disagree with each other are highlighted, in brass. Green/red always means "versus the current text"; brass always means "versus each other" — two separate semantics, two separate colors, never mixed.

    Default heuristic: small patches open in Marked up; wholesale rival rewrites open in Differences; challenger-vs-incumbent cards open in Marked up (the diff against base is the difference). The toggle remembers nothing — every card opens in its own best mode.

    A fourth pattern, parked for P3

    For long documents and multi-hunk patches, prose comparison stops scaling and a structured change ledger earns its place — each option summarised as what it adds, drops, and keeps:

    Option A, in short
    • addsspending only against an approved budget or house decision
    • addsreporting of income and spending at every meeting
    • drops"reimburses what seems fair" — the discretion standard
    • keepsthe Purse-holder as sole payer of bills

    The bullets have to be generated faithfully from the patch, which is an LLM job — this is a P3 feature by construction, sketched now so the card layout reserves room for it.

    What is deliberately absent

    • No authors. Sealed until close. The rationale is labelled unsigned to make the absence legible rather than an oversight — Mo's spelling is left exactly as submitted, and judging it is the judge's problem.
    • No standings, counts, or direction. Nothing on the card hints how others voted or which option is leading. A/B order is randomised per serve.
    • No skip-shame. "Pass this card" is a quiet text link, not a button of equal weight — passing is allowed, not celebrated. Skipped cards recirculate personally with decay (§8.3).
    • The bar is shown, the race's evidence is not. The adoption bar (0.74 ▲) is public — it's a function of the clock, identical for everyone, and it communicates session tempo: "things adopted now are well-tested". Per-race win-probability stays hidden.

    The diagonal card

    Salience cards (card 4) reuse the same stage but compare two contested paragraphs instead of two candidates, and the buttons name the sections rather than A/B. The judgment feeds the salience model, not any race — the prompt says so plainly.

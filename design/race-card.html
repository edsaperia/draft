    Design notes — the race-card, third pass

    Desktop-first · interim white/blue styling (visual design deferred, Ed) · card content is real output from run clubhouse-1

    Two documents that join, not one that splits (Ed, decision 2)

    The mental model is inverted from the last pass: you are reading two parallel versions of the charter, and wherever they are identical the two lanes join into one full-width column. Where they differ, the page splits into two cards side by side — the split itself is the diff display. Joined text is quiet and slightly dimmed; the split is where the eye lands. The document above and below the split is real, scrollable context, and the page opens scrolled to the split.

    Controls live on the cards (Ed, decision 3)

    No detached judgment bar. Each parallel card carries its own big Prefer this button, centered — choosing a card is a gesture at the card. The third choices stack in the middle gutter, between the things they refuse to choose between: Indifferent, Skip, and Propose something else. The middle column is deliberately narrower and quieter than the cards — the two main verdicts are big targets at equal visual weight; the middle is legitimate but not celebrated.

    The comparison modes, inside the split

    • Fair copy — clean final text: what the charter would say. Default for wholesale rewrites, since the split already locates the difference.
    • Marked up — amendment style against the current text (struck deletions, green insertions). Default for small patches — see the Keys card.
    • Differences — within the split cards, dims what both options agree on and highlights in blue only where they disagree with each other.

    One strict color rule: green/red always means "versus the current text"; the blue highlight always means "versus each other." When both candidates replace a paragraph, the current text sits above the split in the joined flow, marked "currently — both proposals replace this."

    Discontinuous patches

    Patches are not continuous — one proposal can touch several places (card 4: renaming the Purse-holder everywhere it appears). The lanes split at every site of the union footprint, badged "change N of M — one proposal, judged together"; the verdict buttons appear at the last site and speak for the whole patch. One judgment, many sites — if judges keep wanting to split a patch, that's evidence it should have been two patches, which is surgery's job (P3), not the card's. When two rivals race and only one touches a site, the silent rival's card reads "leaves this unchanged" — absence rendered, not omitted. Beyond two or three sites the change ledger (adds / drops / keeps, a P3 feature) becomes the primary representation, with the sites as its receipts.

    What is deliberately absent

    • No authors. Sealed until close; rationales are labelled unsigned. Mo's spelling ships exactly as submitted.
    • No standings, counts, or direction. A/B order randomised per serve.
    • The bar is public, race evidence is not. The adoption bar in the chrome is a function of the clock, identical for everyone.

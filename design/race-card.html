    Design notes — the race-card, fourth pass

    Desktop-first · plain Bootstrap-style theme (visual design deferred) · card content is real output from run clubhouse-1

    Two lanes, top to bottom

    The page is structurally two parallel versions of the charter, Version A down the left, Version B down the right, headed as such. Where the versions agree, a single card spans both lanes (grey, quiet — agreement is background). Where they differ, the card splits into one card per lane (white, blue-bordered — disagreement is figure). Reading down the page is reading either version in full; the alternation of spanning and split cards is itself the diff map.

    The floating bar holds rationales and verdicts together

    The sticky bottom bar is the judgment surface: Option A's unsigned rationale and its big Prefer A button on the left, B's on the right, and the middle stack between them — Genuinely can't split them · Skip · Propose something else. Argument and verdict travel together, always in view while the document scrolls behind. Long rationales scroll within their box rather than growing the bar.

    Comparison modes, inside the split cards

    • Fair copy — clean final text. Default for wholesale rewrites, since the split already locates the difference.
    • Marked up — amendment style against the current text (struck deletions, green insertions). Default for small patches (Keys card).
    • Differences — dims what both versions agree on inside the split; highlights in blue only where they disagree with each other.

    Color rule unchanged: green/red = versus the current text; blue highlight = versus each other. When both candidates replace a paragraph, the outgoing text appears as a dashed "currently" card spanning both lanes, directly above the split.

    Non-contiguous patches (card 4)

    One proposal, two sites: the lanes split at the Money paragraph and the Offices paragraph, with spanning agreed-cards between them — the join-split-join-split rhythm shows the patch's footprint at a glance. Badges count the sites ("change 1 of 2"); the floating bar carries one verdict for the whole patch ("Adopt the patch — both changes"). One judgment, many sites: if judges chronically want to split a patch, it should have been two patches — surgery's job (P3), not the card's. Where rivals race and only one touches a site, the silent rival's lane card reads "leaves this unchanged."

    What is deliberately absent

    • No authors. Sealed until close; rationales labelled unsigned. Mo's spelling ships exactly as submitted.
    • No standings, counts, or direction. A/B order randomised per serve.
    • The bar is public, race evidence is not. The adoption bar in the navbar is a function of the clock, identical for everyone.

    Diagonal (salience) cards

    Card 5: the two contested paragraphs sit one per lane with their section names, and the floating bar asks which matters more ("Equally important" in the middle). Weighing attention, not text. Extra visual distinction is parked as QUESTIONS #46.

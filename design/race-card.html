    The document is the card (decision 47)

    A card is no longer an excerpt floating in space: you get the entire charter, scrollable, and at the contested paragraph the page forks into the rival candidates side by side, then the document resumes beneath them. Context is never a courtesy strip — it's the actual document above and below the fork. The judgment bar travels with you (sticky), so reading far from the fork never strands you from the verdict. The page opens scrolled to the fork; the scroll position is yours from there.

    Discontinuous patches (Ed's note)

    Patches are not continuous — one proposal can touch several places at once (card 4: renaming the Purse-holder everywhere it appears). The document view handles this as fork sites: the patch's union footprint renders as multiple linked cutouts, each badged "change N of M — one proposal, judged together", sharing one rationale and one verdict. Rules that keep it from getting messy:

    • One judgment, many sites. The verdict buttons name the whole patch ("Adopt the patch — both changes"); there is deliberately no per-site vote. If judges keep wanting to split a patch, that's a signal the patch should have been two patches — surgery's job (P3), not the card's.
    • Sites where a rival is silent say so. When two rivals race and only one touches a site, the other's panel reads "leaves this unchanged" — absence rendered, not omitted.
    • Beyond two or three sites, prose comparison stops scaling and the change ledger (below) becomes the primary representation, with the sites as its receipts. Site count also wants surfacing up in the eyebrow, and a site-to-site jump control once M > 2.

    The comparison problem, inside the fork

    • Fair copy — clean final text, no markup: what the charter would say. Judgment's home mode.
    • Marked up — amendment style against the current text (struck deletions, green insertions). Wins for small patches — the Keys card is the demo. Degenerates honestly on wholesale rewrites.
    • Differences — for rival rewrites: what both options agree on is dimmed; only where they disagree with each other lights up in brass.

    One strict color rule: green/red always means "versus the current text"; brass always means "versus each other." Each card opens in the mode its patch shape calls for (small patch → Marked up; rival rewrites → Differences); Fair copy is always one click away.

    What is deliberately absent

    • No authors. Sealed until close; rationales are labelled unsigned so the absence reads as designed. Mo's spelling ships exactly as submitted.
    • No standings, counts, or direction. Nothing hints how others voted or who leads. A/B order randomised per serve.
    • No skip-shame. Passing is a quiet link; skipped cards recirculate personally, with decay.
    • The bar is public, race evidence is not. The adoption bar in the chrome is a function of the clock, identical for all; per-race win-probability stays hidden.

## V1 product decisions

- Target context: constitutional conventions for Newspeak House cohorts. Rosters typically 5–10, conventions 15–20; design must not preclude 100+/1000+ instances, but v1 tunes to small rosters (data-efficiency over throughput).
- Hosted multi-tenant web service; magic-link auth against roster emails. Invitations send from **docs.vote** via **Resend** (Ed, 2026-08-18) — the sending domain was the real hosting decision, and the host follows from it.
- Documents are Markdown, rendered as rich text; usually a few pages, long-document behavior stays in scope for sim experiments.
- TypeScript end-to-end; engine-core is a pure, dependency-free library shared by sim-harness and product server.
- Sim personas are LLM-powered on a cheap model (Haiku-class) and speak the same participant API as human clients — no sim backdoor; mixed human/bot sessions are a goal.

- UI north star (Ed, 2026-08-14): **suggestion-mode with escalation** — the default surface reads like familiar Google-Docs-style inline suggestions with approval; the race view (overlay model, pairwise cards) is the escalation state that appears only where suggestions collide or stakes demand ceremony. Most of a session should feel like approving typo fixes; the machinery earns its visibility.

## The two kinds of decision

**This is a constitution editor, and the question is which decisions are ordinary and which are constitutional** (Ed, 2026-08-18). The test is his: **a constitutional decision is one that would make past decisions mean something different.** Disclosure, the chamber, quorum, the bar, the roster and whether the document ends at all are constitutional; the starting text, the title, the link, the closing date, the grant, the drip, the stake and AI proposals (Q352, Ed 2026-08-18: the auditor is not a member, so switching it re-rates nothing — *the tool is just a convenience for the membership*) are ordinary; a member's name and picture are neither. The line can fall **inside** one setting: the window is one question whose answers include *never*, so moving the closing date is ordinary and removing the ending is constitutional — the route belongs to what a motion changes rather than to what card it sits on (Q329). A `motion` is the act of proposing a change to any of them, and the route it takes is a fact about the *setting*: an ordinary motion is an ordinary race, judged at the bar; a constitutional one is **a unanimous vote on a proposed amendment** (Ed, 2026-08-18, superseding the earlier asked-again design): one refusal keeps what stands, and until everyone has answered, what stands stands. The founding itself still collects minima by construction (§9.0a) — unanimity is the one decision rule with no quorum question inside it to govern, which is what keeps the bootstrap shut at both ends. SPEC §9.6–9.7, v0.48.

**Three consequences worth keeping in mind.** *The test is time-indexed by its own wording* (Ed, 2026-08-18, §9.6a): before judging opens there are no past decisions for a change to re-rate, so **before the start nothing is amended — only set**. The convenor re-sets their settings and re-shapes the roster freely until the moment judging opens; a motion is the act of changing a *settled* document’s rules. With it: **membership begins at first arrival** — an invitation is an invitation *to become* a member, invitees are listed as *invited* and count toward nothing (which is what keeps one unopened email from blocking judging), and a roster change while ceremony answers are being collected is a **ground shift**: answers stand, their authors are notified and may revise. And **delegate the decision, not the field** (Ed, Q341: *rethink this given what is being decided*): a delegated question collects exactly the binding scalar — the bar **at the close**, the quorum **number** in the convenor’s chosen form (count or share, Ed’s 338) — while the machinery it rides on (the ramp’s shape and start, quorum’s form) is pacing or wording, stays with the convenor, and is **ordinary by the test**: every adoption is recorded against the bar as it stood, so re-shaping the ramp re-rates nothing. Which split ✒️ into two cards — *How sure must the room be?* (constitutional, the close) and 📈 *How does the bar get there?* (ordinary, pacing) — and made quorum one card with two eyebrows, *Asked as* (the convenor’s) and *The number* (the room’s). Quorum also stopped being fiction: F = max(Q, min(⌈E/3⌉, F_max)) in §4.2, the room’s number riding on the statistical minimum.

**Two consequences worth keeping in mind.** *Constitutional settings default to the roster and ordinary ones to the convenor*, because the room can take an ordinary one back cheaply and cannot take a constitutional one back at all. And *the opening question is the amendment question*, so amending a constitution needs no new object anywhere on the surface.

**The tab you click does not move**, and it took a QA to make it true (Ed, 2026-08-18: *why do my constitutional clause-tabs-piles jump around when I click on them?*). It was claimed here and false in the code, by 33px down and 14px right: a closed pile inherited `.chipcol`'s 14px gutter margin, which is the right distance from a **paragraph** and the wrong one from a **card edge**; and the open card's strip hangs off the `.headclause`, which starts below the card's own label, so the first tab arrived a label's height lower than the pile front it replaced. The margin is 0 for a closed pile — its right edge is exactly the card's left edge — and the vertical half is corrected in `fitBand` by **measurement** rather than by a constant, because the label wraps on a narrow card and the distance is not a number anybody can know in advance. Measured after: 0px in both axes.

**Two more rules the piles carry.** *A constitutional setting a member had no say in is a decision they are owed* (Ed, 2026-08-18): it sits in their rail until they open it and press **OK**, and then it leaves the rail and lives in the pile — the live rail's unacknowledged-decision rule arriving from the other end, because a rule you never chose and are bound by is exactly the thing a margin must not let scroll past. Reading is not enough, because reading is what you do to everything. The **ordinary** settings the convenor made never enter a member's rail at all: nothing is being asked, and anybody may motion one at the bar whenever they like. And *the pile headings are the people* — the convenor's name and picture under **Convenor**, the membership's under **Membership**, arriving as they do, with a placeholder before anybody has one. Which turns the band at the head of the document from a list of settings into the room itself.

## Names (Ed, 2026-08-18)

**founder** — whoever creates and administers a document (Ed, 2026-08-18: *it's time to change "convenor" to "founder". It's much more natural* — reversing the same morning's never-founder rule; never "admin" survives). "Convenor" persists in SPEC and engine code as the older term, like roster for membership. **member** — anybody on the roster; the word "roster" survives in code, but the surface says **membership** (Ed, 2026-08-18). A convenor who is not a member is a **clerk**, and a convenor who is one appears in the membership like anybody else. **Observers are not members** (Ed, 2026-08-18: *we don't collect any information about them, they just have the public URL*) — there is no observer role, no row, no name and no picture, because nothing about them is known. Who may read a document is settled entirely by the 🌍 **Visibility** setting (the engine's `chamber`; titled *Who Else Can See It?* until Ed renamed it, 2026-08-18 — a noun term of art like Quorum, glyph 🌍), and SPEC §9.3's observer role does not survive that (Q324). **Three words left the surface on 2026-08-18.** *The bar* became the **approval threshold** — a bar is a metaphor that has to be learnt, it reads as a height, and the copy then had to keep explaining that clearing it is not winning a vote; the literal name says what the number is for, and it is still a **confidence** rather than a vote share (§5), which the copy has to keep saying either way. *Economy* and *tokens* became the **proposal rate** — both described the machinery from outside, where the question a member is actually being asked is how often they may propose. And **participant** is the engine's word for whatever speaks the `participant-api` and should never appear in anything a member reads. **"Ordinary" left the surface too** (Ed, 2026-08-18): ✏️ already means a proposal, so the label said the machinery twice — the kind pair is glyphic on cards, **✏️** *anybody may propose changing it* and **🏛️** *a constitutional change, the ask-everyone route* (Ed, choosing it over 🤝 and 📢: *about time we used 🏛️ for something!*). On a card head the pair reads "🏛️ Constitutional · the members decide" / "✏️ Open to proposals · reserved by the convenor"; the word *constitutional* survives on the surface, "ordinary" survives only as engine vocabulary in SPEC and code. And **"ceremony" has left the surface entirely** (Ed, 2026-08-18: *something more neutral like "everyone is ready to begin"*) — it named a real stage and dressed it in robes, which is the wrong signal for a screen whose whole argument is that this is ordinary. What is left is what happens: everyone answers, and when everyone is ready the document begins.

## Glossary — named parts

Use these names in all discussion, commits, and code. Literal and stable beats elegant.

**Engine (mechanism, no UI):**
- `engine-core` — session state machine: candidates, races, adoptions, the adoption-threshold ramp, tokens/refunds, adoption floors, certification. **Caught up through 367(b)** (2026-08-19): quorum rides the floor (§4.2), the drip is real minutes (`tokenDripMinutes`, Q353), amendments bind races in flight (`amend`/`standing-set`, §4.3 anchor re-seating — a bar never jumps), suspension is lapse engine-side (§9.5a), **setting races** carry ordinary motions (Q390 — a candidate is a patch *or* a typed value; adoption of a value is a verdict, applied only by the host's `setStanding`, which is also the ground shift), and the hash is pure TS — the engine is node-free and browser-loadable. **Adoptions batch on the cooldown metronome** (Ed, 2026-08-19, closing Q399; SPEC §4.2 v0.58): when the cooldown from the last batch elapses, every race clearing bar+floor adopts at once — the document changes at most once per cooldown, by as much as the room decided; one decision per race per batch (the ready set snapshots before anything lands), oldest first, each rebasing the next; a race adopts only once somebody has actually judged in it (two rival authors meet a floor of 2 on derived self-preferences alone — the old one-race trigger enforced this structurally, the sweep says it explicitly). `Session.tick(t)` is the host clock; the bridge's `tick(t)` rides it (judge's act-harvest-sync pattern) and the server's minute timer drives the bridge, so a due batch lands even in a quiet room. The cooldown stays **engine tuning, never constitutional** (Ed: a convenience, part of the UI), and a cleared race is never surfaced as waiting — no ⏳, since “cleared and cooling” does not exist as a state and deriving it would be a standings reveal.
- `adoption-threshold` — the confidence bar a challenger's win-probability must clear; ramps over the session window (wall clock). Formerly "θ"; always use this name.
- `patch-engine` — text machinery: diffs, footprints, three-way merge, rebase, surgery.
- `overlap-gates` — the three-gate classifier for colliding patches: textual composition → semantic composition → rivalry.
- `ranking-model` — Bradley–Terry preference model (per race) + global salience model, with active pair sampling and deadlock detection.
- `router` — v/c_p feed ordering, hot set, exploration/salience streams, floors-aware serving, notification digests.
- `event-log` — append-only hash-chained log, seeded RNG, participant receipts.
- `dedup-gate` — submission-time duplicate check (embeddings, edit distance, LLM equivalence) plus behavioral dedup probes.
- `race-labeler` — advisory naming and typing of disputes: a name ("treasurer oversight") and a type (copy-edit · substantive · structural) per race, from the oracle's `describeRace`, with a deterministic nearest-heading + excerpt fallback. Outside the state machine — labels never enter the event log or gate anything; type is stored but not yet wired to routing (Q49).
- `document-modes` — two **independent** settings, both fixed at creation (SPEC §9.0). **Window**: an end datetime, or none (perpetual) — **one question with one answer**, which is why the surface asks it on one card (⏰ *When does it end?*) with *never* as an option rather than on two (Ed, 2026-08-18). **Threshold shape**: fixed, or ramping. A windowed document may hold its bar fixed; only a perpetual one is *constrained*, since a ramp needs an endpoint to ramp toward. A window gives you the closing ceremony and a drip paced against it; without one the drip runs against real time and the document ends by `freeze` instead.
- `founding-ceremony` — the stage before any drafting where the roster sets quorum and threshold: each member states, blind, the *lowest they will accept*, and the document takes the **maximum** of each. A consent rule rather than a vote, which is what lets it escape the constitutional bootstrap — there is no vote to govern, because the result satisfies every stated minimum by construction. Distribution published without names (SPEC §9.0a).
- `sign-out` — a member declaring they are done, choosing **holding** (they stay in the quorum base, so enough of them freezes the document — *I do not consent to you finishing without me*) or **abstaining** (they leave the base — *I trust you to finish up*). Plain silence is never sign-out; judgments already cast keep counting afterwards; the base is the whole roster minus abstainers, never the active remainder (SPEC §9.5).
- `freeze` — what happens when active plus holding falls below quorum: races park, participants are notified, the record is cut. A stall with an alarm, not a death — it thaws if enough return, and it is the perpetual mode's equivalent of a close.
- `coherence-auditor` — machine drafter patrolling document drift, fixed token budget. **Not a member** (Ed, 2026-08-18): it never judges and counts toward no quorum — it only proposes, and its proposals compete like anybody's. The surface card is **AI Proposals** 🤖 (was *Is There a Machine Member?*), and it lives in the **Proposals** section (the renamed Proposal Rate) beside the grant and the drip. **Ordinary since Q352** (Ed, 2026-08-18): it defaults to the founder, judging never waits on it, and a motion on it is a race — delegating it to the room stays the founder's option, most-restrictive answer winning.

**Design system (session-view, tokenised 2026-08-16 — the composer should adopt it rather than invent its own):**
- `cards.js` — **the decision-card grammar, one implementation** (2026-08-18, the system.css move again, after Ed's QA kept failing on setup cards for the structural reason that they were hand-written imitations of session-view's). `window.CARDS`: a pure layer (the diff/markdown engine, `resultOnly`, `speakerHtml`, `fieldHtml`, `fieldOf`, the drawn TICK/CROSS and MARK glyphs) and a `make(env)` factory (lane bars, proposal blocks, `clauseHeadHtml`, commit row, `laneBoxHtml`, the open/close geometry, `keepStill`) whose env keys are **functions read at call time** — session-view's `topUrgentId` is a reassigned `let`, its `chilled` a mutable Set, so nothing may be captured at make() time. Seams: `o.marks`/`o.washAttrs` on the clause head (the setup strip arrives pre-wrapped; session's chips/wash inject unchanged), `valAttr` (session radios speak `data-v`, setup composers `data-mval`, motion judging `data-motion`), a bindable speaker tooltip (setup carries the audited no-spec-citation copy). Load order on every consumer: `cards.js` → `setup.js` → inline. **The contamination guard**: `design/reference/` holds byte-frozen pre-extraction copies (tag `pre-cards`; never edit — re-freeze on intentional change), and `design/tools/session-probe.js` walks all 43 cards on the live page against the frozen one — whitespace-normalized outerHTML equality per card plus 0.0px geometry; it must be re-run **whenever cards.js changes**, and both runs must share one window size. The lift removed ~820 lines from session-view and was proven byte-identical. **The open/close animation clips only the bottom edge** (Ed's QA, 2026-08-19: *when I click on a tab, it disappears briefly before the decision-card appears*): `overflow: hidden` during expand/collapse was clipping the tab strip, which hangs entirely outside the card's box (`right: 100%`), for the whole motion — replaced with `clip-path: inset(-60px -100px 0 -100px)`, so the vertical reveal survives and the overhang stays visible; probe re-run IDENTICAL, 0 diffs (both pages probed from scroll 0 — a restored scroll position on one side reads as a constant offset in every rect). Two finds it forced: fitBand's head-rule correction had been **silently defeated by margin collapse** since the piles dissolved into block `.cpara`s (fixed with `display: flow-root` on the open paragraph — the discipline read 0.0px again only after being genuinely 20px off), and `motionCompose` plus the `.setrow` pile-fit pass were dead code the settled-card-is-the-composer build had already superseded.
- `wash` — the lifecycle colour on any surface that carries one: a clause, a `clause-head`, a held-open gap, a queue entry. **Colour changes fade** rather than jump, over `--wash-ms` (700ms), because a wash changing means something happened and a jump leaves the reader to notice it after the fact (Ed, 2026-08-17). Two things make that work: a queue entry's wash is painted as a **bar** (a `::before` whose width is the fill and whose background-color is the hue) rather than as a hard-stopped gradient, since no browser interpolates between two gradients; and because both rails are rebuilt wholesale on every render, each washed element is rendered wearing its **previous** colour and handed the new one after one forced reflow — keyed by what the wash is *about*, which is why a clause and the head of the card that swallows it share one key and opening a card deepens the colour rather than repainting it.
- `room-pulse` — one grey dot in the topbar that beats once per action by anybody, yourself included (Ed, 2026-08-17). The document is live and other people are working in it, and nothing on the surface said so. Deliberately **content-free**: no count, no direction, no author, so the only thing it can say is *the room is moving* — which is also what keeps it inside SPEC §3.5, since a heartbeat with nothing in it cannot leak standings. Grey at rest and grey at its brightest, because activity is information and nothing is being asked of you; it beats by growing a ring rather than by changing colour, since a colour change in the topbar would read as a state where this is an event. In the mockup the other fourteen members are a timer on a fixed *irregular* cycle — irregular because a metronome reads as a machine rather than as people, fixed because a mockup that behaves differently every time is one you cannot QA.
- `me` — the user avatar at the right of the topbar. Initials, not a photograph: there are no accounts behind it yet. It is the only face on the surface, since everybody else's authorship is sealed (SPEC §3.4) — Q278 asks whether that stays true.
- `lifecycle palette` — **three** hues held as raw RGB channels (Ed, 2026-08-17, third pass): `--lc-open` 🔥💡⚔️ yellow, `--lc-weigh` 🌶️ pink, `--lc-yours` ✏️ **blue**, and one grey shared by `--lc-deciding` ⏳ and `--lc-closed` ✔✖↻. Two moves made it one idea rather than six colours. **⏳ goes grey**: you have judged it and the race runs on without you, which is the purest piece of information on the surface and was wearing the accent — and it is the same thing ✅❎☑️ say, so they share a hue. **✏️ goes blue**, specifically the accent, because your own work is the one state about *you* rather than about the question, and the accent is already what this surface uses for you: your caret, your selection, the control you have turned on. Green leaves the margin entirely as a result, which is most of Q264 — `--ok` now means *decided* and nothing else. The greys are lighter than they were, on the same reasoning: a state that wants nothing should not be as loud as one that does. The rule this costs is 164's *colour means you can still affect it*, since a ⏳ race is revisable until it seals; the replacement is narrower and truer — **grey means nothing is being asked of you** (🔄 strains it and keeps grey anyway: what it tells you is that your old judgment is gone, not that it wants a new one yet). **🔥 gave up its own hue** (Ed, 2026-08-17): a flame is *an ordinary judgment that wants you most* — the same kind of thing as a bulb with a priority on it — so orange was claiming a difference in kind to say a difference in degree. It is the deepest yellow instead. Getting that to work took one measurement: dropped straight onto the urgency ramp it came out at alpha **0.292** against a neighbouring 💡 at **0.280**, which nobody can see, and which is not a fixture accident — the flame is usually only marginally the most urgent, because somebody has to be first among a set of close things. 🔥 does not mean *the top of the ramp*, it means **the question you are being asked next**, which is a category, and a ramp cannot express a category. So it takes a fixed alpha clear of the ramp's ceiling (`FLAME_A`, 0.44) and the ramp runs underneath it: same hue, decisively more of it. And **hot for actions, cold for information** (Ed, 2026-08-17) — orange and yellow are the states that want something from you, blue and grey are the ones telling you where things stand. That second rule is what placed the diagonal: it wants a judgment, so it must be hot; it is a different *kind* of judgment from a proposal, so it must not be the same hot as one. Applied identically to the queue card's wash, the paragraph's wash and the gutter mark. Green is available in a margin only because every *decided* state washes grey there — `--ok`'s green never leaves a card interior, and the two must not be allowed to meet (Q264).
- **Open is said by depth, not by outline** (Ed, 2026-08-17): no active box carries a border — not the decision card, not the drafting box, not the open rail entry. `--shadow-xl` is the only step allowed to be big, and it is still a light source rather than a halo (offset grows with blur, blur near 2.5x it, contact shadow keeping the near edge crisp). This retires 198’s one-blue-for-every-card as a *boundary*; the accent survives as an accent.
- **A settings choice is the session-view’s own radio** (Ed, 2026-08-18: *the non-slider choices in the cards are just radio buttons, and the session-view already has those*). Three controls became one: document-creation had `.opt`, founding-ceremony had `.rung`, and both were a hand-rolled radio in a bordered box standing a millimetre from `.lanepick` — which is the radio this system already has, and the one every member will have used a hundred times by the end of a session. A setting is exactly the judgment a lane bar puts: *one of these, or none of them yet*. The box went with it, and that is a gain: **open is said by depth, not by outline**, and a rule drawn round each option was competing with the radio for the job of saying which one is chosen.
- `--primary` — the one accent: every wire, every selection, every control that is on. `--ok` — green, meaning *decided* and only that.
- type scale `--t-lead / --t-body / --t-ui / --t-small / --t-cap / --t-micro` — **and every piece of text on the surface is on it** (housekeeping pass, 2026-08-17). It had six steps and the stylesheet used thirteen: 0.58, 0.62, 0.7, 0.75, 0.78, 0.8, 0.85 and 0.95rem all appeared as literals, most within half a pixel of a token that already existed. What is still allowed to be a literal is exactly one category, and naming it is what keeps the scale honest: a **glyph** size (`.achip` 1.125rem, the glyph buttons 1.2/1.35rem, the serif italic *I* 1.05rem) tunes a silhouette to a box rather than text to a scale, and has no business on the same ladder as prose. The `.eyebrow` class carries the single upper-case label treatment, at `--t-micro`, and it **names its users in its own selector** — `.headlab`, `.fieldlab`, `.rtag`, `.rechead`, `.pwhere` — because the same pass found the class had been written with the comment *one rule, so they cannot drift apart* and had zero consumers, while four hand-rolled copies of it sat at three different sizes. Each user keeps colour and layout and nothing else. A shared rule nothing points at is not a shared rule, it is one more opinion.
- **A commit row is one object, so its controls share a height** (housekeeping pass, 2026-08-17): 40px, set by the row, on every card.   - `clause-tab` — the object in the `chip-gutter`: one per live or filed decision at that clause, carrying its `lifecycle mark`, and the **only** way into a decision card from the document. **An object, not a glyph with a hit area** (Ed, 2026-08-17): a resting ground in its own lifecycle hue, the contact shadow, 34×30 with the glyph above document size, lifting on hover and going *down* on press — the same object the rail's sealed dot already was, since they are one control in two columns. **On an open card they are literally its tabs** (Ed, 2026-08-17): they already travelled into the head, already stood beside the card, and clicking another already switched the card to it — what was missing was that any of it looked like a tab strip. **The strip does not reorder** (Ed, 2026-08-17): the active card's tab used to be lifted to the top, so every switch dealt the column again and the tab you were aiming at moved out from under the pointer on arrival — a tab strip is a fixed set of places you move a highlight around, which is the whole of what makes it a strip rather than a list of shortcuts. It costs nothing that the active one is no longer first, because the gutter pile only ever opens its *front* tab, which is index 0 in the same stack order the strip uses, so the mark you clicked is still at index 0 when the card arrives. The active tab instead **grows 8px out to the left** (Ed, 2026-08-17) — leftward is the only direction available, since the column is right-aligned onto the card's edge, so growing is automatically growing left and the strip keeps its single vertical joint; the 8px goes on `padding-left` as well as `width` so the glyph does not move. Rounded on the left only so the right edge is a joint; the active one deepened and 6px wider so it carries under the card's left edge with no seam, casting leftward only. The extra width is pushed out with a negative right margin, because the column is right-aligned and widening alone moves the glyph — the one thing the card-opening motion is built on it not doing. It **keeps its colour when active** rather than taking the card's white: now that the prose carries no highlight the gutter holds the only lifecycle colour on that side of the screen, and a tab that went white on opening would throw away the one thing it is there to say. A flatter glyph set is Q288.
  - `tab-stack` — what a clause's tabs are when the card is **closed** (Ed, 2026-08-17). A strip of tabs seen closed is a *pile*: the front tab is the object, the rest slide up under it leaving a sliver each, and opening it expands the pile into the tab strip down the side of the card, which already existed and already worked because a card is ~380px tall. It replaces a column that ran at full height — § Bringing a Guest was 129px of tabs beside a 36px clause, so three of four marks stood beside prose they had nothing to do with and the overhang rendered *on top of* the held-open gap's own mark below. Four rules. **The front is what most wants you**, and that order is deliberately *not* `KEEP_ORDER`: the contents rail ranks by what must not be lost (so ✏️ sits above 💡, since a proposal of yours carries the largest remaining act), the stack ranks by what most wants you (so ✏️ sits *below* 💡, since your own work is asking nothing and you do not click into a pile to be shown it). Retention and priority are different questions with the same-looking answer, and the stack is the first place they disagree. **The slivers carry hue, not a count** — colour is the whole vocabulary, so an orange sliver says *something here is urgent* without drawing a flame, and a count would be the tally Ed removed from the queue the same morning; they are mixed stronger than a tab face (42% against 18%) because four pixels of an 18% mix is a smudge, and squared at the top because a radius eats a 4px band. **The slivers are inert**: one stack, one target — the full strip is a click away, and a 4px sliver is not a control to ask anybody to hit. **The pile is fitted, not fixed** — `fitStacks()` runs after layout over the whole gutter and shrinks each stack's peek until it reaches no further than the next mark, which is what makes two anchors claiming one strip *structurally* impossible rather than merely rarer; it is the same move the `needs-you-queue` makes when it steps entries around a pinned card, and it is the answer to the question 294 could not reach, since each `chipcol` is laid out inside its own anchor and knows nothing of the one below. The residual — a single tab is 30px and cannot shrink — is Q308. **Filed decisions live at the bottom of the strip as a pile of their own** (Ed, 2026-08-17, answering 294): a card lines its live tabs up at full height and the ones already filed keep the closed posture underneath them, newest at the top — because only the top glyph shows when it is shut, so it must be the last thing that happened here — until you click the pile, when they line up too and **the card grows to hold them all**, a floor on its height rather than a height. Which is the stack applied one level down, and it is what makes the answer to *where does history live* cost nothing: no history tab, no separate surface, no rule about when a filed mark may appear. Filed decisions stay **off the closed gutter**, where the pile is still live-only. Decided-but-unread is not filed and stays with the live tabs, because it is still asking for its OK. Related fix: hole rectangles in the wire-shadow clip are now **merged** before being punched, because the clip is one `evenodd` path and two overlapping holes XOR back to solid; a pile overlaps by 26px of every 30, and 74 boxes in the fixture resolve to 48 non-overlapping holes.
  - `decision card` — **the clause, opened.** It *replaces* its paragraph rather than opening beneath it (Ed, QA 2026-08-16; also settles 265), so the thing being proposed about appears exactly once on screen. The shape is a post and its replies: `clause-head` → `proposal-block` for each candidate → commit row, with a hairline between each of the three bands. One card for every type, differing only in what fills the two halves — quick card: clause + one proposal; race: clause + two; patch: the same per site, plus the ↑/↓ stepper; sealed record: clause + the whole field ranked with its numbers. Submitting does not close the card. **Opening**: the card does not grow from nothing — it grows from exactly the height at which only its `clause-head` is showing, so frame one looks like the document with the paragraph still in it, the gap then slides open beneath the clause, and the field fades in 60ms behind the motion. The clause itself never moves (measured 0px on every card type). **Closing runs the card's whole box back onto its paragraph** (Ed, 2026-08-17) — height down to the `clause-head`'s own washed block, padding away, eyebrow shut, lift and white ground gone, and the top edge descending by exactly what it stops holding so the clause still does not move. It used to stop at head height, which is where *opening* starts but is not a paragraph, so the last 93px arrived as a jump; opening gets away with that discrepancy because it happens in the frame of the click, closing put it after the motion, which is the one order the eye will not forgive. The card's own mark closes it — a stale guard had made every mark inside a card inert.
    - `clause-head` — the clause itself at the top of the card, at document size and colour, under an eyebrow. **It keeps the clause's own highlight and its own gutter mark** (Ed, 2026-08-17): the washed block inside it is given the same box as a `.anch` paragraph — same negative margin, same padding — so the wash lands on the same rectangle and the `.chipcol` inside it lands in the same gutter column. Measured: the mark you clicked moves 0px in both axes, and the clause text sits on the prose's own axis to the pixel, which is what makes the card read as the clause opening rather than as something replacing it. The card's mark is the same control it was in the gutter, so it also closes the card; other live suggestions at that clause stack under it exactly as they did beside the paragraph. It carries a `lane-bar` exactly when the judgment can keep the clause: on a quick card or a patch the incumbent is one of the two things being judged; on a **race** both candidates are challengers and nothing on the card can vote to keep it (the adoption-threshold decides that, SPEC §5), so there the head is reference only.
    - `proposal-block` — one candidate: its wording, stating its own change against the clause (see `result-only`); then its rationale behind a `sealed-speaker`; then its `lane-bar`. The reply in the post-and-replies shape, and the reason each argument now sits with its own wording instead of being mapped column-to-column.
    - `sealed-speaker` — the featureless head-and-shoulders disc a rationale sits behind. Ed's note was that nothing said a *person* was saying the rationale; the disc says it, and its blankness says the rest, because authorship is sealed until the record (SPEC §3.4). Drawn rather than left as a plain circle — a bare disc reads as a bullet, and a bullet says *list item*.
    - `result-only` — a proposal states **the text as it would stand**, with what it adds marked green and nothing struck through (Ed, 274, 2026-08-17: *normal people struggle to read or interpret redlines*). This restores 91 and retires the `redline` that briefly replaced it. One rendering everywhere — card, lane and record — so a proposal reads the same wherever you meet it. Two rules survive from the redline: a **marking floor**, below half the new text surviving from the clause the proposal states itself plainly (which is what keeps a race candidate, usually a whole rewrite, from being lit green end to end — Q92); and **punctuation as its own token**, without which a clause that gains a comma marks the whole word. The blind spot it accepts: in a stacked card a proposal whose only change is a *partial* cut looks like the clause above it, and you find it by reading. The worst case is covered — a proposal that removes the text **entirely** would render as blank, which cannot be told from unchanged, so it says *(all text removed)* instead, drawn as a pseudo-element so it is never content the caret can land behind or `htmlToMd` can serialise.
    - `lane-bar` — the control strip at the foot of each text, replacing "the lanes are the buttons" (197, retired at Ed's QA: clicking a clause is what `always-on-typing` means by *put a caret here*, so the gesture could not also mean *I prefer this*). A radio marked **Prefer this** / **Preferred** (272), plus ✏️ *edit this*. The rule that governs it: **a lane may carry what is about that lane, and nothing else**. The radio is the **loudest thing in the lane** (Ed, 286) — UI size at weight 600 on a 1.5px edge with a 15px dot, above the ✏️ beside it — because since 197 was retired the radio *is* the judgment, and a card whispering the one act it exists for was the wrong way round.
    - `commit row` — the card's bottom band, and the third radio (Ed, 2026-08-16, reversing 202/204/209/213). **Indifferent** sits at the left as the last of the trio — labelled, not drawn as 🤷, because every candidate carries a radio now and *neither of these* is plainly one more answer to the same question; it stays out of the lanes because it is a judgment about the *pair* (SPEC §3.2). The green **✓** sits at the right, always present and **greyed until something is chosen** — an absent button gave the row no shape and the reader no idea what finishing looked like. **❄️ appears only on the 🔥 card**, immediately left of the ✓ and paired with it (Ed, 2026-08-17). It is not an act on the judgment — nothing is skipped, recirculated or decayed and no evidence is touched — but a **toggle on the flame**: it takes the card out of the running for 🔥, so it drops back to an ordinary 💡 and the next most urgent question takes the flame. That is what a member means when they want to move on, and it is reversible because a mood is. Pressing it **closes the card** (going somewhere else is the whole gesture); un-pressing it does not, because there you are coming back. Its pressed state is the toggle, so it needs no words — the old control needed five. 🤷 has left the surface. **Every radio on a card lines up down its left edge** (Ed, 2026-08-17), locked cards included — a locked one used to keep a centred row on the argument that a lone disabled radio pushed left leaves the other end empty, which is true and does not matter: the alignment is what says the three are answers to one question.
  - `deadlock-card` — what a ⚔️ race opens into, and **its own card rather than a race card with a bridge button on it** (Ed, 2026-08-17). By the time you see it the race has nothing left to ask you (SPEC §8.3b), so there is no live judgment card here for it to replace. The clause at the head, reference only; then the card's one raised voice, in the band the field would otherwise start — *the room has stopped moving on this; more judging will not decide it* — which on any other card would be a caveat at the foot and here is the point; then **everything in flight**, full width, oldest first, because arrival order is the only ordering that is not a ranking and a field of eight is largely a conversation in which each wording answers the ones before it; then what you said as a grey record line, one line for however many pairs it took, since the card is about the race and not about any pair in it; then **the desk**: the same `laneBoxHtml` the `editing-card` uses, with the same commit row — 🗑️ at the very left, hold-✏️ at the very right — because the eight wordings are not a reference you go away from, they are what you write *against*, so the reading room and the desk are one surface (Ed, 2026-08-17). Before a draft exists the lane holds the clause and is a real editor, and the first keystroke opens the draft with that character applied — `always-on-typing` applied to a box instead of a paragraph, and literally the same function underneath. Every wording in the field carries ✏️ *propose edit*, the lane bar minus its radio, so you take whichever came closest and write from it. The field is marked `result-only` **with the floor forced off**: left alone the floor marks three of eight, because each is far enough from the clause to count as a rewrite, so the card that most needs the marking is the one the floor silences — and the floor exists to stop a *lane* being lit beside its incumbent, where here the incumbent is at the head and comparison is the point. **No standings, and therefore no lock** (Ed, 303): a card showing rankings would be the briefing §3.5 permits on a race out of the judgment stream, and would have to close your judgments with it, because reading the standings and then revising is informed judgment in a blind field. A card showing only wordings and rationales leaks nothing — both are already public — so it takes nothing away and needs no rule. Two renderings tried and rejected, both recorded in Q301: marking against the clause (`result-only` marks three of eight, since at this distance they are rewrites and the floor states them plain) and ranking (unavailable by construction — being level is what deadlock means). The open question is that at eight the comparison you need is each-against-the-others, which nothing computed against the incumbent can draw (Q305, Q306).
  - `sealed record` — every entry opens one, filed ones included. **The card starts at the top of the field** (Ed, 2026-08-17): on a decided card the top of the ranking *is* the clause — the winner where it carried, the incumbent where it held — so the top entry **is** the head, keeping the head machinery (the gutter mark, the washed block on the paragraph axis, the opening geometry) and giving up only its label, which becomes its own rank, outcome and score. It carries no box either: a green-bordered panel round the winner was the fourth device saying *this one*. Then **one list of the whole field**, winner first, each proposal full width with its win probability as a **percentage**, right-aligned into a column. **The incumbent is in the list** (Ed, 2026-08-17), and at a true place: every score is the probability that proposal beats the current text, so the current text sits at **50%** by construction — which makes the column say *these two beat what we had and those three did not*, the most useful sentence on the card and one that was nowhere on it before. It wears a dashed edge, shows its own 50% at a muted weight (a construction, not a measurement), and is labelled *the text it replaced* or *the text that stood*. **Whichever text is the clause at the head does not print itself again** — the winner where it carried, the incumbent where it held — which is the whole of the duplication three separate bands used to produce. The bar is stated **once**, in the field label, rather than once per proposal and again in the record. Plus quorum met and what you did. Unread decisions pin themselves until you press **OK**, at the bottom right where every card commits; reading alone does not clear it. The button is a word, not a glyph: a tray-and-arrow reads as *download*, an archive box says *storage*, an eye says *seen* rather than *done* — there is no glyph in common use for *I have taken this in*, and when a set has no member the word is the answer rather than the fallback.
  - `evidence-meter` — closeness-to-resolution as a magnitude, never a direction. Lives **only** on queue cards, where the wash's fill *is* the bar; decision cards carry no progress. Since Ed, 285 the bar sits on a **ground** of the same hue across the whole card, so the card *is* its colour and the bar says how far through that colour you are — a card had no visible right edge before, and width is the one dimension of a rail entry that carries no meaning and so has no excuse for being the one you cannot see. The ground is derived from the bar's own colour by swapping its alpha, so the two can never drift apart; and the bar's leading edge is drawn a second time in that colour, because at the bottom of the urgency scale the fill would otherwise be too close to its own ground to show an edge, and the edge is the whole of what a progress bar says.
  - `queue-wire` — for the open judgment only, the run from its entry down the gutter into the place in the charter it refers to. **A patch is joined at its cards, not in the rail** (Ed, 2026-08-17): the spine down the middle of the gutter is gone, and the patch's decision cards are linked instead by a cable in the `chip-gutter` that leaves each `clause-tab` by its **left side**, curves onto a run just outside the column, and curves back into the next. Joining the pointers said *these three entries are one*; the thing that is actually one is the three clauses. The side exit is also what retires the stacking problem outright — a cable that never enters the column cannot cross a tab in it — and it lets the cable leave the patch's own tab rather than whatever strip happens to hold it. It carries no cap: a landing ball says *this clause, here*, and both ends of this one are a tab that has already said it. Consecutive segments share the run and overlap at each middle tab, which costs nothing (the ink group carries the alpha) and gives the middle sites a drop off a through-run rather than two cables meeting end to end. `clause-tab`s are punched out of the shadow for the same reason cards are: a tab is an object at card height, and it sits *outside* its card's box. **6px, opaque, and exactly the colour of its own queue card** (Ed, 287 and the QA of 2026-08-17), with a 10px cap at the **document end only** — the rail-end cap said nothing the card it was leaving did not already say, where the far end is the wire's actual claim, *this clause, here*. The colour is the card's wash **composited over white and painted on a white cable of the same weight**: opaque as an object, identical as a colour, which is why it also carries urgency for free — a 🔥 card's cable is the strongest on the surface. Three versions to get there and the last two are one lesson: a hue darkened toward the ink reads as a different object from the card, and the wash used *as a wash* is the right colour in the wrong medium, because a translucent cable shows the charter through it. **Match what the eye sees, not what the token says.** It is **lifted to the cards' own height** (Ed, 289a) — the same three-layer light as `--shadow-xl`, on the container so the union of underlay and colour casts one shadow rather than two of the same cable stacked. A cord lying flat between two things visibly off the page was the odd object out, and depth is the only thing this surface uses to say where something is. The colour layer is painted at **full strength inside a group carrying the alpha**, so every run, cap and link composites exactly once: painting translucent shapes over each other doubled the alpha wherever they met, which made the landing ball darker than its cable and a patch's shared segments darker where they met. 198's one-colour rule was about *kind*, which the wire was never asked to say; lifecycle is what its mark and its wash already say, so the wire agreeing adds no vocabulary. The accent is not missed: only the open judgment ever draws a wire, so its existence already says *this is the open one*. A sealed dot carries no wash, so its wire takes the fixed grey the dot itself wears. It **follows its card's colour**, fading over the same 700ms by the same technique the washes use — born wearing the previous colour, handed the new one after a forced reflow, keyed by the judgment so a redraw at a new scroll position does not re-run the fade (Ed, 2026-08-17). Its shadow is drawn by hand and clipped so that **every** card on the surface is punched out of it, not only the two it joins: the rail is a layer of cards at one height and a cable at that height passes several of them, and a thing does not shadow its own layer. Two nested groups, clip on the outer and the shadow's offset on the inner — a `clip-path` resolves *after* the referencing element's own transform, so both on one group moved the holes down with the shadow and left a 3px sliver along every card's top edge. `section-toggle` — the fold triangle, mirrored in the contents rail and the document gutter. The `contents-rail`'s own hierarchy was **inverted** until the housekeeping pass of 2026-08-17 — a Part at 12px, a chapter inside it at 13px, a section inside that at 12.48px, so the outermost level was the smallest text in the column — and it now reads as a divider and two levels: `--t-cap` upper-case bold for a Part, then one size (`--t-small`) for both levels under it, told apart by weight and indent, which is the job indentation was already doing.
- `race-card` — the judging surface: contested text, two candidates, A/B/indifferent/propose-C. In the UI it is the session-view's escalation state. **design/race-card.html is stale and should not be copied from** — it predates the whole current card grammar (yellow cards, per-candidate verdict buttons, "Option A/B" labels), all of which session-view has since replaced. Session-view is the only current word on card design; bringing race-card.html forward is now a rebuild, and Q70 asks whether it should exist at all.
- `composer` — **not a surface**: you compose by editing the charter (Ed, 224–241; built into session-view 2026-08-16). Its parts:
  - `always-on-typing` — every clause **and every heading** carries a caret (headings since Ed, 2026-08-17 — a section that cannot be renamed is one the charter cannot revise), and every input into one is intercepted: the charter is never edited in place, and the keystroke instead opens the `editing-card` with that character already applied. A selection may **span blocks**: the run is flattened to one string with a newline between blocks, the selection is located in *that* string, and the edit applied to it — so deleting a whole section is one edit to one piece of text, which is why it comes out as **one candidate and not a patch** (Ed, 2026-08-17). The block type travels with each origin, so a heading still reads as a heading in the lane. The caret is the accent blue, and **where it lands is announced when the surface moved it** — a short flash that shrinks onto the bar and fades — which fires on the one caret move the reader did not make: typing in the charter carries the caret off into the proposing lane of a card that has just appeared. Not on a click; a click is you putting the caret somewhere and you already know where (Ed, 2026-08-17). Its shape is not settable — CSS gives `caret-color` and nothing else (Q281). This is what makes a caret in a constitutional document safe. It also makes the **gutter mark the only way** into a decision card from the document, which is why the mark is now a 24×22 target rather than a bare glyph (Ed, 224).
  - `editing-card` — the same `decision card` shape as everything else (Ed, 2026-08-16, closing Q275 and Q270 with it): the clause at the head, your draft as the single reply in the one box left on the surface, your rationale a field in the `sealed-speaker`'s own slot — **inside the same lifted box as the wording** (Ed, 2026-08-17): the two are one *editing surface*, because you are expected to fill in both, so they sit at one height with a hairline between rather than as two boxes at different depths — so you write the sentence in the place, and at the weight, everybody else will read it in. Propose and Cancel below. It had been two side-by-side lanes on the argument that while writing you want the original level with what you are typing; that does not survive contact — the original is one line up, your additions are **marked green as you type** (Ed, 263), and a full-width lane is a far better place to write prose than a 300px column. Free to open; the edit is spent at **Propose**, which is where the price is said in words. The rationale's placeholder reads *We should change this because…* and is drawn **out of the text flow** — as inline content it sat in the line and put the caret after it, so an empty field looked like text you could not delete (Ed, 2026-08-17). The lane preserves spaces: it rewrites its own markup on every keystroke so the green marking can follow the caret, and a trailing space collapsed to nothing in the re-render until the blocks were set `pre-wrap`.
  - `lane-controls` — the only editing controls on the surface, in the top right of the `editing-card`'s box (Ed, 2026-08-17). **B**, *I*, and a two-segment **Rich / Markdown** toggle. They were going to live in the topbar; that is the wrong home, because nothing outside an open card can be edited, so a topbar toolbar would be greyed for the whole session except the minute you are writing. A candidate's text **is** markdown — most people edit it rendered, some switch to monospace source to check the characters are exactly what they meant, and that only means anything if the characters are what is stored. Rich mode serialises back through `htmlToMd` so editing rendered never silently drops the emphasis it is showing; switching view **converts the caret offset**, because markdown mode counts the syntax characters and rich mode does not. Inline only — bold, italic, code: a charter is prose, and block structure is already carried by the run of clauses.
  - `draft-site` — a **run of adjacent blocks** — clauses, headings, or both — replaced by one piece of text. One site is **one candidate**, whatever the run's length: a patch is one judgment shown at several *separate* places, and a run is one place that happens to be several blocks long (Ed, 2026-08-17). Editing across a paragraph break joins them (Ed, 225); editing somewhere else makes a second site, and the draft becomes a patch in the making — a card at each place, one Propose, the entries cabled on the wire spine (Ed, 232, 237). One draft at a time, because there is one caret (Q266).
  - `propose-edit` — ✏️ on either lane of a decision card: start writing from *that* wording rather than from the charter's (Ed, 228). `Propose something else` survives only on a **deadlocked** race, where a bridge is the ask rather than an extra.
  - `proposal-row` — **the proposal's whole lifecycle is one commit row** (Ed, 2026-08-17), and the row does not move as the proposal advances: 🗑️ at the **very left** throughout, the commit control at the **very right**. While it is a draft the right slot is a bare **✏️ you hold down**, and while you hold it one of your pencils **flies out of the `edit-wallet` and lands on the button** — the proposal goes in when it arrives, and letting go early sends it home with nothing spent (Ed, 2026-08-17). The gesture and the price are one object: what you are holding down for is the time it takes the edit to travel. Under reduced motion it fades at the wallet and arrives at the button instead — same gesture, same duration, no flight. 🗑️ discards, and nothing has been spent; once pressed the card becomes your-proposal, the right slot becomes a pressed *✏️ Submitted*, and 🗑️ is `withdraw`, which hands the edit back in full (SPEC §3.3a) and is the reason your entry stays pinned. **Withdrawing flies the pencil home** (Ed, 2026-08-17) — the same object that paid, making the return journey in 640ms, leaving in the pose it arrived in and landing flat. Discarding an unproposed draft flies nothing, which is the point of the symmetry: nothing was spent. **The flight is an arc, drawn fresh each time** — a quadratic bowed off the straight run by a signed amount, sampled into keyframes because a shape that changes every flight cannot be a CSS rule; which side it swings and how far are the whole of the variation. Let go and it comes home along its own arc, run backwards at four times the speed. In both directions the **edit in flight is drawn exactly once**: outbound the wallet keeps its count and leaves the traveller's slot empty (it is not spent until it lands), inbound it holds its old count until the pencil is in it — render state, not a poke at the DOM, because the drip rebuilds the wallet every second and was putting the flying pencil straight back. The right-hand control going from the act to the fact of it is exactly what the judgment row's ✓ does when pressed — one lifecycle, not two screens. 🗑️ stays an ordinary outline button rather than a red one: discarding and withdrawing are both real things to do and not dangers. The Propose button is **blue, not green**, a consequence of the palette move rather than a separate decision — green means *decided* and a submission is the start of a decision, and everything else about your work is the accent.

- `motion` — a proposal to change a **setting** rather than a clause (Ed, 2026-08-18), and the one act by which a settled document changes its own rules. Which route it takes is a fact about the setting and not about the motion: an **ordinary** motion is an ordinary race, judged pairwise against the value as it stands and carrying at the bar with quorum — **and since Q390 (Ed, 2026-08-19, SPEC v0.53) it is the race machinery whole**: rival values join the same race (propose-C over values), entries stake and refund like text proposals, and a standing-value change mid-race is a ground shift; identical values dedup by equality; a **constitutional** one is **a unanimous vote on the proposed amendment** (Ed, 2026-08-18, v0.47 — superseding the re-ask-the-founding-question design): accept it or keep what stands, one refusal keeps what stands, blind while it runs. There is therefore **no new object on the surface for amending a constitution**: an ordinary motion is a decision card with a value where the prose would be, and a constitutional motion is the card that asked the question in the first place, live again. An invitation is a constitutional motion (Ed: *drafters can also make proposals to invite new members, which are passed in the constitutional way*), so one member who says no keeps a joiner out. SPEC §9.6; raising one is free and open to any member, limited by the holder rather than the setting — **one 🏛️ out per member at a time**, returned whole on withdrawal or settlement (Ed, 2026-08-18, closing Q327). **It settles live** (v0.48): accept / keep / abstain, abstention never blocks, and it carries the moment every *currently active* member stands at accept-or-abstain with zero keep — no electorate snapshot, the base re-checked on every answer and roster event. **The mover stands at accept from the open** (Ed, 2026-08-18, v0.49: proposers prefer their own proposals, as §3.3 counts an author's own preference without asking — revisable, and 🗑️ stays their way out), and the consent card offers all three answers, the third labelled **Abstain** (Q370 closed (a), Ed's word). ## Reversals worth keeping (2026-08-21)

- **"Untitled" in the birth heading, restored the evening it was removed.** That morning the heading was blanked until a title was set, on the argument that *Untitled* was helper text standing where the answer goes — the one place this surface never puts a suggestion. That evening Ed watched a user tester **type the document's title into the prose column underneath the empty heading**, and asked for it back. The argument had misidentified the object: the answer goes on the 🪶 card, and the heading is where the *result* appears, so "Untitled" is an honest statement of the result so far rather than a suggestion competing with a lane. Kept as a reversal rather than folded silently, because the losing argument is a good argument that a single observation beat — which is the whole case for testing surfaces on people.

- **Acknowledgment gates the power, not the receipt.** The grant tasks (Q448/453) were built so that no power arrives without acknowledgment, but the acknowledgment reached only three `style.display` lines — the ✏️ ✒️ 🏛️ wallets. Everything a power actually *does* was gated on the module's own `can*` predicates, which go true at 🍾. So between the press and the OK a member held every power with no wallet to show for it: the receipt was hidden and the power ran. Ed's rule (*I should not be able to do actions until I have acknowledged that I have been given the right to do them*) makes the OK the conferring act rather than a notification about one. What made this cheap to get wrong: the wallet is the visible half, so gating it *looks* like gating the power, and every fixture walk presses the OKs in order and never observes the window in between.

## Glossary — named parts

Use these names in all discussion, commits, and code. Literal and stable beats elegant.

**Retired names**, kept here so a lookup lands: `document-creation`, `founding-ceremony` and `setup.html` (the surfaces) → `session-view.html`; `edit-wallet` → `propose-wallet`; `setup-piles` → `constitution-section`; `drafting-desk`, `arrival-bar`, `bounty-board`, `redline` → gone. Their reasoning is in `design/DECISIONS.md`.

**Engine (mechanism, no UI):**
- `engine-core` — session state machine: candidates, races, adoptions, the adoption-threshold ramp, tokens/refunds, adoption floors, certification. Caught up through 367(b): quorum rides the floor (§4.2), the drip is real minutes (`tokenDripMinutes`), amendments bind races in flight (`amend`/`standing-set`, §4.3 anchor re-seating — **a bar never jumps**), suspension is lapse engine-side (§9.5a), **setting races** carry ordinary motions (a candidate is a patch *or* a typed value; adoption of a value is a verdict, applied only by the host's `setStanding`, which is also the ground shift). The hash is pure TS — the engine is node-free and browser-loadable.
  **Adoptions batch on the cooldown metronome** (§4.2): when the cooldown from the last batch elapses, every race clearing threshold+floor adopts at once — the document changes at most once per cooldown, by as much as the room decided. One decision per race per batch (the ready set snapshots before anything lands), oldest first, each rebasing the next; **a race adopts only once somebody has actually judged in it**. `Session.tick(t)` is the host clock; the bridge's `tick(t)` rides it and the server's minute timer drives the bridge, so a due batch lands even in a quiet room. The cooldown is **engine tuning, never constitutional**, and a cleared race is never surfaced as waiting — no ⏳, since deriving "cleared and cooling" would be a standings reveal.
- `adoption-threshold` — the confidence bar a challenger's win-probability must clear; ramps over the session window (wall clock). Formerly "θ"; always use this name.
- `patch-engine` — text machinery: diffs, footprints, three-way merge, rebase, surgery.
- `overlap-gates` — the three-gate classifier for colliding patches: textual composition → semantic composition → rivalry.
- `ranking-model` — Bradley–Terry preference model (per race) + global salience model, with active pair sampling and deadlock detection.
- `router` — v/c_p feed ordering, hot set, exploration/salience streams, floors-aware serving, notification digests.
- `event-log` — append-only hash-chained log, seeded RNG, participant receipts.
- `dedup-gate` — submission-time duplicate check (embeddings, edit distance, LLM equivalence) plus behavioral dedup probes.
- `race-labeler` — advisory naming and typing of disputes: a name ("treasurer oversight") and a type (copy-edit · substantive · structural) per race, from the oracle's `describeRace`, with a deterministic nearest-heading + excerpt fallback. Outside the state machine — labels never enter the event log or gate anything; type is stored but not wired to routing (Q49).
- `document-modes` — two **independent** settings, both fixed at creation (SPEC §9.0). **Window**: an end datetime, or none (perpetual) — one question with one answer, which is why the surface asks it on one card (⏰ *When does it end?*) with *never* as an option. **Threshold shape**: fixed, or ramping. A windowed document may hold its threshold fixed; only a perpetual one is *constrained*, since a ramp needs an endpoint to ramp toward. A window gives you the closing ceremony and a drip paced against it; without one the drip runs against real time and the document ends by `freeze`.
- `founding-ceremony` — the stage before any drafting where the membership sets quorum and threshold: each member states, blind, the *lowest they will accept*, and the document takes the **maximum** of each. A consent rule rather than a vote, which is what lets it escape the constitutional bootstrap — the result satisfies every stated minimum by construction. Distribution published without names (SPEC §9.0a).
- `sign-out` — a member declaring they are done, choosing **holding** (they stay in the quorum base, so enough of them freezes the document — *I do not consent to you finishing without me*) or **abstaining** (they leave the base — *I trust you to finish up*). Plain silence is never sign-out; judgments already cast keep counting afterwards; the base is the whole roster minus abstainers, never the active remainder (SPEC §9.5).
- `freeze` — what happens when active plus holding falls below quorum: races park, participants are notified, the record is cut. A stall with an alarm, not a death — it thaws if enough return, and it is the perpetual mode's equivalent of a close.
- `coherence-auditor` — machine drafter patrolling document drift, fixed token budget. **Not a member**: it never judges and counts toward no quorum — it only proposes, and its proposals compete like anybody's. Surface card: **AI Proposals** 🤖, in the **Proposals** section beside the grant and the drip. **Ordinary**: defaults to the founder, judging never waits on it, a motion on it is a race; delegating it to the room stays the founder's option, most-restrictive answer winning.

## ⏱️'s *I set it* fills its own fields (2026-08-24, Q740)

Ed, 2026-08-23: *when you choose "I set it" on the proposal rate, it should have defaults — 4 to
start with, up to a maximum of 6, an additional every 10 minutes.* The rule is in `SURFACE.md` F6;
what is here is why it does not contradict the thing F6 has said since Q511.

**Why ⏱️ alone.** Every other founding card asks for a *choice* — a rung, a date, a number the
founder has a view about. ⏱️ asks for a **shape**: three numbers that only mean anything together,
whose relation (the maximum is not below the start, the drip is in minutes) is the whole of the
decision. A founder who has never run one of these has no view on 4 against 6 and no way to form
one from the card, and the card answered that with three empty boxes and a dark ✓ — the one place
on the surface where *no defaults* left somebody with nothing to work from rather than a clear
question. The other multi-field card, 🌡️'s ramp, is F6's standing exception for a related reason
(Q512: the ramp is part of what the threshold says). ⏱️ does **not** join it there, and that is the
point of the next paragraph.

**Why a clarification of F6 rather than an exception to it.** The test F6 states is about what a
card **arrives** as, and SPEC §9.0a says the same in the spec's words — a setting is born with its
value unset, nothing arrives answered. Under Ed's answer (1) that stays literally true: the fill
hangs on the *rung click*, so the card opens with three blank boxes and the founder has to pick
before anything appears. What the machine then writes is an answer to a question the founder has
just asked it. F6 already carries the converse half — *typing into a rung's own field is choosing
that rung* — and this is the other direction of the same identity between a rung and its fields.
It is also **complete** rather than partial, which is what makes it a rule and not a special case:
`.pick .inner` is `display: none` until the rung is on (`design/setup.css:148`), so ⏱️'s fields are
physically unreachable before the pick and F6's typing half can never fire first on this card. The
radio is the only entrance, so hanging the fill there covers every path in.

**Why the anchoring ban does not reach here.** The `consent-slider` has nothing preselected on the
stated principle that *a suggested value is exactly the anchoring blind collection exists to
prevent*. That ban is about **blind collection**: many people answering the same question without
seeing each other, where one number on the screen coordinates a room that must not read itself, and
the document then takes an extremum of answers that were never independent. None of that machinery
is present on the founder's half of ⏱️. One person has taken the decision, there is nobody to
coordinate with, and they can see the numbers, change them and commit. Anchoring a room is a defect
in a collection mechanism; anchoring one decider who can overwrite what they are anchored to is
called a starting point. The ban stays exactly where it is, on the member's answer card.

**The two guards, and what each is for.** *Only empty fields* is the one that keeps the fill from
being destructive: a founder who typed 12, wandered off to the delegate rung and came back keeps
their 12, because the fill has nothing to say about a field that already carries an answer. *Only
on the transition* is the subtler one: without it, re-clicking a rung that is already on would
refill a box the founder had just deliberately cleared — the machine quietly undoing an act, which
is the failure mode every no-defaults rule on this surface exists to prevent. Together they mean
the fill can only ever put numbers where there were none, and only in answer to a fresh pick.

**Silent, per Ed's answer (3).** No *suggested* note and no explanatory line. A note would be the
card apologising for numbers the founder can read and change; and the surface has one place where
it does announce a machine-chosen value — 📍's suggested address (F11), which says so because that
value is about to become *public and permanent* and the founder may never look at the field again.
Three numbers sitting in three visible boxes above a lit ✓ are neither.

**Scope, per Ed's answer (4).** The delegated fallbacks follow — `ANSTYPED.rate` widened a member's
one-number answer with a hard-coded `8` and `240`, which were literally the defaults Q511 forbade,
firing exactly where the founder had delegated ⏱️ and set no machinery, so the document took
numbers nobody gave. They read `RATE_START` now. ⏩'s `SEED` and the post-start motion composer keep
theirs: the first is fixture furniture that writes `S` directly and never meets the click handler,
and the second should show what stands today rather than the factory numbers. The engine's own
fallbacks are untouched, being engine tuning (R-040). That the three sets now disagree in three
ways is real, and is filed as Q741 rather than fixed under a ruling that did not ask for it.

A render detaches the button under the pointer, so the browser fires a boundary event at the node it just removed; and `.holding`'s own `scale(0.97)` insets the hit box by **0.78px**, which can cancel a stationary press with no render involved at all — measured, and the reason the earlier poll-deferral fix could not work: the live walk shows the hold already dead 500 ms in, *before* any render. It now takes the ✒️ pen hold's shape — bound once on the document, released on `pointerup`/`pointercancel` only, sliding-off-to-cancel deliberately gone. What made the timer survivable already is that it commits through `act(id, …)`, which resolves the draft **by id, not by node**: a hold that outlives its own button still lands. `spec-check` asserts the release set; `npm run journey` drives the real thing and fails on the pre-fix page.
- **`npm run journey`** (`scripts/journey-walk.mjs`, against a running dev server) walks **founding → 🍾 → grants → caret → proposal** on the live path and exits non-zero if it stalls. It exists because the two probes and `founding-walk.mjs` all drive the fixture, which never reaches `hydrateS`, never polls and never sends a command — every live-only bug this project has hit was invisible to them. **Since 2026-08-22 it drives the propose hold for its full three seconds with a real mouse, with a render forced into the middle**, asserting at the wire (`propose-text` 2xx, the wallet one lighter) rather than on page state, where a proposed draft and an unproposed one both read as *mine*. The disclaimer it replaces — that the holds could not be exercised because the tab is backgrounded — confused an *animation* with a *pointer held down*: rAF never fires here so no flight can be judged, but `page.mouse.down()` holds as long as you like and the commit is a `setTimeout`. That confusion is why the hold bug survived a first fix. It fails on the pre-fix page at *propose-text null · wallet 5→5 · held false*. Two harness rules it encodes, both learned the hard way: **press with a real pointer** (a synthetic `.click()` does not choose a rung), and **scroll the control into view first** — a pointer cannot press what is off screen, which is what made the founding look stuck at 💤. Two assertions were added on 2026-08-22 after both of that day's birth bugs walked straight past it: **the rail immediately after the save must hold the pen alone**, and **no `/api/` response may be 4xx or 5xx** — the double send's 409 was invisible because the surface *recovered* from it, so the check belongs at the wire rather than at the outcome. The refusal net does not reproduce the double send headless (the second click needs a real collapse animation to still find `S.open` set), so it is a net for the future rather than the proof of that fix; the rail assertion is the one that bites.
- **A change carries a reason, and says who made it** (Q530, built 2026-08-22). The pen was the only route that changed a rule *without asking anybody* and the only one that left no reason behind. Now: **the founder writes one on the card**, and **everybody else is told what changed and reads it**. Which half you meet is which side of the act you stand on. Four things worth knowing.
  - **A first decision is not a change, and shows neither half.** The founder answering a question for the first time has changed nothing — there is no *from* — and a room told the founder has decided fifteen things nobody asked them about is noise. The test is the module's own `previousValue`, which is null until the pen changes a value that already stood. §9.6a says the same in the spec's words: before the start nothing is amended, only set.
  - **Nothing about the hash chain moved.** `previousValue` and `setWhy` **fold from `setting-set` events the log already carried**, the way `arrival` did in Q524; and the reason itself rides an **optional** `why?` on that event, which is hash-safe because `stableStringify` drops undefined keys — an event without one serialises exactly as it did before the field existed. The golden log passed unedited, which is the assertion of both.
  - **An ordinary setting owes an acknowledgement when it is *changed*, never when it is first made.** Constitutional settings owe one on any set, as they always have (*a rule you had no say in is a decision you are owed*); an ordinary one the founder simply made stays out of every rail, since nothing was asked and anybody may motion it. One the founder *changed* has undone something the room was living under, so it is news by the same argument.
  - **The module is the truth about what is owed, and the page now listens on both paths.** `hydrateS` only ever *added* to `S.okd`, so an acknowledgement given once could never be taken back — and a change re-owing an OK is the whole mechanism by which it announces itself. It is symmetric now, and `syncOwedOks` does the same for a local module, since the fixture never reaches `hydrateS`. It only ever removes: a change may re-open an acknowledgement, and nothing there may quietly grant one.
  - **The founder's rationale is not sealed** (Ed, 2026-08-22: *the rationale should have the founder's name and avatar on it, and 👑 so people know that it was a founder action, even if they have no name or avatar*). Every other rationale wears the featureless disc because authorship is sealed until the record (§3.4). A ✒️ act is attributed **by construction** — only the founder can perform it — so the disc would be a lie the first time it was drawn. `founderSpeaker` / `founderSpeakerLane` put the avatar where the disc goes, with the name and 👑 over the reason; the 👑 is what carries the sentence when there is neither, since an anonymous founder is ordinary.
  - Copy rule inside it: **a long value is shown, not narrated**. *from x to y* reads well for a rung, a percentage or a date and badly for ⏱️, whose value is three numbers in a sentence — the one number that moved is buried and the *to* is lost mid-clause. Past 32 characters the pair goes on two aligned `was`/`now` lines instead, where the eye finds the difference itself.
- **A unilateral rule change by the founder is an amendment, and is reported as one** (Ed, 2026-08-22, reframing Q530 mid-build). It gets no list of its own: it joins **the motions**, where every other amendment already lives, so the record renders it beside them without knowing it is different. `MotionRoute` gains **`'pen'`** — the four verbs' own ladder of who had to agree, minus 🪶, which is not an amendment because nothing exists yet to amend — and `RaisedRoute` is the narrower type for anything that *raises* one, since the pen is never raised: it opens and settles in the one act, so it is only ever seen `carried`, and every place that acts on a live motion tests `status === 'running'` and steps over it. **Folded, not emitted**: synthesised in the `setting-set` fold from the event the log already carried, with `amendedFrom(id)` beside it for the old value a motion payload never needs. Hash chain untouched; golden log unedited.
  - The dividend is the one Ed's framing predicted: **a clause now carries its own history whoever changed it**. `changeHalf` reads the amendment record rather than the setting's last-set fields, so the founder's pen and the membership's motion produce one line with one shape — and **who it names follows the route**, as it must: the pen is attributed by construction so the founder's name and face are on it, while a raised motion's mover is sealed until the record (§3.4), so that one is *the membership* and wears the featureless disc.
  - **The clause keeps it permanently** (Ed's answer, 2026-08-22). Two hoists made that work: `changeHalf` and `lastAmendment` are **function declarations**, because a member's settled card post-start renders through the composer's *earlier* return — as arrow consts the history was drawn on the news card and vanished the moment the OK turned it back into a composer. Above the fold it is news; once acknowledged the same block wears a **Last amended [date]** eyebrow, because a line a reader meets months later has to date itself or it reads as something that has just happened.
  - **The record's own Amendments section** (`amendmentBlocks`, before Backlog on the closed page): every carried change in order, one paragraph each, naming the **office** rather than the person — the pen is the Founder's by construction, a raised motion is the membership's act, and neither sentence has to reach into the anonymity ladder to know what it may say. An **absolute** date, never `whenOf`'s relative one: *today, 11:28* is false by morning and the record is read long after.
- **✋ and 🖼️ are answered, and null is not the answer to *were you asked*** (Ed's live walk, 2026-08-22, Q645: *I'm never offered the "Your Name" and "Your Picture" tasks*). The same lesson as 🎩 above, in its sharpest form. `ready()` is permanently true for both — neither is in `CHOSEN`, and `myname.ready` is forced `() => true` because a task that cannot be committed is a task nothing can get past — so `S.seen` was the **whole** of `settled()` for them, and `hydrateS` could not rebuild it, so it simply declared both seen on every live render. Steps 9 and 10 of the founding order were dead on every real document while working perfectly in the fixture, which is why **no probe and no golden walk could ever have caught it**. What blocked the honest version is real: a blank name is a real answer — §9.0c shows it as **Anonymous**, *a name, not a gap* — and a picture is removed by choosing initials, so the value cannot say whether the question was put. The module therefore records **the act**: `nameSet`/`pictureSet` fold from the `identity-set` events the log already carries (`!== undefined`, never truthiness), the way `arrival` (Q524) and `previousValue` (Q530) do — no new event, no envelope change, **golden log unedited**. Two dividends: it is **per seat** by construction, where `S.seen` is page-global — the one "have you dealt with this" store that was not, so one seat's committed ✋ used to read as settled for everybody — and it survives a reload. ⏩ must answer both (a **null** picture is the answer *my initials*), or *settle the founding* leaves 🖼️ standing and the setup-probe diffs. Guarded live by `npm run journey`, which asserts the rail after 🎩 is exactly `myname, mypic` — **position, not presence**, since arriving in the wrong place is its own failure. Related, and fixed with it: **🎩 decides where the founder sits, not who they are** (Q646) — `convenor-membership-set` rebuilt the record from `freshMember` on every tick, wiping name, picture and both OK sets, and it carries **both** ways, since while the founder is a member their identity lives on the `MemberRecord` and the convenor struct goes stale.
- **The address is the machine's until you touch it, and the machine says when it picks** (Q534 (c), Ed 2026-08-22). 📍 is born pre-filled with `slugify(title)`, so on a collision the ordinary birth would open a task that is *already refused* — correct, but an unpleasantness at the third step of a founding. A **suggested** address moves itself to the nearest free one before the founder ever meets it; a **typed** one never moves, because there the address is theirs and a collision is a refusal to read and correct. `S.slugAuto` is the whole distinction: set in `fillSuggestedSlug` (the one place a suggestion is applied — it was three), cleared by the one act that can make the address yours. The move **says so**, naming both addresses — *Suggested from the title — docs.vote/charter is taken, so this one is docs.vote/charter-2* — since silently handing somebody a suffixed address is the machine deciding without telling them. Two guards worth keeping: the address it moves **to** is asked about in its own right on the next render rather than trusted from the response that offered it, and `autoSlugMoves` caps the chain at three so a server that keeps offering an address it then refuses cannot become a request storm. **`npm run slug-walk`** (`scripts/slug-walk.mjs`, against a running server, with one address reserved first) walks both halves; it fails on the pre-fix page at *the commit is dark on a typed address that is taken*. It exists because `journey` births with a unique title and so can never meet a collision, and both probes drive the fixture, where the check is synchronous and the live path is never reached.
- **A grant is the holder's, and nothing hid them** (SURFACE E8's audience column; found and fixed with Q639). A **member** was served two ⏳ tabs titled *Your Pen* and *Your Shield*, about powers they do not hold, cannot acknowledge and were never asked about — invisible while the clauses stood, since the tab had a sentence beside it. `hide: () => !amFounder()`, the seam 🍾 already uses; `spec-check` asserts both predicates and that the retired sentences never come back.

## The glossary prose, lifted 2026-08-24 (Q737)

`CLAUDE.md`'s entry rule says that every glossary bullet, at any depth, names something, declares
its kind and fits in one line. This is what the fourth extraction moved out to make that true:
forty-seven bullets that named nothing — prose sub-bullets grown eight deep under
`constitution-section` and eight more under `server` — and thirty entries that named something and
then argued for it at up to a thousand characters. Every one of them is below verbatim, in the
order it stood in and at the indent it stood at, so the entry each hung under is readable from the
indent alone.

Nothing here was deleted. Where a bullet was a **rule**, the operative file now carries the §
pointer and the rule's own file carries the rule; where it was a **reason**, this section is where
it lives now; where it was a **post-mortem**, it is in `CLAUDE.md`'s own *Gotchas*. A good many
were none of those three but a **name** with no name written on it — a part of the system described
in prose under a parent entry — and those were promoted to entries of their own rather than moved,

`linkify`, `pictureBody`, `emojiface`, `FACE_TONES`, `RESERVED_EMOJI`, `closedBlocks`,

## The Proposals run merged into one clause (2026-08-25, Q748–Q752)

Ed, 2026-08-23, reading the founding: *this should be one clause — maybe two — with the tabs in a
stack*. The rule is SURFACE **Y23**; what is here is why four became one, and what it reverses.

**The fragmentation was structural, not careless.** The band's rule is *one distinct paragraph per
decision, its tab in the gutter to its left*, which quietly assumes every decision has enough
prose to **be** a paragraph. These four do not: 💡 and ⚖️ are **gates**, which follow from the
other decisions rather than being decided; 🏛️ is a **grant**; and 🍾's is the governance preamble,
which speaks only in deviations. So a reader met four stubs where there is one topic — how a rule
changes, and who may take part — and four full-height tabs claiming four decisions of equal
weight. Worse, two pairs said one thing twice: *Members may propose changes to rules* (🍾) against
*Members may propose as soon as they arrive* (💡), and *Constitutional proposals 🏛️ need
everyone's consent* (🍾) against *Each member holds one voice in every constitutional question*
(🏛️) — the same fact from the room's side and the member's.

**Composed, not concatenated** (Q749). Each decision contributes a **fragment** and one function
joins them, so no gate loses its variants and nothing is stated twice: sentence 1 is 💡's own two
`canPropose()` branches joined to ⚖️'s, sentence 2 is what it takes to pass with 🏛️'s clause on
its tail, sentence 3 is the deviation baseline `holderLine` states exceptions to, verbatim. No
new copy was invented; the composition removes the duplication and supplies the joins. Two calls
made under stated assumption rather than asked: the merged sentence says **when the document
begins**, not the preamble's *when the session begins*, because 💡's own sentence already said
*document* and *session* is engine vocabulary the surface otherwise avoids; and sentence 2 goes
**singular**, because the appended *each member holding one voice* only scans against a singular
subject. Either is a one-string revert.

**Two rulings this reverses, deliberately.** **Q641** (2026-08-22) held that 🏛️ *Your Voice*
keeps its clause, "since *Each member holds one voice in every constitutional question* is not
restated anywhere and so earns its paragraph by Ed's own test". It **is** restated — the
consent sentence is the same fact — so the test was applied to a sentence nobody had read beside
its neighbour. It survives as a subordinate clause (Q751). **Q605** put 🏛️'s clause under the
first blind question asking you, falling back to ⚖️; Q750 **narrows** rather than reverses it.
Q605's reasoning is about when the **news** arrives, and the news still travels: `tasksFor` is
untouched and the rail entry appears with the question that needed it. What stops depending on
session state is where the **sentence** lives.

**The shapes rejected.** *Two paragraphs, split taking-part from passing* — it splits one thought
at exactly the seam the duplication runs along, and leaves two two-tab piles twelve pixels apart,
which is the Q308 floor the members block already pays for. *Preamble untouched, the three stubs
merged under it* — it keeps the duplication, which was the actual complaint, and buys nothing but
a shorter run. One paragraph won because the four state one rule together.

**The mechanism is `chipsFor`**, the supported N-tabs-on-one-paragraph route — the one 🌡️
already uses to carry 🪜 and its two power tabs — not the Members block's two absolutely
positioned `.chipcol`s, an exception paid for by a hard-coded 42px that does not generalise. The
host paragraph is **`begin`**, so `data-para` is stable open or closed, which is what `birthPass`
and `scrollToCard` key on. Closed, only the front tab opens; open, the whole strip is live and
switching is a `card-morph`. Measured after the merge: **0.0px on both axes** for the front mark
when the card opens and for each of the four reached within the open strip, at 1600 and 1280 —
including the path nothing had ever exercised, a **non-front chip opened from its rail entry**
(the strip's order is `stackOrder`'s, the same sort the closed pile uses, so the front chip is
index 0 whether or not it is the active one). `fitBand` leaves the four-chip pile at its default
4px peek with 93px of clearance to ⏱️'s tab below it.

**One consequence, accepted.** `birthPass` keys band paragraphs `p:<data-para>`; `.achip`s are
not keyed, so a tab never animates as born. 💡 and ⚖️ used to arrive as new `.cpara`s and fade in
over F14's 840ms; now `p:begin` is already born, so at the press the three tabs appear without a
fade while the paragraph's text changes under them. Their rail entries still grow, and 🍾's own
ceremony carries the moment. No bespoke re-fade: anything keyed on content would re-fire on every
state change of the sentence, which is the opposite of *what is born arrives*.

**Two things the build found that the plan did not.** **🏛️ stays out of `SEC.rate.keys`.** The
plan's edit list put it there so `spec-check` could resolve its host through `secOf`. But
`secLive()` makes a section live exactly when one of its keys is visible, and 🏛️ is `visible`
from its own place in ORDER (row 22) or earlier via `early()` — so admitting it would drag the
whole Proposals section forward and offer its news before 🍾, which is the F9 situation Q745 had
just closed. The clause is pinned by `chipsFor` instead, the checker takes an explicit `hosts`
override, and `voiceInStack()` — *the preamble is standing and the voice is visible* — decides in
one place whether 🏛️ is a fragment or a paragraph of its own. It keeps its own paragraph in the
one state with no preamble to join: a member met by a blind question before the constitution has
reached Proposals at all. **And the group's note had to count it back**: the pinned tab leaves
the `ctx.tasksFor` seam `wants` counts from, and a tab that still wants an OK is one of the
things left wherever its sentence lives.

**The latent hole this closed** (Q752). `anchorOf` resolved a rail entry's anchor through
`tabFor`, which reads `data-tab` — and an **inert** chip carries none, because nothing may click
past the tab in front of it. So a non-front stacked tab was unfindable and its entry fell all the
way through to the title paragraph. Nothing reached that branch before: 🪜 has no rail entry and
neither do the power tabs. This group is the first where three of four do. Every chip now carries
`data-chip` whether or not it is clickable and `hostParaOf` consults it — a fix for every pile,
not a patch for this one. `para()` gained the matching correction: a group whose other members
are not being served yet leaves **one** chip standing, and it must be the group's, not the
host's.

## The three open items

Full text is in `QUESTIONS.md` under **910**, **911** and **912**. Short form,
with what I would do:

**Q910 — the setup-probe walks past four cards and reports green.** Thirteen
dead steps in one run mean 🤝, 🎩, ✋ and 🖼️ have no probe coverage at all, and
a dead step is a warning rather than a failure, so the run prints *46 steps,
0 diffs* and reads as proof. This is the same species as Q900 — a hole in a
guard that reads as a green guard. It is **older than this session** (filed as
Q504(a)) and survived the re-freeze untouched. Do (a) find out *why* each step
misses, before re-pointing any selector; then (b) turn `--strict` on in CI.
(b) without (a) is a red build nobody can act on.

**Q911 — a walk can run against a server another session left listening.**
Two were up on the default ports; the one on 8140 still knew the `signing`
setting spec v0.70 retired, so the first run of `applicants-walk` produced a
page of confident, entirely fictitious failures and cost an hour. Recommended:
every walk asserts the server's build — its catalogue's setting ids against the
tree it was started from — and stops rather than reporting nonsense. Note this
is plan-queue backlog **65** seen from the draft side.

**Q912 — two undecided details in the admit card's news form.** At 🪪 *pen* an
application is news rather than a task (Ed, 2026-08-26). Its OK is
**session-local** — not in `ACK_KEYS`, so the news returns on reload — and it

in and so no name to use. Both defensible; neither ruled on.

## Two things worth knowing before touching this area

- **`'roster'` in `design/session-view.html` means three unrelated things**:
  the 🪪 card key, the *delegated to the membership* holder sentinel, and
  `S.roster`, the member-row array. Q903's plan turns the first into
  `admission` and says explicitly not to sweep the string.
- **The walks' own traps**, both learned the hard way writing
  `applicants-walk` and recorded in its header: a second page in the same
  browser *context* takes the founder's seat (one cookie per document), and the
  magic link's origin is the server's `baseUrl` — so a walk started at
  `127.0.0.1` lands on `localhost`, and navigating back to the starting host
  arrives seatless and reads very convincingly as the product losing the
  founder's seat on reload.

## What happens today

Consequences: the rail carries every losing proposal and its pair tabs until the close (Q1431's crowded rail); an ordinary motion never fails during the document's life, so Q1447's card for it is raised only on a document that then shuts (its OK unpressable); a losing invitation motion holds its address under the twin rule (R-103) until the close; and a proposal that lost 8–7 can be won back on one changed vote, which is the reversion the churn study counts (`REPORT-churn.md`).

## The rule

For a live candidate X on a race at time t (setting races and membership races alike — they are races), over the members of E with a usable X-vs-incumbent comparison plus the awaited (as `approvalCore` already builds them since Q1439):

- **a** = members whose latest judgment of X against the current text prefers X (the author by their derived preference, as today); **o** = prefer the current text; **w** = members of E with no such judgment (awaited or abstained — an abstained member could still answer, so they count as *could yet approve*). *Indifferent* is in none of them.
- **Dominated by the current text** when X's best possible future fails either test that passing needs: **a + w ≤ o** (more must prefer it than not; a tie keeps the current text, §4.2), or **a + w < F(G)** where F is `floorFor` on a group **G = a + o + w** (the floor as Q1439 stage 6 leaves it: the chosen quorum against the group, capped at half of it, never below `min(2, E)`). Why the best case is *every one of the w approves*: an approval raises approvals by 1 and the floor by at most ½; an abstention lowers the floor by at most ½ and approvals by nothing — so no mix of answers and time-outs beats all-approve (Q1439's properties 2 and 3; pin this as a test, not a comment).
- **Dominated by a rival Y** when, on the X-vs-Y pair, the members preferring Y outnumber those preferring X **plus every member of E who has not answered that pair**: X can never be preferred to Y by the membership. The leader is the top of the *fitted* ranking (R-114), not a pairwise majority, so this clause is an approximation of *can never be on top*; state that in the code and the spec, and make it **conservative** — a candidate is retired by it only when the count is strict.
- **A dominated candidate retires**, at the next sweep (`tick` / the adoption batch — the same moment adoptions land, so the two are decided on one snapshot, dominations after adoptions in the batch, oldest first), as `candidate-retired` with a **reason** the record can read (`dominated`), its stake refunded by §7 as any retirement is; judgments on it lock as they do at any seal. If it was the race's last live member the race **seals with the current text standing** — the event and record the close already produces for that, so SURFACE E15 (*a race seals, the incumbent held*: ✖ pinned for members who judged it, OK) fires during the document's life. For a motion the module's status becomes `held` and Q1447's card is owed to the mover (`heldBy: 'members'`; the E41 sentence *The membership rejected your proposal*). For a text proposal the author's `yours` entry ends as it does when a proposal is retired today — find that surface (SURFACE §6's `yours` mark, E15, E17) and make the author one of the members E15 pins for (their derived preference is a judgment, §3.3).
- **Closed for good**: a later arrival does not revive it (E grows, but the candidate is retired); the author re-proposes. Revision of a cast vote (§4.4) can no longer rescue it — knowingly, Ed's sentence. The twin rule (R-103) releases the moment the motion is held. The dedup gate (§5): re-proposing the same text after a domination is a fresh candidate with a fresh floor — say what the gate does today with a retired identical candidate and leave it as it is unless it refuses, in which case report.
- **Never before anybody has judged**: a candidate with `leaderMeasured`-style measured evidence of zero (nobody but its author has answered) is not dominated by counts that are all zero — check the arithmetic: with a = 1, o = 0, w = E − 1 it passes both tests unless E is 1 or 2 (E = 2: a + w = 2 > 0, F = 2 → not dominated). Pin E = 1, 2, 3 in tests.
- **Determinism and blindness.** The test reads counts the engine already holds; it runs inside the sweep and emits an event, so replay folds it. It reveals an outcome, as an adoption does (§3.5 seals standings, not outcomes). No new number reaches the member view except the record's reason.

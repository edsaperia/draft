Question 1006, backlog entry 191 as your ruling 1050 redefined it: §9’s card kinds were prose once and are a table now; a pass that tabulated F1–F22 the same way would do more for the file’s size and readability than every item in the extraction proposal put together. Every rule is below, in full, both ways — its prose as it stands and the row it would become — so nothing is lost invisibly. The build’s recommendation is stated per rule and you can overturn any one of it with the checkbox on that rule. Questions 1122–1127 are the choices; 1128 is a finding, which stands unless vetoed. Answers save in this page; Copy all answers at the foot gives the paste-back block.

The finding, first

F1–F23 do not tabulate. Not because the rules are untidy, but because of what they are: an F-rule is not one normative statement, it is a named cluster of them — and the clusters are wildly uneven. A faithful table of all 23, nothing shortened and nothing dropped, is 1,681 B larger than the prose, and its worst cell is 2,577 characters wide.

§9’s card-kinds table is the model, and the comparison is what makes the case. It works because every row is the same kind of thing and eight repeated field-labels lift out of every row into the header — opened from · head · field · radios · left · right · closes · files as. That lifting is where a table’s savings come from. §8.1 has nothing to lift: F1 is the founding runs in single file, F14 is four animation durations, F21 is the Membership section’s whole structure. There is no field they share, so a table over them has one honest column — the rule — and that column is the prose again, with scaffolding added.

The choices — 1122 to 1127

1122 The pass’s own verdict — does §8.1 become a table?

The measurement is above. Tabulating all 23 faithfully — nothing shortened, nothing dropped, which is what refactor, not cull requires — makes the section larger by 1,681 B, because the prose has no repeated field-labels to lift into column headers. §9’s card-kinds table works precisely because it does: nine labels lifted out of every row. §8.1’s rules each say a different kind of thing, so there is nothing to lift.

And the readability half goes the same way: the eight rules that tabulate cleanly are 7.6% of the section’s bytes; the six that resist are 62.8%. The table would be at its best on the rules nobody struggles with and at its worst on F21, F19 and F5.

1123 The eight that tabulate cleanly

F1 · F4 · F8 · F9 · F10 · F12 · F13 · F15 — 1,030 B, 7.6% of the section. Each is one to four atomic statements and would make an honest row. Each row is also 28–60 B larger than the sentence, and a reader arriving at §8.1 would meet a table of the easy rules followed by prose for the hard ones.

1124 The nine that are strained

F6 · F7 · F11 · F14 · F16 · F17 · F18 · F20 · F22 — 3,990 B, 29.6%. Each fits in a row, and each row carries the whole rule in one cell with its internal seams flattened to commas. F17 is the one rule in the section whose condition column earns its keep (the birth · a task outstanding · no card open); F14 is the one carrying real numbers (240 ms, 840 ms, 55 ms, six).

1125 The six that resist

F2 · F3 · F5 · F19 · F21 · F23 — 8,460 B, 62.8% of the section. Between them 58 separable normative statements. F21 alone is 14 statements in one 2,733 B rule.

Option (c) is here so the ruling is visible rather than assumed: it is what a table of these would actually require, and you have ruled it out twice.

1126 F19’s six reason codes — a table inside F19?

The one genuinely tabular thing the pass found. F19 enumerates six readiness() reasons — judge-gate, deps-unsettled, invitation-open, one-voice, collecting, text-unconfirmed — and then says different things about three of them: one-voice is the only one no amount of answering clears, deps-unsettled is served without the ✉️ remedy, and a dependency that is itself a one-voice hold says what waits on it in turn.

That is a reason · what it means · is ✉️ the remedy table, and it is the one place in §8.1 where rows would carry more than the prose does. It was not built, because it changes F19’s shape and this plan’s ruling was never force a table.

1127 An index table above the prose?

1006’s argument was size and readability. The size half is answered above. The readability half could be had without any tabulation: a 23-row id → what it governs index above the rules, roughly 1.2 KB added, so §8.1 opens with a map and then states the rules in full.

Against it: the ids are already bolded, already in order, and each already leads with its own summary sentence — - **F1 The founding runs in single file…**. An index would be a second, shorter statement of 23 things that already state themselves, and a second statement is the thing CLAUDE.md’s admission rule exists to stop.

The finding — 1128

1128 The F-id guard asserts definitions, never occurrences

The plan’s step 7 asked spec-check to assert that each F-id appears exactly once in SURFACE.md. That is unbuildable, and it would also be wrong: §8.1 is built out of cross-references. F19 is written seven times in the file, F5 and F18 five each, F7 and F3 four, F9, F8 and F23 three — and §8’s own ORDER table cites F5, F9, F18 and F19 in two of its cells.

Built instead: each id is defined exactly once — one list item in §8.1 whose lead is - **F<n> — the ids are exactly F1–F23 in order with no gap and no duplicate, no rule is defined outside §8.1, and every F<n> written anywhere in SURFACE.md resolves to a definition, so a citation cannot outlive the rule it points at. Green: 23 rules defined, 53 citations, all resolving. Verified to bite on a renumbering, a deletion and a dangling citation.

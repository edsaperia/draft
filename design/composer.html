Design notes — the composer, first pass

Desktop-first · light mode · Bootstrap-plain (visual design deferred) · content from run clubhouse-1, minutes after the session-view moment: the § Money race has just deadlocked

A writing desk, not a dashboard

The composer is the only surface in the product that shows direction, so the temptation is to make it a war room. Resisted: it is one column you read downward — arrival context, then the briefing, then the desk, then the gate. The brief is what a good clerk would hand you before you draft: the heat, who wants what and why, what's already dead, and what a winning draft must do. Ceremony is earned here (the north star), but it is the ceremony of being well briefed, not of watching dials.

The one briefed surface

SPEC §3.5: judgment is blind; composition is briefed. Standings, splits and camps appear here and nowhere else, and everyone who sees them has paid for the peek — propose-C forfeits that pair's comparison (never collected) — or was summoned by the engine. The ribbon at the top states the inversion so it reads as a rule of the room, not a leak. Camp cards carry reasons and rough cohesion, never names; the why-digest quotes rationales, never people.

The loss account's honesty tiers

The dominated account is labeled tier by tier as what it is — facts (standings, head-to-heads, indifference, decision speed, the camp cut), the winners' own words (their pinned rationales, quoted verbatim), and hypothesis (the fitted span-level attribution, with its evidence volume attached and an honest note about what the data cannot isolate, plus the isolating redraft it suggests). Thin data would get a thin account. The three options price honestly: retire refunds by peak strength; co-sign refunds fully; redraft informed seeds the desk and pins the hypothesis above the editor — and the counter shows 0 of 2 used, because two failed informed redrafts carry the position to the backlog as a recorded persistent current.

The bridge bar

Because this race is deadlocked, the brief ends with the bar an entering draft must clear: minimum support across camps — beat A among B's preferrers, beat B among A's, via stratified probes (§6.3). Rendered as two slots rather than one meter because the min() is the point: the second-best camp is the score. The machine's geometry read (§6.4) sits beneath it as a labeled hypothesis — one-dimensional split, a median can win — with the note that machine seeds compete by the same arithmetic.

The desk

The contested ground renders in race yellow, exactly as the race-card and session-view draw it, so the series' color grammar carries: yellow is the contested text wherever you meet it. Seeding (current / A / B) matches the propose-C promise made in the race-card notes. The footprint chip is live commentary — the footprint follows the edit and decides the race — and the separable-bundle flag is the §4.1 submission-time choice, defaulted to “one intent” because this draft is a bridge and bridges are take-it-or-leave-it by nature. Stake is stated flat with the refund curve in one line: enough to price the move, not an economy lesson.

The gate is a moment, not a wall

Submission runs the dedup gate (§5.1) inline: the existing candidate, the similarity evidence as magnitudes, and the machine-articulated delta — which is deliberately the best writing on the page, because the delta is what makes “differentiate” a real choice rather than a scolding. The demo draft was chosen to sit honestly near Option A so all three options are live: co-sign if the delta isn't your point, differentiate if it is, insist if you think the room can tell them apart — and insisting routes to behavioral dedup, where being wrong costs a merge and being right sharpens the ranking. The gate borders purple: in the series grammar purple is “something changed since you looked,” and here it is the engine interrupting your intent with news.

What is deliberately absent

  • No identities, anywhere. Camps are structures, digests quote rationales, the loss account's “camp cut” names no one. Heat shows magnitudes; standings show probabilities, not voters.
  • No live poll of your draft. The desk never predicts how your draft would fare — that would be a standings feed by the back door. The bridge bar states the test; the judging answers it.
  • No chat. To argue is to draft (§3.4); the rationale field is the one utterance and it is pinned to the candidate.
  • No sight of other composers. Whether anyone else is drafting here right now is private state (§11).

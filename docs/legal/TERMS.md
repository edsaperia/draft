## 4. How a document works

A document has one **founder**, who creates it and invites people. Everybody
invited who arrives is a **member**.

The members write the document together. Anybody may propose a change;
proposals are judged against each other; a change is made when the group's
agreement is strong enough by the rules that group set for itself. Some rules
can only be changed if every member agrees.

Two consequences worth stating plainly, because they are unusual:

- **The group decides, not us.** We do not moderate documents, arbitrate
  disputes between members, or overrule what a group has agreed. If a document
  goes somewhere you do not want to go, your remedies are the ones the document
  itself gives you: propose a change, refuse to consent, or leave.
- **What the group agreed is what the record says.** The record is
  append-only. A decision that was made stays on the record as having been
  made, even after the people who made it have left.

## 7. If you leave, or are removed

A membership can end in several ways: you sign out of a document, the members
remove you, or your membership lapses because you have been inactive for
longer than that document allows.

In every case:

- **the judgments you already cast keep counting.** They were inputs to
  decisions the rest of the group relied on. They were never attributed to you
  and still are not.
- **you can ask us to erase you.** What that does, and what it cannot do, is
  set out in the Privacy Policy. Read that section before you join — it is
  written there rather than here on purpose.

## 8. Documents that close, and documents that stall

A document ends by its own clock, at the time the group set. When it does, a
record is published to the members and to anybody who was invited.

A document can also freeze, if too few of its members are still active for the
group to decide anything. A frozen document is not deleted; it thaws if enough
people come back.

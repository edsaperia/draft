94. **A rationale is machine-drafted and human-edited** (opened 2026-08-16; settled by Ed 2026-08-16). ### Queue ordering — what survives Ed's 104–108

Ed replaced ranked ordering with a **margin index** (2026-08-16): entries stand beside their own clauses in document order, sealed items are dots, a patch appears once per site. Urgency was carried by colour strength and card size; since 2026-08-16 the card sizes are equal and urgency instead decides **which** entries fit on the screen, so the number now gates visibility rather than compression — which makes 98 below considerably more load-bearing than it was. That closed 99 (document position doesn't move when others judge), 101 (sealed stay, as dots — the rail is a map, and a map shows settled ground) and 103 (there are no serving *slots* left to explain; everything is shown at once). Three remain, all about the urgency number itself rather than about order.
98. **What is a race's urgency?** SPEC §8.1 scores each *pair* by pivotality v. A race with six candidates has many pairs of differing v; the rail shows the race once. Is that number the v of the single pair it would serve you next (honest — it's what clicking gets you), the max over its servable pairs (its potential), or a sum (its total remaining work, which would leave big races permanently at the top of the rail)? The mockup hand-authors one `urgency` number per entry and dodges the question. Now that urgency decides what you can see rather than how loudly it is drawn, a wrong answer here costs a member sight of a question rather than a shade of colour.

100. **Where c_p goes now.** A participant's judgment cost is one scalar dividing every v, so it never reorders their own list — and with the rail showing everything at once it can't truncate one either. It has to act on the *drawing*: since 2026-08-16 the rail already shows only as many questions as fit, most urgent first, so c_p's natural home is that cut — an expensive judge is shown fewer of them, and the rest stay in the count at the foot of the rail rather than being hidden. Undecided, but the mechanism it would act on now exists.

102. **Urgency is kind-blind.** Nothing distinguishes a copy-edit from a constitutional fight, so a typo near its floor can burn as bright as a structural race. Ed's north star ("most of a session should feel like approving typo fixes") argues for letting cheap items shine; the risk is a margin lit up by typos while a structural race sits pale. The `race-labeler` already stores copy-edit/substantive/structural but nothing consumes it (Q49) — the same decision arriving from the other end.

109. **Scale: the margin assumes a short rail.** Sixteen entries beside a twenty-page charter is a map you can scan. Two hundred would be a filing system, and document order would stop steering anyone toward decision margins at all — which is what §8.1 exists to do. V1 tunes to 5–10 members, so this is not urgent, but it is the assumption the whole design now rests on and it should be named before anyone builds for a bigger room.

111. **An open patch cannot keep its sites level and stay on screen** (found 2026-08-16, building 110). The decision card opens inline at the patch's first site and is tall, so the second and third sites move hundreds of pixels down the document — further than the visible band. Ed's 110 says needs-you entries stay on screen, so the sibling entries hold at the band edge and their wires run at an angle; only the topmost is level. Three ways out: (a) leave it — the spine still cables them and each wire still points at its own site, so the relationship is legible even when the geometry isn't; (b) let a patch's siblings leave the band while it is open, treating an open judgment as an exception to 110; (c) open the decision card somewhere that doesn't displace the other sites — a fixed panel rather than an inline insert, which is a much bigger change and cuts against the whole in-place design. I would take (a), but it is the first place where 108 and 110 genuinely pull against each other.

113. **Where read state lives, and when a decision goes quiet** (raised 2026-08-16, from Q112; part (c) settled by Q114 — an explicit acknowledgement is the only thing that clears an unread decision, so nothing marks itself read). Read/unread is the first per-member state on this surface — everything else in the rail is identical for everyone. Two things remain. (a) It has to be **stored somewhere**: a per-member marker in the event log is the obvious home, but an acknowledgement is not a judgment and probably should not be hash-chained with them; a side table keyed by member and candidate is more honest, at the cost of the log no longer being the whole story. Still worth deciding whether some relief is wanted — expire at a session boundary, cap how many pin at once and count the rest, or an *acknowledge all* — since the clutter is what the overflow count in 110 will be reporting.

122. **When do rankings become visible — at seal, or at close?** (raised 2026-08-16, from Q121.) A sealed race now shows its full field ranked, with each candidate's win probability against the current text. The warrant is good: Bradley–Terry already carries a strength per candidate, SPEC §8.3's no-standings rule governs *live* feeds (where knowing who is ahead makes judging strategic), and the `record-builder` publishes rankings at close regardless. But this is still rankings appearing **during** a session rather than after it, which is a small extension nobody has ruled on. Two things to weigh. (a) Sealed races are independent of live ones, so there is no direct leak — but seeing standings on settled questions normalises the idea that standings exist, and a member may start reading the sealed ones for camp structure. (b) It gives an author immediate, precise feedback on how their proposal did, which is arguably the humane thing and arguably a reason to redraft on scoreboard rather than on argument. If Ed wants rankings held until close, the card can show the outcome and the field without the numbers, and the numbers arrive with the record. Needs a SPEC §8.3 sentence either way.

189. **"Hot", "warm", "cool" are three different vocabularies wearing one word** (raised 2026-08-16, Ed's question — he asked where the terms come from and whether they are well defined; the honest answer is that two of the three are mine and one is the spec's, and they do not mean the same thing). (a) SPEC §8.3's **hot set** is a real defined term: the ~3 races receiving concentrated sampling at any moment — a property of the *router*, global, the same for everybody. (b) SPEC §3.2's **care-map cold** is also defined: a span the group is indifferent about, which keeps its incumbent cheaply and sinks in the feed — a property of the *group's* attitude to a clause. (c) The mockup's **hot / warm / cool tiers** are neither: they are three buckets of the per-member `urgency` scalar at 0.66 and 0.33, invented for the rail's compression ladder (Q104), personal rather than global, and unrelated to (a) or (b) — a card can be mockup-"hot" while not being in the router's hot set, and a care-map-cold span would show as mockup-"cool" only by coincidence. Three fixes, pick one: rename the tiers to something with no thermal meaning (`full` / `brief` / `line`, describing what is drawn rather than what is meant); rename them to what they measure (`yours-most` / `yours` / `background`); or define the collision away by making the tiers actually derive from the router's hot set, which would be a mechanism change rather than a naming one. I lean to the first — the tiers are a *drawing* decision and naming them after the drawing keeps them honestly separate from both spec terms.

264. **Green now means three things** (raised 2026-08-16; widened the same day by the fix to 263). In a rail or a gutter it means *yours, and you can still act on it*; inside a card it means *decided* — the winning text in a sealed record, the tick that commits — and inside a **lane** it means *new wording*, which it has meant since 91 and now appears in the composer as well as on every decision card. They never meet, because 164 washes every decided state grey in the margin, so `--ok` is confined to card interiors. The margin pair are kept apart by 164, which washes every decided state grey in a rail, so the *decided* green never leaves a card interior — a genuine dependency between two decisions rather than a coincidence, and one that breaks the moment a decided state gains a colour in the rail. The two **inside** a card are kept apart only by scale and place: a whole block or a whole button for *decided*, a few words inline for *new wording*. One collision has already been headed off — the editing lane’s focus ring was green and is now blue, because a green box around green words was two meanings an inch apart. Worth a proper look at the palette pass; the likely answer is that *new wording* wants a hue of its own, since it is the only one of the three that is not about a state.

282. **A rendered document and a source offset do not agree** (raised 2026-08-17, from 279's build; Ed asked for a recommendation the same day). The charter fixture is plain text, so the composer can take a caret offset straight from the DOM and use it against the clause's source. The moment the *document* renders markdown that stops being true: a DOM selection measures the rendered text, and `**bold**` is four characters there and eight in the source, so every offset the composer computes is wrong by two for each mark above the caret.

**Recommendation: render markdown in the document, and make the rendered→source mapping a property of the block rather than of the composer.** One function that takes a block element and a DOM position and returns a *source* offset, used by everything that asks the question — `offsetIn`, `startDraftFromTyping`, `startDraftFromRun`. The arithmetic already exists (`richToSource` / `sourceToRich`, written for the lane's view toggle); what matters is that exactly one place knows about the distinction, because the failure mode here is a two-character drift that nothing complains about and no test catches unless the test happens to put a mark above the caret.

Two other options, for the record. Keep the charter plain and let markdown live only inside candidates — cheapest, but an adopted candidate would then print its asterisks into the charter, so it only defers the problem to the first adoption. Or store the document rendered and treat markdown purely as import/export — which gives up "the characters you see are what is stored", and that was the whole reason 279 chose source-of-truth markdown in the first place.

**Do it before the charter carries any formatting**, not after. Until then every test passes and the bug does not exist; the day somebody bolds a word near the top of a long clause it exists everywhere at once, and it will look like a caret bug rather than an offset bug.

292. **Salience arrives late, and thin** (Ed, 2026-08-17, on SPEC §8.3a): *if a diagonal needs a certain number of people to be ratified, and it is only shown to people who have judged everything, then it only ever does anything when we have a quorum number of people who have judged everything.* There is no ratification threshold — salience is advisory and never touches adoption — but the timing objection stands on its own: salience exists to prioritise routing while the room is busy, which is exactly when nobody has an empty queue.
